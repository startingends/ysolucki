(1) D3 (Gamma kernel mirror positive on the half line): Widder's theorem, phi(x+y) positive definite on an interval <=> phi = int e^(-lambda x) dmu(lambda), mu >= 0 on R; with 'decreasing' added, mu on [0, inf) (Hausdorff-Bernstein-Widder). Read as quoted in Neeb-Olafsson 2018, Ch. 4. (2) S1-mirror's Theorem 1: Perfekt-Pushnitski 2018, Thm 3.3 (I read Sections 1-5 in full in the TeX source, 6-7 and the appendix by statements): hypothesis alpha(n) = O(n^a); (i) M(alpha) >= 0 <=> moments of a unique positive measure on R^infinity; (ii) M(alpha) >= 0 and M(alpha(p .)) >= 0 for all p <=> measure on [0,inf)^infinity. They call it 'essentially known' (Berezansky, Berg-Christensen-Ressel, Samoilenko). The bounded case with the simplex statement (extreme points = semicharacters with values in [-1,1]) is Berg-Christensen-Ressel 1976 (Ressel's survey read). (3) Corollary 3, the count of negative squares of [c(p^(i+j))]: the HERMITE-JACOBI theorem (1856): the Hankel form of the power sums of a real polynomial has rank = number of distinct roots and signature = number of distinct real roots; here with weights m_j. (search summaries only). So the mirror test at p is Hermite's real-root test for the local Euler polynomial; its Toeplitz twin (Schur-Cohn, from memory) counts roots off the unit circle and is the Ramanujan test of B117. (4) Twist invariance R2: the Schur product theorem; the mirror cone is closed under pointwise products with ANY mirror-positive function, the twists being its invertible elements. (5) The 'multi-time Kallen-Lehmann' extension of B17 (c~(a/b) = c(ab) for coprime a, b positive definite on the rationals when |chi| <= 1): the regular unitary dilation of commuting self-adjoint contractions (Sz.-Nagy-Foias, Brehmer; from memory); in scalar form a product of Poisson kernels. (6) Bounded-versus-unbounded for the two kernels (PROVED from (2), NOTES N5): [Lambda(ab)/sqrt(ab)], a,b >= 2, is a bounded operator of norm exactly log 2 (blocks log p/(p-1)); the Gamma mirror matrix is unbounded on l^2 only through its m = 0 square n^(-1/2), an atom AT the boundary s = 1/2 of Perfekt-Pushnitski's Carleson condition, the same square that cancels half the pole (B25); the m >= 1 part is trace class.

A POINT OF THE CONE, three candidates from the brief, answered. EXTREME RAY: no. KNOWN: extreme rays = single semicharacters. For c carried by prime powers these are (one prime, one real root); zeta's Lambda is an infinite positive combination, one ray per prime (rank one per prime, S1-mirror B96), and a twist n^d moves each ray to another ray. What IS special about zeta's rays: chi_p is idempotent (B120). DETERMINACY: no. Every object on the board has a compactly supported representing measure; such moment problems are always determinate, for every d. Indeterminacy exists on the OTHER side: continuation of the shift form from a window (Krein; SP-operator B131: one or infinitely many); question to SP-operator follows. BOUNDEDNESS: yes, this is the item that is not twist invariant, and it is an EDGE condition: in analytic normalisation zeta's roots sit on the boundary of the bounded part of the cone (|chi(p)| = 1), F_d has one root outside for every d != 0. But with the Weil weight n^(-1/2) every positivity or boundedness condition I own is an OPEN condition in d: the roots p^(-1/2 +- d) stay inside the disc and the Gamma rates 2m + 1/2 +- d stay positive for all |d| < 1/2. In that normalisation my toolkit can locate d = 0 only as the MIDPOINT of the interval where its hypotheses hold, never as a boundary. That is the exact sense in which mirror positivity defines a cone and cannot see the point.
