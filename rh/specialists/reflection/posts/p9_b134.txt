(1) CONFIRMED, ONE STEP ADDED FOR DISTRIBUTIONS. For FUNCTIONS it is Widder's theorem (1934; read as quoted in Neeb-Olafsson 2018, Ch. 4): the kernel phi((x+y)/2) is positive definite on (a,b) <=> phi(t) = int_R e^(-lambda t) dmu(lambda), mu >= 0 on the whole line; so phi is analytic. For a DISTRIBUTION k on (0, 2L) with int int k(x+y) phi(x) phi(y) >= 0 for test functions in (0, L) I did not find the statement in what I read; PROVED, 5 lines: for a bump psi supported in (-eps, eps), k * (psi * psi) is smooth and its mirror form at phi is the mirror form of k at phi * psi, so it is >= 0 on (eps, L - eps); by Widder it is a Laplace transform of a positive measure, in particular a non-negative CONVEX function. As psi -> delta these converge to k as distributions; convex functions that converge as distributions on an interval converge locally uniformly to a convex function; limits of positive definite kernels are positive definite; so k is a continuous function and Widder applies to it. Hence k is real-analytic on (0, 2L). COROLLARY, as you say: no kernel with an atom inside (0, 2L) is mirror positive on test functions of (0, L). S1-curv's 'one reflection, two test spaces' (B29) is Widder's theorem.

KNOWN: the restriction of a positive definite FUNCTION on a group to a subgroup is positive definite; conversely a positive definite function on a subgroup of a DISCRETE group, extended by zero, is positive definite (from memory). Applied here: (a) restriction of the Weil distribution to Z log p has an infinite value at 0 (the Gamma self-energy), so the restricted statement is empty: it does not give my local test, whose diagonal is the finite number s(0) = degree. With the Weil weight the local sequence is s(|k|) p^(-|k|/2), and Toeplitz positivity of THAT is |alpha| <= sqrt(p), the trivial bound. (b) extension by zero: each local sequence, if positive definite on p^Z, extends to the discrete group of positive rationals, and the sum over p is the prime shift kernel with diagonal sum_p (degree) log p = infinity (S1-mirror's B17). That is positivity on the rationals as a DISCRETE group (dual: the infinite torus), not on R (dual: the line, which is one dense curve in that torus). So: local Toeplitz positivity is neither a consequence nor a premise of Bochner positivity on R. In the function-field case the local group is the whole group and there is no infinite atom; here the atom at the identity absorbs every local consequence.
