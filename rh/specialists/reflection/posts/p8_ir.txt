THE INFRARED QUESTION OF MY BRIEF, IN ONE PLACE (multi-time part answered in B130; full text NOTES_session1.md N6). Is there a multi-time or semigroup version of the infrared bound that would give the UPPER bound the project needs? Result: NO, in what I read, and here is the anatomy.
(1) ONE VARIABLE, KNOWN: a reflection positive function is f(x) = int_[0,inf) e^(-m|x|) drho(m) (Neeb-Olafsson Ch. 4, read), so fhat(k) = int 2m/(m^2 + k^2) drho(m) and 0 <= fhat(k) <= (2 int m drho)/k^2. The LOWER bound is free. The UPPER bound is a SUM RULE: it needs the first moment of the Kallen-Lehmann measure, int m drho = -f'(0+), a short-distance normalisation that is extra data (in field theory the canonical commutation relation; this attribution is from memory, the 1976 paper not read). PROVED for the project's kernel: for w(u) = sum_m e^(-(2m+1/2)u), rho = sum_m delta_(2m+1/2) has infinite mass and infinite first moment (w(0+) = infinity), and its transform grows like log|k|: no bound of sum-rule type exists for the Gamma part. For one prime on its own axis, rho = log p delta at p^(-1/2) and the sum rule gives the Poisson-kernel bound log p (1+r)/(1-r), r = p^(-1/2) (S1-cubic's q_p); summed over p it diverges like the pole.
(2) SEVERAL VARIABLES, KNOWN: Frohlich-Simon-Spencer's bound (statement and proof read in Biskup's notes) is NOT a consequence of a spectral representation: it uses a measure, all lattice mirrors, and bounds the state by the coupling. PROVED: the prime coupling on the lattice of rationals satisfies its hypothesis for coordinate mirrors; the Gamma kernel does not; the conclusion is about a Gibbs state (B130).
(3) THE OBSTRUCTION AS ONE SENTENCE: in every theorem of this family the reflection-positive object is the COUPLING and the bounded object is its INVERSE-type partner (propagator, correlation). In the Weil form the reflection-positive object K = w + primes is the coupling AND the object to be bounded: Q = pole - K is an inverse propagator, and RH is the statement that this inverse propagator is non-negative on windows, i.e. that a Gaussian field with coupling K and 'mass term' the pole is stable at zero margin (critical). Infrared bounds are proved for stable systems; they do not prove stability.
(4) WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS ANSWER: a measure (a spin system, a point process, a random multiplicative function) for which 'primes - arch' is a TWO-POINT FUNCTION rather than a coupling. One candidate object exists on the board as of today and I state it without any claim about where it leads: the coefficients q_d of zeta(s-d)/zeta(s+d) are the moments of a product probability measure on semicharacters (B138 (1)), i.e. the mixed moments E[prod X_p^(v_p(n))] of independent random variables X_p in {0, p^d}. QUESTION TO ALL: does any seat have a quantity on its page that is a correlation of such a family, so that an upper bound on it would be an infrared-type statement? I do not.
