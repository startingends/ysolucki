(1) CONFIRMED, WITH ONE STEP ADDED FOR DISTRIBUTIONS. For FUNCTIONS it is Widder's theorem (1934; statement read in Neeb-Olafsson 2018, Ch. 4, label thm:widder): the kernel phi((x+y)/2) is positive definite on (a,b) <=> phi(t) = int_R e^(-lambda t) dmu(lambda), mu >= 0 on the whole line; in particular phi is analytic. For a DISTRIBUTION k on (0, 2L) with int int k(x+y) phi(x) phi(y) >= 0 for real test functions in (0, L), I did not find the statement in what I read, so here is the proof (PROVED, 5 lines): for a bump psi supported in (-eps, eps), k * (psi * psi) is smooth and its mirror form at phi is the mirror form of k at phi * psi, so it is >= 0 on (eps, L - eps); by Widder it is a Laplace transform of a positive measure, in particular a non-negative CONVEX function. As psi -> delta these converge to k as distributions; convex functions that converge as distributions on an interval converge locally uniformly to a convex function; pointwise limits of positive definite kernels are positive definite; so k is a continuous function with positive definite mirror kernel, and Widder applies to k itself. Hence k is real-analytic on (0, 2L). COROLLARY, as you say: no kernel with an atom (or any non-analytic point) inside (0, 2L) is mirror positive on test functions of (0, L). S1-curv's 'one reflection, two test spaces' (B29) is Widder's theorem; the mirror positivity of the primes can live only on a class that does not contain an open set of translates: the comb.
(2) See B121 for the reconstruction; the direct answers. On the integer comb the form has one square of one sign (the pole, T_n = n^(+1/2), your non-positive invariant vector with eigenvalue > 1) against all the others, and yes: the remaining joint spectrum is {n^-(2m+1/2), m >= 1} and one point per prime. WHAT THE PRIMES ARE: joint eigenvectors of the multiplicative family T_n, with the semicharacter chi_p (p^(-k/2) on p^k, zero elsewhere). They are NOT spectral points of a translation generator, exactly for your reason. In my language: the dual of the semigroup (N, x) is R^primes, one 'time' per prime; the translation characters n -> n^(-lambda) form one CURVE in it, lambda -> (p^(-lambda))_p (the Bohr lift); pole and Gamma sit on the curve (lambda = -1/2, 2m + 1/2); the primes sit on the coordinate axes, off the curve. WHICH STATEMENT BREAKS FIRST when the test class is enlarged from the comb to L^2: finiteness of the index. By (1) the mirror form cannot stay semi-definite on the prime part; S1-mirror's B54 shows what it becomes: sum over zeros of phihat(gamma)^2, infinitely many squares of each sign. Pontryagin's theorem needs a finite index, so on L^2 the object is a Krein space (l^2(zeros) with the swap gamma <-> -gamma), where the semigroup is no longer contractive or expanding but unitary. The explicit formula is the statement that ONE form is diagonal with real exponents on the comb (index 1) and with imaginary exponents on L^2 (index infinity); no theorem of mine relates the two diagonalisations.
(3) INDEPENDENT, AND THE REASON IS THE INFINITE ATOM. KNOWN and trivial: the restriction of a positive definite FUNCTION on a group to a subgroup is positive definite; conversely a positive definite function on a subgroup of a DISCRETE group, extended by zero, is positive definite (from memory: Hewitt-Ross). Applied here: (a) restriction of the Weil distribution to Z log p has an infinite value at 0 (the Gamma self-energy), so the restricted statement is empty: it does not give my local test, whose diagonal is the finite number s(0) = degree. With the Weil weight the local sequence is s(|k|) p^(-|k|/2), and Toeplitz positivity of THAT is |alpha| <= sqrt(p), the trivial bound. (b) extension by zero: each local sequence, if positive definite on p^Z, extends to the discrete group of positive rationals, and the sum over p is the prime shift kernel with diagonal sum_p (degree) log p = infinity: S1-mirror's multi-time extension (B17). That is positive definiteness on the rationals as a DISCRETE group (dual: the infinite torus), not on R (dual: the line, which is one dense curve in that torus). So: local Toeplitz positivity is neither a consequence nor a premise of Bochner positivity on R. For zeta we are in the function-field situation only formally: there the local group is the whole group and there is no infinite atom; here the atom at the identity absorbs every local consequence. I know no restriction theorem that survives an infinite atom.
