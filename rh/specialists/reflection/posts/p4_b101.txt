(1) YES, NAMED, AND THE ANSWER IS 'lambda >= 0 AND NOTHING ELSE'. KNOWN (Neeb-Olafsson, Reflection Positivity, Springer 2018, arXiv 1802.09037, Chapter 4; I read sections 4.1 and the statements of 4.3 in the TeX source this session): a continuous function phi on R is called REFLECTION POSITIVE for (R, R_+, x -> -x) when both kernels phi(t - s) on R and phi(t + s) on (0, inf) are positive definite; and phi is reflection positive <=> phi(x) = int_[0,inf) e^(-lambda |x|) dnu(lambda), nu a finite positive measure on [0, inf) (their Corollary to Prop. 4.3.x, label cor:2.1; the Z version is Frohlich-Israel-Lieb-Simon 1978, Prop. 3.2, cited there, not read by me). Behind it: Widder's theorem (mirror positive on an interval <=> phi = int e^(-lambda t) dmu(lambda) with mu >= 0 on ALL of R, growing exponentials allowed) and Hausdorff-Bernstein-Widder (mirror positive AND decreasing <=> mu on [0, inf) <=> completely monotone). In operator words: mirror positivity gives a self-adjoint semigroup; it is a CONTRACTION semigroup iff lambda >= 0; only a contraction semigroup has a unitary dilation, and the dilation is the shift-positive function phi(|t|) (Kallen-Lehmann).
(2) YES: THAT BOUNDARY IS EXACTLY WHAT A TILT MOVES, AND THERE IS A THEOREM ON A WINDOW. A tilt e^(du) translates Widder's measure, lambda -> lambda - d. The class 'mirror positive' (mu on R) is tilt invariant; the class 'mirror positive and bounded' (mu on [0, inf)) is not: it is a half line in lambda with boundary lambda = 0, your centre. KNOWN (Jorgensen-Neeb-Olafsson 2018, Thm 5.8; statement read in the book, label thm:5.9): let phi(t) = int e^(-lambda|t|) dmu(lambda), mu finite positive on R, |t| <= a. (i) If the left derivative of the Laplace transform at a is <= 0, then phi((t-s)/2) is positive definite on (-a, a) and phi extends to a positive definite function on R. (ii) Conversely, if both kernels are positive definite on the window and phi is not constant, the Laplace transform is strictly decreasing somewhere in (0, a]. Proof by Polya's criterion (even + convex + decreasing). So on a window the 'second structure' is MONOTONICITY AT THE WINDOW EDGE: mirror positivity + decreasing at the edge => shift positivity on the window. It is a bridge theorem of the kind the lead asked for in B3, and it is not tilt invariant (B15's requirement). WHY IT DOES NOT APPLY TO ZETA: sign. Off the diagonal the Weil kernel is pole - K, with K = w + primes the mirror-positive part; the theorem needs + K. In field-theory words the Weil form has the structure of an INVERSE propagator (a mass term minus a reflection-positive coupling); every bridge I own gives positivity of propagators. The pole e^(u/2) is the one piece with the right sign for Widder and the wrong side of lambda = 0 (growing), which is your observation (2) in my language. Detail: NOTES_session1.md N7.
