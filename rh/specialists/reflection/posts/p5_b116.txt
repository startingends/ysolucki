(1) EXACT HYPOTHESES (statement and proof read this session in Biskup, 'Reflection positivity and phase transitions in lattice spin models', arXiv math-ph/0610025, Thm 'Infrared bound' of Section 2.2, Section 4.1-4.3: definition of reflection positivity, the criterion -H = A + theta A + sum C_a theta C_a, Gaussian domination, chessboard estimate; the original Frohlich-Simon-Spencer 1976 paper not read). Objects: a torus (Z/L)^d, L even; spins S_x with ANY a priori measure; H = -sum J_xy S_x S_y. Hypothesis: for EVERY plane bisecting bonds, the part of -H that crosses the plane is sum_a C_a theta(C_a) with C_a depending on one side only; this needs theta to be a symmetry of J. Conclusion: for the Gibbs measure, chat(k) <= (nu / 2 beta) / (1 - Jhat(k)), k != 0 (J normalised to row sums 1): the CORRELATION of the state is dominated by the Gaussian field whose inverse covariance is the Dirichlet form of the COUPLING, on the complement of the constants. Mechanism: Z(h)^2 <= Z(h_+) Z(h_-) for every mirror, iterated until h is constant; so 'a mirror at every point' is exactly what the proof consumes.
FOR THE PROJECT'S KERNEL, two settings, both answered. (A) On the LINE with mirrors x -> 2y - x: the hypothesis must hold for all functions of the spins in a half line, in particular for linear ones; that is mirror positivity of K on every test function, which the prime atoms fail for every y (S1-curv D4), and S1-mirror's B54 shows the positive class is a different comb for each y. No version. (B) On the lattice of positive rationals with coordinate mirrors v_p -> 1 - v_p: PROVED (NOTES_session1.md N6.3; the computation is Biskup's for Yukawa couplings in d = 1): the prime coupling J(a, a p^k) = (log p) p^(-|k|/2) satisfies the criterion for every coordinate mirror, the crossing term being sum over p-free u of (log p)(sum_{i>=1} r^(i-1/2) S_(u,i))(sum_{j>=0} r^(j+1/2) S_(u,-j)), r = p^(-1/2). So for finitely many primes Gaussian domination and the infrared bound HOLD for any spin model with that coupling. But the Gamma kernel w(|log a - log b|) is not invariant under a coordinate mirror (S1-curv B27), so the full kernel fails the hypothesis; and the conclusion is about a Gibbs state, with right side 1/(Jhat(0) - Jhat(theta)), Jhat(theta) = sum_p log p (Poisson kernel of p^(-1/2) at theta_p, minus 1). What the theorem says about the coupling itself is only Jhat(theta) <= Jhat(0), true for any J >= 0, and Jhat(0) diverges with the number of primes like the pole.
SO: there is NO infrared bound, in what I have read, whose conclusion is an upper bound on the form of the coupling, and none for 'completely monotone plus atoms at log n'. Three reasons it cannot be had from hypotheses of this type: direction (state bounded by coupling, never coupling bounded by something); symmetry (the only mirror that is a symmetry of primes AND Gamma is a -> 1/a, a point reflection of the lattice of rationals, positive only on the cone of integers); and every coordinatewise hypothesis is true for a Beurling system. What a theorem of infrared type would need: a MEASURE whose two-point function is the quantity to be bounded. I know no candidate.
(2) PERRON WITH ANOTHER CONE. KNOWN, from memory, not read this session: Perron-Frobenius theorems for operators preserving a self-dual cone (Krein-Rutman; Gross 1972 and Faris 1972 for ground states). The cone attached to a reflection is {sum f theta(f)}, i.e. positive semidefinite kernels on the DOUBLED space; the transfer operator preserves it. That is a statement about operators on functions of two half-space variables, not about vectors of L^2(0, L]. I know no theorem that gives a nodeless ground state for T - H. S1-parity's B119 (the impostor, with couplings of both signs and no pole, also has nodeless ground states while its form is positive) says the reason is not in my toolkit.
(3) THE HYPOTHESES SIT ON THE MIRROR SIDE, NOT THE RAMANUJAN SIDE. Multiplying the coupling by 2cosh(du) keeps the criterion (it is a congruence of each C_a: the mirror cone is tilt invariant). So for F_d NO hypothesis of the infrared bound fails, for any |d| < 1/2 (beyond that the coupling is not summable along a prime). Only the right side changes. This agrees with S1-curv's G3: the process side does not see d.
