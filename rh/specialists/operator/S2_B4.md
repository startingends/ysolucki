## (a) The structure: tensor factorisation on BOXES, glued to the simplex window by Buchstab's recursion
Divisor matrix T_1 = [1_{a|b}] on l^2(N). In exponent coordinates (v_p(n))_p it is the tensor product over primes of the
one-variable upper-triangular all-ones Toeplitz matrices U_p (symbol (1 - z_p)^(-1)), and T_{1/zeta} = tensor of (I - S_p).
(a1) BOX IDENTITY (PROVED). For a box B = {d : d | P}, P = prod_{p<=z} p (squarefree box; any product of local windows works),
     P_B T_1 P_B = tensor over p <= z of the 2x2 matrix [[1,1],[0,1]], and for c = mu 1_B:
         (T_1 c)(j) = sum_{d|j, d|P} mu(d) = 1_{(j,P)=1}   for EVERY j (not only on the window).
     So on a box the Moebius vector has an exactly known image everywhere: the Eratosthenes indicator. Its partial sums
     Phi(k;P) = #{j <= k : (j,P) = 1} are periodic up to the linear term k phi(P)/P, period P; over a FULL period (family length P,
     sum length 2^pi(z) terms of size up to P) the mean square is exact (finite Fourier analysis, Ramanujan sums).
(a2) THE SIMPLEX WINDOW {1..N} is not a box. Buchstab (largest prime factor) decomposition, PROVED and checked:
         R_N(k) = - sum_{p <= k} R^(<p)_{N/p}( floor(k/p) ),    R^(<p)_M(y) := sum_{M < d <= y, P+(d) < p} mu(d) floor(y/d),
     (12 cases, N in {5, 12, 30}, k in {40, 77, 150, 301}: 0 mismatches). Iterated, it writes the window as a disjoint union of
     products {p_1 > p_2 > ... } x (box of smaller primes): every piece is a tensor product, the gluing is exact and finite.
     "The sum" = d in (N, k] (length ~ X on the block k ~ X); "the family" = k ~ X. After one step: the family is the PAIRS (p, k)
     and the sums have length X/p: H/D grows from 1 to about pi(X)·(X/p)... i.e. the bilinear (Type I / Type II) structure.

## (b) Near side
On k ~ X the recursion turns R_N(k) into bilinear forms sum_{p} sum_{d'} alpha(p) beta(d') floor(k/(p d')) with alpha, beta EXACT
(mu restricted to smooth numbers), plus Type I pieces with d' small that are evaluated exactly by (a1) (periodic, explicit).
RISK, my expectation, not computed: the Type I part is harmless; the Type II part with p in [X^a, X^(1-a)] can be bounded by the
long-family large sieve (pairs (p, d') against k), giving cancellation only away from the configuration "d = one large prime times
a smooth part with the prime near X"; at that configuration the decomposition reaches the trivial bound. So I predict it gives
R_N(k) at the strength of the prime number theorem (X exp(-c sqrt(log X))), NOT sqrt(X): no exponent below 1.
The named reason: Selberg's parity barrier (KNOWLEDGE B8). Sieve-type decompositions use only the data (Type I sums, sieve
weights) that do not distinguish mu from functions with the same values on numbers with few prime factors; the passage box ->
simplex is exactly the sieve truncation problem (Legendre box -> Brun/Selberg simplex), and the NB weights need sifting level
s = log D/log z = 1 (sift down to {1} with D = z = N): the parity-critical point.
The pole bookkeeping (PROVED): on the box M_c(1) = phi(P)/P ~ e^(-gamma)/log z, so the box vector alone is a bad Nyman-Beurling
approximant (HEURISTIC: its distance^2 is about z e^(-2 gamma)/log^2 z, from the interval 1 <= 1/x <= z, and grows); the simplex needs M_N(1) = sum_{d<=N} mu(d)/d to be tiny, which is
PNT-strength input. The box knows the Euler product exactly and the pole badly; the simplex the other way round.

## (d) Outside
