# S3_B5: SP-operator on exit E6 (weighted or thinned families), long form of the board answer to s3 B5

2026-11-11. Read: rh/board/s3 B1-B6; rh/specialists/sieve/COPY_BARRIER.md (whole); verifier/VERDICT.md section 3 (whole);
NOTEBOOK N69-N71; the 11/12 digest (earlier). Labels: PROVED / KNOWN (what I read) / CONJECTURE / RISK.
Notation as in COPY_BARRIER section 0: rows u of a family F (multiset), A_u = sum_{n<=D} mu(n) psi_u(n) W(n/D), copies = rows with
psi_u = 1_{(n, r_u)=1}, eta-copies with sum_{p|r_u} 1/N(p) <= eta. A weight is w: F -> [0, inf); S_w := sum_u w_u |A_u|^2,
W_tot := sum_u w_u, C_w := sum over eta-copies of w_u. V_w := the matrix (w_u^(1/2) psi_u(n))_{u,n}, so S_w = ||V_w a||^2, a = mu W.

## (a) The weighted chain, and what the verifier's optimality step covers
P1 (PROVED, same proof as section 0). For every w:  |A_1|^2 <= 2 S_w / C_w + 2 (eta D + O(log D))^2.
   (Cauchy-Schwarz with the weights w restricted to the copies: |sum_cop w_u A_u|^2 <= C_w sum_cop w_u |A_u|^2 <= C_w S_w, and
   sum_cop w_u A_u = C_w A_1 + O(C_w (eta D + log D)).)
COVERED by VERDICT.md section 3: w uniform or smooth in N(u)/H on the FULL family F_H, with any weights c on the copies inside the
Cauchy-Schwarz step (uniform c optimal), any subset of copies, any H. NOT COVERED: (i) w correlated with the copy set (extra weight
on copies or on cosets of the kernel); (ii) thinning to a subfamily that keeps all copies; (iii) w depending on D (e.g. amplifier
weights built from characters of length L = D^lambda); (iv) any w for which (H2) is assumed for S_w itself rather than for F_H.

## (b) What a weight that is not covered must give
P2 (PROVED, arithmetic of P1). |A_1| << D^(3/4 - c) needs S_w << C_w D^(3/2 - 2c - eps). If S_w is bounded at its diagonal,
   S_w <= W_tot D^(1+eps), this is the condition C_w / W_tot >= D^(-1/2 + 2c + 2 eps): the WEIGHTED copy fraction must exceed D^(-1/2).
   (Full family, uniform weights: C/H << H^(-1/2) <= D^(-1/2 + eps0/2), Theorem 2: exactly the barrier.)

## (c) The copy directions are ALWAYS top singular directions; what must change is the alignment of mu with them
P3 (PROVED; weighted Theorem 1). With 1 the all-ones vector on [1, D]: <V_w^* V_w 1, 1> >= C_w (1 - eta)^2 D^2, so
   ||V_w||^2 >= (1 - eta)^2 C_w D, for EVERY weight. No reweighting or thinning that keeps copies can stop the copy direction from
   being (up to (1 - eta)^2) a top direction of the sampling operator; a family "in which the copies are not top" does not exist.
   What the weighted rows of exact copies see of the coefficient vector is its component along 1 on the coprime n: that component IS
   A_1 (up to the eta-error). So the requirement of P2 is that mu be nearly orthogonal to the top direction: the trivial-twist
   statement of my S2 file (SHORT_FAMILY 2(b)), not a new structural property.
P4 (PROVED; the circularity of copy-concentrated weights). Write w = w' + w'' with w'' supported on the eta-copies. Then exactly
       S_w = S_w' + C_w'' |A_1|^2 + O( C_w'' (eta D + log D) (|A_1| + eta D + log D) ).
   So the weight added on the copies contributes C_w'' |A_1|^2 and nothing else. A diagonal bound S_w <= (W' + C_w'') D^(1+eps) then
   gives |A_1|^2 <= D^(1+eps) (1 + W'/C_w''), which for C_w'' >~ W' is RH-strength; and no argument can bound the C_w'' |A_1|^2 term
   without bounding A_1: the extra weight on copies proves exactly what it assumes. Thinning to a subfamily that keeps the copies
   is the case w' = smaller: same conclusion. This makes exit E6 CIRCULAR in the lead's S2 B9 sense for every weight that is not
   controlled by the same computation as the whole family. The only non-circular weights are those the PROOF of the diagonal can
   carry, i.e. weights the first step (Poisson in u) can handle.
P5 (PROVED identity; the one non-circular boosting weight I know, Iwaniec's amplifier). In the October family psi_u(n) = (u/n)_6,
   and u -> (u/l)_6 is a character modulo l, so the weight w_u = |B_u|^2, B_u := sum_{l <= L} b_l (u/l)_6, IS Poisson-able in u.
   On a copy B_u = sum b_l (up to coprimality), so C_w = C |sum b_l|^2 (1 + o(1)); on average over u, |B_u|^2 ~ sum |b_l|^2. And,
   by multiplicativity of the symbol in the bottom entry,
          S_w = sum_u | sum_m beta(m) (u/m)_6 |^2,     beta := b * (mu W)   (Dirichlet convolution; length L D).
   So amplification by |B_u|^2 is the PLAIN mean square of the longer coefficient beta. If beta had a diagonal, S_w <= H (sum |beta|^2) D^eps
   with H >= L D, then |A_1|^2 <= H D L/(C L^2) and the exponent would improve with L (with b_l = 1: |A_1|^2 << D^(3/2 - lambda/2), L = D^lambda).
   FIRST FAILING LINE: the mu-specific diagonal (digest step 5) uses that the coefficient is mu(n) times a character POINTWISE on
   squarefree n (gamma_2(p)^3 = -alpha(p): "the -1 at each prime IS mu") to turn sextic Gauss sums into cubic theta coefficients.
   beta = b * mu is not of that form unless b is a point mass; the Gauss-Jacobi conversion is unavailable, and the diagonal for beta is
   only the general large sieve, which by Theorem 1 pays for the copies (Corollary 1: no better than trivial). So Iwaniec weights are
   Poisson-able but destroy the mu-specific input.
P6 (where it meets the parity wall; RISK, heuristic). To boost copies the amplifier needs |sum b_l|^2 >> sum |b_l|^2 . (copy-free average),
   i.e. b positive-biased; then beta = b * mu is a SIEVE-WEIGHTED Moebius function, and a mu-specific diagonal for it is the statement
   that positive weights preserve the cancellation of mu: Selberg's parity barrier (KNOWLEDGE B8) says sieve weights cannot see the
   sign of mu. If instead b is mu-like (b_l = mu(l) . (character)), beta keeps a Gauss-sum structure but |sum b_l| is small: no boost.
   RISK (one candidate that keeps structure): b supported on cubes l = k^3, where (u/k^3)_6 = (u/k)_2 is quadratic; the copies it
   boosts are the copies of the QUADRATIC subfamily (u squares), of density H^(1/2): by Theorem 2 that lands exactly ON 3/4, not below.

## (d) Summary of what is proved and the first failing line
- P1-P4 PROVED: any weight works in the chain; copies are always top directions; weight placed on copies contributes exactly
  C |A_1|^2, so concentrating weight is circular; the requirement to beat 3/4 is a weighted copy fraction above D^(-1/2) together with
  a diagonal the proof can carry.
- P5 PROVED as an identity: the only Poisson-able boosting weight I know turns the problem into the mean square of b * mu over the
  same family; its first failing line is the Gauss-Jacobi step (mu pointwise).
- P6 RISK: positive-biased amplifiers meet the parity wall; structured (cube) amplifiers meet Theorem 2 at exactly 3/4.
So E6 meets: the copy barrier for smooth weights (verifier), circularity for copy-concentrated weights (P4), the loss of the mu-specific
identity for Poisson-able amplifier weights (P5), and parity or Theorem 2 for the two ways of repairing P5 (P6).
