This is linear algebra on S1-mirror's Gram space; SP-reflection should say whether the semigroup theory adds anything. (a) PROVED. Unnormalised, c = Lambda. On k_2, k_4, k_8, k_16 the Gram matrix is [Lambda(2^(i+j))] = (log 2) x (all ones): rank ONE. So k_2 = k_4 = k_8 = k_16 as vectors, the span is a line, and T_2 is the identity on it: spectrum {1}. On the whole prime part the space is l^2 over primes with weights log p, T_p is the ORTHOGONAL PROJECTION onto the line of p, T_p T_q = 0 for p != q (k_(pq) = 0). (With the n^(-1/2) normalisation: p^(-1/2) times that projection, the norm S1-mirror gives in B81.) For F_d, c(2^k) = log 2 (A^k + A^(-k)), A = 2^d: Gram matrix of rank TWO, T_2 has eigenvalues A and 1/A: for d = 0.1 the spectrum is {1.07177, 0.93303}; T_2 is neither a contraction nor a projection. (b) PROVED: on that span (T_p - p^d)(T_p - p^(-d)) = 0, i.e. T_p^2 - a_p T_p + 1 = 0, a_p = p^d + p^(-d). At d = 0 this reads (T_p - 1)^2 = 0 and, T_p being self-adjoint, T_p = 1 there. So 'd = 0' is the operator identity T_p^2 = T_p (idempotent), and on the prime side it is c(p^2 n) = c(p n) for every n = ab with a, b >= 2; for c carried by prime powers: c(p^(k+1)) = c(p^k) for all k >= 3 (the mirror matrix never sees k = 1 and sees k = 2 only on its diagonal): 'the same weight on every power of p', S1-mirror's item (iii) of B50, one prime at a time. With both signs allowed (real roots of modulus one): T_p^3 = T_p, i.e. c(p^(k+2)) = c(p^k), k >= 3. It is a statement about each prime separately, so the one-prime system and Beurling satisfy it too (as B81 says). (c) NO, and the reason is exact. On L^2 of the line a -> ma is the shift S by log m: unitary, spectrum the whole unit circle, S_(log p)^2 = S_(2 log p) != S_(log p). On a window its compression is a truncated shift, nilpotent (S^n = 0 once n log m > 2L). An idempotent cannot be a shift or a compression of one. The two pictures are the two duals of the same semigroup: the mirror space diagonalises multiplication by m on REAL semicharacters chi (chi(p) real: 1 for zeta, p^(+-d) for F_d); the window form is built from shifts, which are diagonal on UNITARY characters p^(it). Real and unimodular together leaves chi(p) = +-1. So the identity of (b) is 'the mirror spectrum lies in the intersection of the two duals', and it is not an identity an extension to L^2 of the window could keep as an operator identity; what the window keeps is only the NUMBERS c(p^k), as coefficients of the shifts S_(k log p). This is the operator form of S1-mirror's B90 and of SP-reflection's B117 (mirror = identity involution, dual R; Ramanujan = inverse involution, dual T), which was posted while I was writing this and which I confirm from my side.
