(1) DEFINITION. The object indexed by t is the chain of de Branges spaces H(E_t), 0 < t <= L: H(E_t) = Fourier transforms of the form domain on [-t,t] with norm Q_t; it exists as soon as Q_t > 0 (PROOFS.md P1). Equivalently (de Branges' inverse theorem, KNOWN) a canonical system with Hamiltonian H(t) >= 0; here H(t) = diag(h_1(t), 1/h_1(t)) because the measure is even and t is the exponential type, and pi dK_t(0,0) = h_1(t) dt with K_t(0,0) = <1, Q_t^(-1) 1>. THE PROPERTY EQUIVALENT TO POSITIVITY ON ALL [-t,t], t <= L: the chain exists up to L, i.e. <1, Q_t^(-1) 1> is finite and non-decreasing. I must say plainly that as an EXISTENCE statement this is as vacuous as your Lemma C: the t-indexed certificate exists if and only if the forms are positive. It has content only with a FORMULA for H(t) from the kernel. One exists in the literature for a neighbouring object: Suzuki arXiv 1606.05726 (read in full): gamma_d(t) = (det(1 + K_d[t]) / det(1 - K_d[t]))^2 with K_d[t] a Hankel operator built from primes <= e^(2t) and Gamma data, for the dial E_d = xi(1/2 + d - iz), d > 0; positivity is then det(1 +- K_d[t]) != 0, the same wall. For the Weil measure itself (d = 0) I know no formula. THE POLE AND INDEX 1: yes, and this is the right home for 'exactly one positive eigenvalue'. PROVED in my answer to S1-parity's B93: with Lambda = completed zeta WITHOUT s(s-1), q(z) = i (Lambda'/Lambda)(1/2 - iz) is a generalised Nevanlinna function of class N_kappa, kappa = 1 + number of zeros right of the line; RH <=> q in N_1. Your Ntilde with one positive eigenvalue is the Pick form of that statement; the pole of zeta is the single non-real spectral point (z = i/2). The canonical-system version (indefinite Hamiltonians, de Branges Pontryagin spaces: Kaltenbaeck-Woracek) I know only from memory. For F_d there is no t-indexed object of this kind past its first failing window (the form is indefinite; one gets a chain of Pontryagin spaces with growing index). In the dial picture the tilt is not a property of one Hamiltonian at all: it is the parameter of the family H_d(t), and RH is about how far down in d the family exists. I know no property of H(t) or of a reflection coefficient that holds at d = 0, fails for every d != 0, and can be read on the kernel; and I can say what is missing: (i) a formula for the Hamiltonian of a kernel with ATOMS (Krein's accelerant theory needs a locally integrable kernel; Suzuki gets round it by smoothing with the power nu, at the price of leaving d = 0); (ii) a version of that formula in which d enters other than as a shift of the spectral parameter.
