## 0. The object, with its constants. PROVED (Hecke; statement read in Zagier 1981, Example 1; checked to 9 digits)
K = Q(sqrt(-3)), O = Z[omega], omega = e^(2 pi i/3), E*(z,s) = pi^(-s) Gamma(s) (1/2) sum'_{(m,n)} y^s |mz+n|^(-2s). Then
   Phi(s) := E*(omega, s) = 3 (sqrt3/(2 pi))^s Gamma(s) zeta_K(s) = (sqrt3/2) xi(s) Lambda(s, chi_-3),
xi(s) = pi^(-s/2) Gamma(s/2) zeta(s), Lambda(s,chi_-3) = (3/pi)^((s+1)/2) Gamma((s+1)/2) L(s,chi_-3). Phi(s) = Phi(1-s), poles at
0, 1, real on Re s = 1/2. Reason: |m omega + n|^2 = N(n + m omega), Im omega = sqrt3/2, six units.
Consequence that limits everything below (PROVED): the zeros of Phi are those of zeta and of L(s,chi_-3). Any mechanism
that puts the zeros of s -> E*(omega,s) on the line proves RH for zeta itself; the extra field buys no slack by itself.

## 1. Periods of the Eisenstein family: which parameter each one sees
(1a) UNIPOTENT period (constant term). KNOWN, read (Zagier (14)): int_0^1 E*(x+iy,s) dx = xi(2s) y^s + xi(2s-1) y^(1-s).
     Parameter 2s. All of Maass-Selberg, scattering, Lax-Phillips, and the half-shift wall live here (my B212).
(1b) COMPACT torus (CM points). KNOWN, read (Zagier Example 1, Prop. 1): for a positive form Q of discriminant D < 0 with
     root z_Q and class A: E*(z_Q,s) = (w/2)(|D|/4)^(s/2) pi^(-s) Gamma(s) zeta(A,s); summed over classes: zeta(s) L(s,D), with
     L(s,D) entire, and for D, Df^2 the functions differ by a finite Euler product. PROVED from it (finite Fourier
     analysis on the class group, D fundamental): sum_A psi(A) E*(z_A,s) = (w/2)(|D|/4)^(s/2) pi^(-s) Gamma(s) L_K(s,psi) for
     every class group character psi. Parameter s.
(1c) SPLIT torus (closed geodesics, D > 0 non-square). KNOWN, read (Zagier Example 2, after Hecke): sum over classes of
     int over C_Q of E*(z,s) |d_Q z| = pi^(-s) D^(s/2) Gamma(s/2)^2 zeta_K(s), K real quadratic. Parameter s.
     Degenerate split torus (the geodesic from 0 to i inf): Hecke's integral, xi(S+d) xi(S-d) = 2 int_0^inf
     [E*(iy,1/2+d) - constant term] y^(S-1/2) dy/y (PROVED, my NOTES D2): the project's window variable is log y HERE.
(1d) TORUS WITH A CHARACTER of infinite order. PROVED (one line; classical Hecke): for 6 | k,
     sum'_{beta in O} (conj(beta)/|beta|)^k N(beta)^(-s) = 6 L(s, conj(alpha)^k), alpha(beta) = beta/|beta|; the left side is the value at
     omega of the weight-k Eisenstein series (the k-th Maass raising of E(.,s), up to Gamma factors). So the K-types of the
     representation generated by E(.,s), evaluated at omega, give all unramified Hecke L-functions of K at parameter s.
     Adelically (KNOWN, read: Zagier sec. 5; Cornelissen-Lorscheid, abstracts): the integral of an Eisenstein series over
     T(Q)Z(A)\T(A) against a character Omega is the Tate integral of L_K(s, Omega).
(1e) RANKIN-SELBERG (period against |f|^2). KNOWN, read (Zagier Example 3): int y^k |f|^2 E*(z,s) dmu =
     4^(-s-k+1) pi^(-2s-k+1) Gamma(s) Gamma(s+k-1) zeta(s) D_f(s+k-1), D_f entire (the symmetric square). Parameter s.
(1f) WALDSPURGER. FROM MEMORY (Waldspurger 1985; read only: Cornelissen-Lorscheid's statement that toroidal cusp forms
     are those with L(pi,1/2) = 0): for a cusp form the SQUARE of the torus period is L(1/2, pi_K x Omega) times local
     factors over L(1, Ad). For the Eisenstein series pi_s the same L-value is zeta_K(s) zeta_K(1-s) and the period itself
     is zeta_K(s): Hecke's formula is the Eisenstein case of Waldspurger with the square root taken globally.
SUMMARY. Parameter s: periods over tori (1b, 1c, 1d) and Rankin-Selberg-type periods (1e). Parameter 2s: the unipotent
period (1a), hence every constant term and every norm built from it. Phi is the simplest period at parameter s: the
torus T_K(A)/T_K(Q)Z(A) is a single point because the class number is one.

## 2. The point omega and the cubic cover: how the two pictures are related
(2a) What lives where. FROM MEMORY (Kubota 1969-71, Patterson 1977; the digest quotes Patterson Thm 8.1): an n-fold
     metaplectic cover of SL_2 over a field F exists with the usual properties only if F contains the n-th roots of
     unity; so the cubic cover lives over K, on hyperbolic 3-space, for a congruence subgroup of SL_2(Z[omega]), with
     multiplier kappa(gamma) = (c/a)_3. Kubota's theta_3 is the residue of the cubic metaplectic Eisenstein series; its
     coefficient at lambda^(-3) n b^3 (n squarefree) is 3^(5/2) |b| chi_n(lambda)^2 gamma_2(n), gamma_2 the normalised cubic Gauss sum.
     The stabiliser of omega in PSL_2(Z) (order 3) is O^x/{+-1}: the same roots of unity. That is a coincidence of
     groups, not yet a map: omega is a point of SL_2(Z)\H^2, theta_3 a function on a quotient of H^3.
(2b) PROVED (from the definition of the metaplectic Eisenstein series, itself from memory). Its constant term contains
     sum_c g(0,c) N(c)^(-w), and the Gauss sum at frequency 0 vanishes unless c is a cube: the series is
     zeta_K(3w-3)/zeta_K(3w-2) up to normalisation. So the cubic Eisenstein series sees zeta_K through a constant term at
     parameter 3w, shift 1: further from the right parameter than (1a), not nearer. Its other Fourier coefficients are
     Dirichlet series of cubic Gauss sums, which have no Euler product. ANSWER to the lead's question: I know NO
     Eisenstein series or theta function on the cubic cover with zeta_K(s) or 1/zeta_K(s) as a Fourier coefficient at the
     right parameter.
(2c) BUT a PERIOD does it. PROVED from the quoted coefficient formula (assuming, as the digest's statements suggest, that
     the non-zero coefficients sit at indices (unit)(power of lambda) n b^3, n squarefree; factors at 3 and ray-class
     conditions ignored): |gamma_2(n)| = 1, so sum |c_theta(n b^3)|^2 N(n b^3)^(-s) = 3^5 zeta_K(s) zeta_K(3s-1)/zeta_K(2s).
     By Rankin-Selberg unfolding on H^3 (mechanism KNOWN; Gamma factors and the shift of s not computed by me) this
     Dirichlet series is the integral of |theta_3|^2 against the ordinary Eisenstein series of SL_2(Z[omega]).
     So zeta_K(s), at the RIGHT parameter, is the Eisenstein transform of two POSITIVE measures:
         delta_omega on SL_2(Z)\H^2 (exactly Phi)   and   |theta_3|^2 dvol on the Bianchi orbifold (times zeta_K(3s-1)/zeta_K(2s)).
     This is the cubic analogue of (1e) in which the symmetric square is elementary. Contrast (PROVED): for the Jacobi
     theta function the same integral gives sum over squares = zeta(2s), the WRONG parameter. The cubic theta function
     is special because its coefficients have modulus one on squarefree integers instead of living on powers.
(2d) THE BRIDGE between omega and the Gauss sums is the CM elliptic curve at omega. KNOWN: digest Lemma 4.1,
     gamma_2(n)^3 = mu(n) alpha(n) (checked numerically by READER-4); FROM MEMORY (Gauss, Jacobi, Weil 1952): the cube of the
     cubic Gauss sum is N(pi) times a Jacobi sum, and the Jacobi sum is the Frobenius eigenvalue at pi of the curve
     x^3 + y^3 = 1, which is C/Z[omega]: the elliptic curve whose period point IS omega (j = 0). Hence, PROVED from the lemma:
         sum over squarefree primary n of gamma_2(n)^3 N(n)^(-s) = sum mu(n) alpha(n) N(n)^(-s) = 1/L(s, alpha) (up to factors at 3),
     and L(s,alpha) is the L-function of that curve (weight 2, level 27; from memory), itself a value at omega of a
     weight-raised Eisenstein series by (1d) with level 3. THE SENTENCE the lead asked for (HUNCHES F1 (ii)+(iii)):
     the pointwise CUBE of the coefficients of theta_3 generates the reciprocal of an L-function that is a period of the
     Eisenstein family at omega; the Moebius function of K is that cube untwisted by the Frobenius angle alpha of the CM
     curve at omega. So the two exact formulas at omega are joined by one classical object, and what joins them is a
     statement about LOCAL ROOTS (|Jacobi sum|^2 = p, i.e. |alpha| = 1), not about zeros.
(2e) theta_3^3. PROVED: kappa^3 = 1, so it is an honest function on Gamma\H^3, of growth y^2 at the cusps (not square
     integrable). It is NOT an automorphic form: Delta(theta^3) = 3 theta^2 Delta(theta) + 6 theta (grad theta . grad theta), so theta^3 is an
     eigenfunction only if (grad log theta . grad log theta) is constant; at the cusp it equals 4/9 + (4/3) y d/dy (first non-constant
     Fourier term / constant term) + smaller, and that term is not zero (sketch). So it is not an Eisenstein
     series and there is no Siegel-Weil statement (FROM MEMORY: theta_3 is not a theta lift from a dual pair). Its
     Fourier coefficients are ADDITIVE triple convolutions sum_{a+b+c=m} c(a)c(b)c(c).
(2f) Is the POINTWISE cube reachable by an integral? Two coefficient sequences: yes (Rankin-Selberg: one unipotent
     integration forces equal frequencies). Three: not by any integral I know. Garrett-type triple products compute
     L(s, f x g x h) for Hecke eigenforms; theta_3 is not one in that sense. And by (2d) the pointwise cube is already
     identified: it is 1/L(s,alpha), a reciprocal; reciprocals of L-functions occur in my toolkit only as Fourier
     coefficients of Eisenstein series at parameter 2s (1/zeta_K(2s), 1/L(2s,.)), never as periods at parameter s.
     Hunch 2 of HUNCHES.md E ("the window form as a trilinear period") therefore has no object in my toolkit: STATUS, the
     substitution mu = conj(alpha) gamma_2^3 produces the Dirichlet series 1/L(s, .), not a period. What the quasi-RH proof
     actually uses is LINEAR in theta: after Poisson summation in the row, mu x (sextic Gauss sum) = (unit) alpha gamma_2
     (digest, step 5), and the mean square over rows becomes a mean square of twisted coefficient sums of theta_3,
     i.e. (my reading, HEURISTIC) an L^2 norm of theta_3 over horocycle arcs at the cusps a/c.

## 3. Three walls: what would make each trivial, and whether my toolkit has it
(a) "THE SPECTRAL PARAMETER IS ON THE WRONG LINE."
 - Trivialising object: a self-adjoint operator whose eigenvalues are s(1-s) for the zeros s of Phi (parameter s).
 - It EXISTS IN PRINT, and its limits are known. KNOWN, read (Bombieri-Garrett 2020, abstract and introduction; the idea
   is Colin de Verdiere's, 1982-83, after Hejhal's analysis of Haas's computation): on the Hilbert space Eis^0 spanned
   by pseudo-Eisenstein series, let theta = delta_omega restricted; theta E_s = (sqrt(d_k)/2)^s zeta_k(s)/zeta(2s), and theta lies in the
   Sobolev space Eis^(-3/4-eps), inside Eis^(-1). The Friedrichs extension S~_theta of -Delta on ker(theta) is self-adjoint, and "the
   discrete spectrum lambda_s > 1/4 of S~_theta, if any, is s(1-s) with s a zero of zeta(s) L(s,chi_-3). There is no assurance of
   existence of any discrete spectrum"; assuming pair correlation "at most 94% of the zeros of zeta(s) can occur";
   numerical tests "suggest that there is no discrete spectrum". Eigen-equation: (-Delta - lambda)u = c theta AND theta(u) = 0.
   History read there: Haas's numerical "eigenvalues" at zeros of zeta and L(chi_-3) were solutions of
   (-Delta - lambda)u = delta_omega, i.e. automorphic Green's functions with pole at omega (Hejhal).
 - Why self-adjointness does not catch the zeros. PROVED given their framework (rank-one singular perturbation; the
   family of extensions is from memory: Krein, Aronszajn-Donoghue): u = c (Delta-resolvent applied to theta) is in L^2 at a real
   lambda_w on the continuous spectrum only if the density |theta E_(1/2+it)|^2 vanishes at t = Im w, i.e. Phi(w) = 0; and
   then theta(u) = 0 is a SECOND real equation J(w) = 0, J(w) := |<theta,1>|^2/((lambda_1-lambda_w)<1,1>) + P.V.(1/4 pi) int |theta E_(1/2+it)|^2 dt/(lambda_t - lambda_w).
   A zero is necessary; it is not sufficient. With the other self-adjoint extensions (point interaction of coupling
   tau at omega; boundary condition J = cot tau) every on-line zero gamma is an embedded eigenvalue for exactly ONE coupling,
   tau_gamma = arccot J(gamma), and an off-line zero for none. So the toolkit HAS the operator, with the right parameter; what
   it lacks is a reason for one coupling to serve all zeros.
 - What they do prove (read): with two functionals (theta and the constant term at height a) the determinant
   a_k^(1-2w)(a^(2w-1) + c_w) J(w) - (theta E_w)^2/(2w-1) has zeros only on Re w = 1/2 and [0,1]. A look-alike theorem at the
   right parameter: it contains Phi(w)^2 and the half-shift object c_w in one line.
 - The other object at the right parameter: Zagier's toroidal space (my B214). There the wall becomes "unitarity".
(b) "POSITIVITY IS EXACT, NO SLACK."
 - Trivialising object: a norm, W = T*T with T written without the zeros.
 - What my toolkit has at omega. (i) |Phi(1/2+it)|^2/|xi(1+2it)|^2 is the spectral density of delta_omega in the continuous
   spectrum (pre-trace formula; this is the theta E_s of Bombieri-Garrett). It is >= 0 for free, and it is EMPTY: the same
   density at z = i sqrt5 is |E*(i sqrt5, 1/2+it)|^2/|xi(1+2it)|^2 >= 0 and that Epstein function has zeros off the line.
   A density that is a square on the line says nothing about zeros off it (barrier B2). I disagree with HUNCHES F1's
   last sentence on this point: the positivity of a spectral density is free and carries no information.
   (ii) PROVED (Parseval on the class group, from (1b)): for D fundamental,
        sum_{z in CM(D)} E*(z,s) conj(E*(z,s')) = (c_D(s) conj(c_D(s'))/h) sum_psi L_K(s,psi) conj(L_K(s',psi)),  c_D(s) = (w/2)(|D|/4)^(s/2) pi^(-s) Gamma(s).
   A second moment over the FAMILY of class group L-functions at the right parameter is a norm of the Eisenstein
   series over the Heegner orbit, for every s, with room (it is a mean square). This is the form "mean square over a
   family" (the quasi-RH proof's bet 2) takes in my toolkit, for L; I have nothing of the kind for 1/L.
   (iii) Waldspurger and Lapid-Rallis: |period|^2 or a residue norm = L(1/2): one real point (my B212 (d)).
 - So: for the single function Phi my toolkit has no norm with content; for families it has exact norms with slack.
(c) "THE IMPOSTOR SHARES EVERYTHING."
 - CORRECTION first (PROVED). The project's impostor (tools/controls.py: period 5, coefficients 1, xi, -xi, -1, 0) is
   c L(s,chi) + conj(c) L(s,conj chi), chi complex mod 5: degree ONE. It is not a value of E(z,s). Its degree-two twin is
   the Epstein zeta function of a form of class number > 1 (Davenport-Heilbronn 1936 treat both: from memory).
 - The exact twin (PROVED, checked to 9 digits): discriminant -20, class number 2, z_1 = i sqrt5 (form x^2 + 5y^2),
   z_2 = (-1 + i sqrt5)/2 (form 2x^2 + 2xy + 3y^2), c(s) = (1/2) 5^(s/2) pi^(-s) Gamma(s):
        E*(z_1,s) = c(s) [ zeta(s) L(s,chi_-20) + L(s,chi_-4) L(s,chi_5) ],   E*(z_2,s) = c(s) [ zeta(s) L(s,chi_-20) - L(s,chi_-4) L(s,chi_5) ].
   The SUM and the DIFFERENCE of the two values are Euler products (the two characters of the class group); each single
   value is half the sum of two Euler products with one functional equation: the Davenport-Heilbronn situation, with
   zeros off the line (KNOWN: KNOWLEDGE B2 from memory; Lagarias-Suzuki, read: Epstein zeta functions "generically have
   zeros off the critical line, and often have zeros with Re s > 1"; a real zero whenever y >= 7.0556).
 - WHAT DISTINGUISHES omega (PROVED, lattice count; checked against the classical formula for x^2 + 3y^2 at p = 2):
   for a prime p != 3, T(p) delta_omega = (1 + chi_-3(p)) delta_omega + 3 sum_{z in CM(-3p^2)} delta_z, hence
        3 sum_{z in CM(-3p^2)} E*(z,s) = ( p^s + p^(1-s) - 1 - chi_-3(p) ) Phi(s),
   and the factor has its zeros on Re s = 1/2. In words: the Hecke algebra maps delta_omega into functionals whose
   Eisenstein transforms are all Phi(s) times a Dirichlet polynomial: on the Eisenstein family the Hecke module of
   delta_omega has RANK ONE. At a CM point of discriminant D the rank is the number of class group characters up to complex
   conjugation (from (1b); it is h(D) for h <= 2); at z_1 above it is 2; at a non-CM point no such closure exists. A CM point is exactly a point lying on infinitely many Hecke
   correspondences (z in T_n z for n a norm), and for p split the two CM isogenies are Frobenius and Verschiebung of
   the CM curve mod p (Deuring, from memory): at omega, T_p "contains" Frobenius.
 - THE SET S = { z : s -> E*(z,s) vanishes only on the line (and on [0,1]) }. Expected to contain the 13 points of class
   number one (9 fundamental discriminants and -12, -16, -27, -28: the CM points with rational j), GRH; proved to
   exclude CM points of class number > 1 and points with y >= 7.0556 (above); for a general non-CM point I do not know
   a theorem (Stark 1967, from memory: for large y all zeros below height 2y are on the line except two real ones).
   CONJECTURE (not mine to claim as new; unsearched): S is exactly those 13 points. Then S is the set of rational CM
   points of the modular curve, and "rank one" above is its characterisation by Hecke operators.
 - Trivialising object: one the impostor cannot have. The impostor at z_1 has the functional equation, the spectral
   density, the pseudo-Laplacian of (a) (Bombieri-Garrett's theta for k = Q(sqrt(-5)) uses the SUM over both points) and a
   Poisson map; it does not have rank one. But rank one is the Euler product, and no mechanism is known that turns
   rank one into zeros on the line: that is the Selberg class again (my B211). My toolkit does not contain it.

## 4. The one risk
RISK. A Hecke-equivariant point interaction. Take the operator of 3(a), which has the right parameter but needs a
coupling per zero, and impose the structure that distinguishes omega in 3(c): constrain not at omega alone but on its whole
Hecke orbit (the CM points of all conductors f, i.e. Zagier's condition for the single field K, which he shows is
stable under the Hecke algebra), hoping that Hecke equivariance selects the coupling, so that ONE self-adjoint operator
commuting with all T_p has every zero of Phi as an eigenvalue. Self-adjointness would give Re s = 1/2 and the Hecke
action would be the structural fact that the impostor's points lack.
WHY IT FAILS, exactly (PROVED, with one cited input): the functionals theta_f = sum over CM(-3f^2) of delta_z have Eisenstein
transforms Phi~(t) P_f(t) (3(c)), and the P_f generate, as f runs, all the functions cos(t log q), q a positive rational
(products of 2 cos(t log p) and constants). Since log Q_+ is dense in R, these are complete: a function h in Eis^1
annihilated by every theta_f has (h-hat x conj Phi~) orthogonal to all cos(t log q), hence zero. So the joint kernel of the
Hecke-stable family is {0}: there is no Hilbert space left for the operator to act on. One can have self-adjointness
(one point; Bombieri-Garrett) or Hecke stability (Zagier's space, which is not inside L^2: its members grow like
y^(1-eps)), not both. The reason in one phrase: the Hecke algebra is TWO-SIDED (it contains p^(it) and p^(-it)), so it has
no Hardy-space structure; the one-sidedness that makes Nyman-Beurling non-trivial (dilations by n >= 1 only) and makes
Lax-Phillips work (incoming against outgoing at the cusp) is absent at a point. A point has no direction.
WHAT SURVIVES AS A QUESTION ONE COULD DECIDE. Each on-line zero gamma of Phi carries a number tau_gamma = arccot J(gamma), the
coupling of the point interaction at omega for which it is an eigenvalue. Bombieri-Garrett's 94% says the tau_gamma are not all
equal. (i) What is their distribution? If they are equidistributed mod pi the point omega knows nothing more than
Montgomery's statistics; if they concentrate, there is a structure nobody has named. (ii) J is the Hilbert transform
of |zeta_K(1/2+it)|^2/|zeta(1+2it)|^2: is J(gamma) expressible on the PRIME side (it is a regularised Green's function at
(omega, omega), which has a closed form as a sum over the group: sum over gamma of Q_(s-1)(cosh d(omega, gamma omega)), i.e. over
representations of integers by the norm form)? That would be an exact formula at a zero. I have not derived it.
WHERE ONE-SIDEDNESS IS AVAILABLE for the same field: on a SPLIT torus. For K' real quadratic the period (1c) is an
integral along a closed geodesic, which has a direction and a semigroup (the unit eps^n); and the degenerate split
torus is where the project's windows already live (1c). For Q(sqrt(-3)) the torus is a point. If the quasi-RH family
must be realised as periods with a direction, it will not be at omega but on the Bianchi orbifold of K, where the
split tori of SL_2 over K are again geodesics and 2(c) gives zeta_K(s) from the positive measure |theta_3|^2.
WHAT I WOULD NEED TO KNOW TO DECIDE anything further: (1) Patterson's theorem in full (support and exact values of the
coefficients of theta_3), to make 2(c) exact with its Gamma factors; (2) whether the Rankin-Selberg integral of
|theta_3|^2 against the Eisenstein series of SL_2(Z[omega]) is in print (Patterson; Bump-Hoffstein; Suzuki: all unread);
(3) Bombieri-Garrett sections 4, 5, 7 (the exact eigen-condition, "an important remark", the interleaving theorem);
(4) Davenport-Heilbronn 1936 and Stark 1967 for the status of S at non-CM points.

## 5. Summary in ten lines
1. Phi(s) = 3 (sqrt3/2pi)^s Gamma(s) zeta_K(s): the compact-torus period of the Eisenstein family at parameter s; class number
   one makes the torus a point. Its zeros are those of zeta and L(chi_-3): no slack comes from the field alone.
2. Parameter s: torus periods (CM points, closed geodesics, Hecke characters via weights) and Rankin-Selberg periods.
   Parameter 2s: the unipotent period, hence every constant term and every norm built from it.
3. The cubic cover's Eisenstein series sees zeta_K at parameter 3w with shift 1 (worse). No Fourier coefficient on the
   cubic cover is zeta_K(s) or its reciprocal at the right parameter.
4. But sum |c_theta|^2 N^(-s) = 3^5 zeta_K(s) zeta_K(3s-1)/zeta_K(2s): zeta_K(s) is the Eisenstein transform of the positive measure
   |theta_3|^2 on the Bianchi orbifold, as it is of delta_omega on the modular surface. The Jacobi theta gives zeta(2s) instead.
5. The bridge between omega and the Gauss sums is the CM curve at omega: gamma_2^3 = mu alpha, alpha its Frobenius angle;
   sum gamma_2(n)^3 N(n)^(-s) = 1/L(s,alpha). It is a statement about local roots. theta_3^3 is not an automorphic form; the
   pointwise cube is not a period in my toolkit: Hunch 2 has no object there.
6. Wall (a): the operator with the right parameter exists (Colin de Verdiere, Bombieri-Garrett): zeros are necessary
   for eigenvalues, a second real equation is needed; each on-line zero has its own coupling tau_gamma.
7. Wall (b): the spectral density of delta_omega is positive and empty (the Epstein impostor has it). Norms with content
   exist for families: a second moment over class group characters is a norm over the Heegner orbit, at parameter s.
8. Wall (c): the impostor is degree one; its exact degree-two twin is E*(i sqrt5, s) = c [zeta L(chi_-20) + L(chi_-4)L(chi_5)].
   omega is distinguished by RANK ONE of the Hecke module of delta_omega on the Eisenstein family (rank grows with the class group).
9. The set of points where the family vanishes only on the line: expected to be the 13 rational CM points.
10. Risk taken: a Hecke-equivariant point interaction. It fails for an exact reason: the Hecke algebra is two-sided,
    its multipliers cos(t log q) are complete, the constrained space is {0}. A point has no direction; a geodesic has.

### R4. The pick, the number, and what an off-line zero would do
PICK: the cubic-theta kernel of (R1). Of the candidates it is the one that separates omega's field from i sqrt5 for a
structural reason (R2 (a), (b)), it is quadratic in an automorphic form and non-linear in Phi, and it is now exact.
THE NUMBER. k_theta(v) := multiplicative convolution of a_0 with the theta kernel of Lambda_K^(3)(2s): an explicit positive function on
(0, inf), a lattice sum over Z[omega] whose weights |tau(mu)|^2 = 3^(n+4 or n+5) N(d) are constant on squarefree indices (the
Gauss sums have modulus one). Its Mellin transform is (R1). Define its Jensen defect, as SP-reflection did for points of the
modular surface: Dfc_theta := sum over zeros sigma of (R1) with Re sigma > 1/2 of log|sigma/(1-sigma)| >= 0.
WHAT AN OFF-LINE ZERO rho = beta + i gamma OF zeta_K WOULD DO (PROVED): put two zeros of the Mellin transform of the positive kernel
k_theta off the line, at rho and (rho+1)/3 (and their reflections), so Dfc_theta >= log|rho/(1-rho)| + log|(rho+1)/(2-rho)| > 0;
conversely Dfc_theta = 0 iff RH_K. The second zero is three times closer to the line but also three times lower; since
log|sigma/(1-sigma)| ~ (Re sigma - 1/2)/|sigma|^2 for |Im sigma| large, its contribution is about THREE times that of rho:
Dfc_theta ~ 4 Dfc(omega) when the off-line zeros are high (PROVED to first order; an amplification, not a new constraint).
WHY IT PROBABLY FAILS (exactly): (i) R3 (d): it sees nothing beyond Phi; (ii) positivity of the kernel is free and shared:
E*(i sqrt5, s) is also the Mellin transform of a positive kernel (the theta series of x^2 + 5y^2 minus 1), with zeros off the line;
so "Mellin transform of a positive kernel" is empty, as the spectral density was. What k_theta has and the impostor's kernel
lacks is that its weights are a MULTIPLICATIVE function (constant moduli on squarefree indices). So the question it reduces
to is the oldest one in this project's list: does positivity of a kernel plus multiplicativity of its weights force its Mellin
transform's zeros onto the line? That is barrier B1 inside the Selberg class (B211). Known consequence of positivity plus
multiplicativity: only Landau's lemma, at the abscissa Re s = 1.

of its Fourier transform: Polya 1926, de Bruijn 1950 "universal factors", FROM MEMORY) that k_theta satisfies and the Epstein
kernel of i sqrt5 violates. Since the impostor's transform has zeros off the line, every such criterion must fail for the
impostor's kernel; a criterion that k_theta passes would be a real separation. Concrete first test (computable, not run):
