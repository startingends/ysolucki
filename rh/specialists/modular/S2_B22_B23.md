## B22. The quadratic stratum u = w^3
(1) WHICH GAUSS SUMS APPEAR (PROVED from the paper's Step 3, run on one stratum). On u = w^3, (w^3/n)_6 = (w/n)_2, so
    A_(w^3)(D) = sum_n mu(n) nu(n) (w/n)_2 W(N(n)/D): Moebius twisted by QUADRATIC characters of K. Expanding sum_w Phi(N(w)/H')|A_(w^3)|^2
    (H' = H^(1/3)) and applying Poisson over w in O gives, at the non-zero frequencies, the complete sums sum_(x mod z_1 z_2) (x/z_1 z_2)_2 e(hx/z_1z_2),
    i.e. QUADRATIC Gauss sums g_2(z) of modulus z = z_1 z_2 (squarefree, prime to 6).
(2) THEY DO NOT ABSORB mu. FROM MEMORY (Gauss; over number fields Hecke): the normalised quadratic Gauss sum gamma^(2)(z) =
    N(z)^(-1/2) sum_x (x/z)_2 e(x/z) satisfies gamma^(2)(z)^2 = (-1/z)_2, and gamma^(2) itself is a ray-class character of z (fixed modulus,
    dividing a power of 2 and the infinite data) up to the quadratic reciprocity cocycle. So mu(z) gamma^(2)(z) is mu(z) times a
    character: mu stays in place. The dual sums are again sum_n mu(n) psi(n) (h/n)_2 W, psi a fixed ray-class character: PROVED from (1)+(2),
    the quadratic stratum is CLOSED under Poisson, with (family H', length D) -> (family D^2/H', length D) and the coefficients unchanged.
    A pure duality in the sense of SHORT_FAMILY 2(d).
(3) THE EXACT OBSTRUCTION. Absorption needs, at every prime, (normalised Gauss sum)^m = -(local unit / character), so that the
    product over the prime factors of squarefree n produces (-1)^(omega(n)) = mu(n). For m = 3: g(chi)^3 = p J(chi,chi) with the primary
    Jacobi sum J = -pi-bar (J = -1 mod 3), whence gamma_2^3 = -alpha: "the -1 is mu" (digest, Lemma 4.1, checked numerically by
    READER-4). For m = 2 the Jacobi-sum route degenerates (chi^2 trivial: g(chi) g(chi-bar) = chi(-1) p, no Jacobi sum), and the sign is
    chi(-1) = (-1/p)_2, a CHARACTER value, not the constant -1. So there is no -1 to carry mu. In one line: the cube of a cubic Gauss
    sum is a Hecke character times -1; the square of a quadratic Gauss sum is a Hecke character times +1.
(4) A SECOND, STRUCTURAL FORM OF THE SAME OBSTRUCTION (PROVED from the coefficient formulas). The automorphic object behind Gauss
    sums of order m is the theta function of the m-fold cover. For m = 3 its coefficients (Patterson, read in Dunn-Radziwill (5.7))
    are cubic Gauss sums spread over squarefree-times-cube indices; for m = 2 the theta function of O_K is sum_v e(v^2 z): its coefficients
    are the indicator of SQUARES. The rows w = v^2 of the quadratic stratum are exactly the copies (u = v^6). So on the quadratic
    stratum the would-be absorbing automorphic object is supported on the copies themselves: absorption would land on the
    trivial characters, which is circular (the large sieve is blind there: including the square rows, the bound is
    (H' + D H'^(1/2)) sum|a_n|^2, whose second term is the copies' trivial contribution).
(5) WHAT THE STRATUM WOULD GIVE IF IT DID ABSORB (PROVED, N49's formula). Inside the stratum the copies are the squares w = v^2:
    density delta = 1/2 relative to the stratum. A diagonal mean square sum_(N(w)<=H') |A_(w^3)(D)|^2 << H' D^(1+eps) at H' = D (the
    stratum's self-dual point) would give |A_1|^2 << H' D / H'^(1/2) = D^(3/2): A_1 << D^(3/4), zero-free Re s > 3/4. Exactly N49's cap
    (delta = 1/2, exponent 3/4), and exactly where SP-sieve and FI place the parity barrier. The quadratic Goldmakher-Louvel
    sieve does give the diagonal at H' = D, but only over SQUAREFREE rows, i.e. without the copies; with them it is the bound of (4).
(6) SHIMURA-WALDSPURGER over K. KNOWN from memory (Waldspurger 1981; Kohnen-Zagier over Q): coefficients of half-integral weight
    forms on the double cover are, up to local factors, square roots of central values L(1/2, pi x chi_w) of QUADRATIC TWISTS. That is
    an exact formula for a family of quadratic twists, at the CENTRE, for L, not for 1/L. I know no exact formula for
    sum_w |A_(w^3)(D)|^2 (Moebius sums, any point) at H^(1/3) < D, and I would not trust one built from Waldspurger: it carries
    |L(1/2)|^2, which is the positive, impostor-shared kind of information (OBJECT_PHI R3), and nothing about 1/L.

## B23. The impostor's rightmost real part: 0.808 is not it
(1) DERIVATION (PROVED except the Kronecker step, which is classical). f = c L(s,chi) + conj(c) L(s, conj chi), c = (1 - i kappa)/2, chi(2) = i.
    For Re s > 1, L(chi)/L(conj chi) = exp(2i F(s)), F(s) = sum_(p = 2 mod 5) arctan(p^-s) - sum_(p = 3 mod 5) arctan(p^-s) (Euler products;
    log((1+ix)/(1-ix)) = 2i arctan x; primes = 1, 4 mod 5 cancel). So f(s) = 0 iff F(s) = pi/2 + arctan(kappa) + k pi, a REAL value; the
    nearest is -(pi/2 - arctan kappa) = -1.2940. On Re s = sigma the closure of the values of F is (Kronecker: log p independent) the
    Minkowski sum of the curves arctan(p^-sigma T); since arctan(2^-sigma) < the sum of the others it is a full region whose real
    section is [-M(sigma), M(sigma)], M(sigma) = sum_(p = 2,3 mod 5) arctan(p^-sigma). Interior values are attained (Rouche + Kronecker; Bohr's
    method, as in Davenport-Heilbronn 1936, FROM MEMORY). Hence zeros exist exactly for 1 < sigma < sigma_1 with M(sigma_1) = 1.2940.
(2) COMPUTED (dh_sigma1.log; primes <= 10^7 summed exactly, PNT tail 0.027; one implementation): M(1.01) = 2.503, M(1.05) = 1.712,
    M(1.10) = 1.381, and sigma_1 = 1.1204 (third decimal affected by the tail estimate). Prediction [1.01, 1.06] MISSED.
    So the supremum of real parts of zeros of f_DH is about 1.12, not 0.808; 0.808517 + 85.699i is the LOWEST off-line zero in the
    strip (Spira), the one the record has been using.
(3) CONSEQUENCES, as SP-sieve says. 1/f has poles with real part up to 1.12; its Dirichlet coefficients c_n are unbounded (a bounded
    sequence would give absolute convergence on Re s > 1); A_1^f(D) >= D^(sigma_1 - eps) along a sequence, S^f >= D^(2.24), which exceeds
    H D for all H < D^(1.24), i.e. also at the paper's H = D^(1+theta) (theta <= 1/10). CORRECTION to my SHORT_FAMILY 4(a): "invisible
    for H >= D" is wrong for this reason. The impostor is excluded already by the NORMALISATION |mu nu| <= 1 (equivalently: sum over
    n ~ D of |c_n|^2 is at least D^(2 sigma_1 - 1 - eps) = D^(1.24), not of order D): a coefficient-size (Ramanujan-type) failure of the
