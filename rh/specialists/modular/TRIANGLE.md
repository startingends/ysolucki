## 2. Question 1: a no-go theorem, and where it breaks
 THEOREM 1 (PROVED from two FROM-MEMORY facts: Jacquet-Shalika's absolute convergence and classification of isobaric representations; the
 Rankin-Selberg integral). Let Pi be automorphic on GL_n, isobaric, with a Hecke-character constituent |.|^(z) chi, Re z = beta != 0.
 Then L(s, Sym^k Pi x Sym^k Pi~) has a pole at s = 1 + 2k beta. Langlands' a-priori input ("rightmost pole at s = 1", which for an isobaric
 sum of UNITARY cuspidal constituents is Jacquet-Shalika) holds for Pi iff beta = 0. So for any Pi whose link to zeta is a Hecke-character
 constituent, B_k is not a consequence of anything automorphic: it is the statement beta = 0.
 In my toolkit (KNOWN shape, Zagier 1981, read in session 1): the fixed half-width of Langlands' argument is the decay of a cusp form at the
 cusp. Rankin-Selberg <|phi|^2, E(., s)> converges for Re s > 1 because phi is rapidly decreasing; for phi = E(., 1/2 + beta) Zagier's
 renormalisation is forced, and the renormalised integral has extra poles at s = 1 +- 2 beta from the growth y^(1/2 + beta). The moving pole
 IS the non-L^2 growth. So: B_k uniform <=> "Sym^k Pi decays at the cusp" <=> cuspidal constituents only.
 PROPOSITION 2 (C at the primes forces a character constituent; FROM MEMORY, strong multiplicity one of Jacquet-Shalika, HEURISTIC in the
 generality "Satake parameters contain p^(1/2 - rho) for almost all p"): if the zero is read off the Satake parameters at almost all
 primes, |.|^(rho - 1/2) is an isobaric constituent. With Theorem 1: (A) + (C at the primes) + (B_k for all k) holds iff RH; nothing in A
 supplies B_k.
 WHERE THE NO-GO BREAKS (the gap). C read at the ARCHIMEDEAN place only: a cuspidal pi on GL_2 with pi_infinity = |det|^(i gamma) (x)
 complementary series of exponent beta is a twisted Maass cusp form with exceptional eigenvalue 1/4 - beta^2. It is automorphic, L^2,
 unitary, and Sym^k functoriality (if known for it) would give beta = 0. KNOWN bound (FROM MEMORY, Kim-Sarnak): beta <= 7/64 for GL_2 over Q,
 so an off-line zero with beta > 7/64 can NOT be an archimedean parameter of any GL_2/Q cusp form (a true no-go, for that corner). For
 beta <= 7/64 it would contradict Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture. But no construction makes the zero CONTROL a cusp form's archimedean
 parameter (C is then a coincidence, not a structure), and Hecke eigenvalues and archimedean parameters of cusp forms equidistribute
 independently (FROM MEMORY: Sarnak; Shin), so nothing at the primes reaches it. A no-go for this corner would be "zeros of zeta are not
 archimedean parameters of cusp forms": not a theorem, and no reason known.

## 4. What object would make the wall trivial (moved since session 1)
Session 1: "a Hilbert space in which a zero is a spectral parameter, Hecke operators act, Sym^k stays in the family". Now: such a space
exists in the weak sense, and it is classical: P_rho, the complementary-series Eisenstein series E(., 1/2 + beta) twisted by |det|^(i gamma),
is automorphic, unitary, and its central L-value vanishes exactly at the zero; off-line quartets are complementary series, and zero-free
regions are bounds toward Ramanujan for it (Re s < 1 is the trivial bound, Re s <= 1/2 + 1/(2k) is unitarity of Sym^k). What would make the
wall trivial is a reason for Sym^k P_rho to be unitary for every k. In Langlands' setting that reason is cuspidality: decay at the cusp
fixes the Rankin-Selberg pole at 1 (Theorem 1, Zagier's renormalisation shows the pole moving with the growth). So the target object is a
CUSPIDAL avatar of P_rho: a cusp form (on some group, any cover) whose archimedean parameter is controlled by the zero, i.e. an exceptional
eigenvalue built from rho. Kim-Sarnak already forbids it on GL_2/Q for beta > 7/64. What moved: the obstruction is no longer "unitarity is
missing" (it is free at k = 1) but "decay at the cusp is missing", which is a property my toolkit can state exactly.
