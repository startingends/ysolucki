Normalisation: for a zero rho = 1/2 + beta + i gamma (gamma != 0), put Pi_rho := Ind(|.|^(rho-1/2), |.|^(1/2-rho)), the representation of

## 1. The lead's table, line by line
 - Base change, shift, dilation, C^k (Kuenneth), Deligne F^(tensor 2k) on a curve, Langlands' Sym^k: correct as written (the C^k line is
   the important one: deviation and half-width both multiply by k).
 - GL_n constant terms and covers: correct, and the reason is uniform. FROM MEMORY (Langlands; Shahidi): every constant term of a cuspidal
   Eisenstein series is a product of ratios L(z, pi, r_i)/L(1 + z, pi, r_i) (with z replaced by nz on an n-fold cover). A zero of the
   denominator sits at Re z = Re rho - 1 = beta - 1/2 < 0: always in the non-spectral chamber, so never a residue in L^2. Moeglin-Waldspurger
   (FROM MEMORY): the residual spectrum of GL_n comes only from the pole of zeta at 1 (Speh representations). Kazhdan-Patterson theta
   representations: residues at poles of zeta(n z), parameters fixed rational exponents. No exception on any group I know.
 - Theta / Shimura-Shintani: the Shimura lift of half-integral-weight Eisenstein series doubles the parameter (s -> 2s): the dilation line.
   Rankin-Selberg with E(., w): L(s + w - 1/2) L(s - w + 1/2): the shift line. Rallis inner product / Waldspurger: ||theta(phi)||^2 or
   |toric period|^2 = (L-value at ONE point, the centre) x local factors: zeros become VANISHING of a global lift, at s = 1/2 only.
   None changes r.
 - ONE LINE IS WRONG AS A STATEMENT, AND THE COUNTEREXAMPLE IS EMPTY (PROVED, from standard facts). "No operation on global zeros
   amplifies" fails for the composite  rho -> Pi_rho -> Sym^k Pi_rho.  Sym^k Pi_rho is the isobaric sum of the characters
   |.|^((k - 2j)(rho - 1/2)), j = 0..k: automorphic on GL_(k+1) (a Borel Eisenstein series; FROM MEMORY, Langlands), with exponent k beta at
   every place, while the a-priori bound for UNITARY generic representations of GL_(k+1) stays 1/2 (Jacquet-Shalika, FROM MEMORY). So r is
   multiplied by k with fixed half-width: formally an amplifier acting on global zeros (via induction they become local parameters).
   Why it is empty: Langlands' converter needs the a-priori bound (unitarity or cuspidality) for EVERY k. Here it holds at k = 1 only if
   Pi_(rho,infinity) is unitary, and for the principal series of GL_2(R) with Im parameter gamma != 0, unitary <=> tempered <=> beta = 0
   (the complementary series needs a REAL parameter; FROM MEMORY, unitary dual of GL_2(R)). So the input of the amplifier is already the
   conclusion. The amplifier is free; the bound is the scarce resource. Second defect: "rho is a zero" is a GLOBAL condition on Pi_rho
   (its K-toric period, Hecke's formula, E*(omega, rho) = 0 for K = Q(sqrt-3), OBJECT_PHI) and Sym^k does not carry it: the toric periods
   of Sym^k Pi_rho involve zeta_K(1/2 + (k - 2j)(rho - 1/2)), and only the terms with k - 2j = +-1 see rho. The amplified exponent k beta is
   attached to points that are not zeros (HEURISTIC for k >= 2: I did not compute the GL_(k+1) periods).
 Corrected thesis (my wording): amplification of local parameters is available for anything Eisenstein-induced; what no construction
 supplies is a parameter that is BOTH controlled by a zero of zeta AND constrained a priori by unitarity (an L^2 / Hilbert-space bound
 with fixed width), uniformly under Sym^k.

 Pattern (PROVED as a reading of the table): each candidate has at most two of the three properties {automorphic with Satake data at
 primes, unitarity at the parameter, the parameter is controlled by a zero}. Eisenstein induction: 1 and 3. Pseudo-Laplacian: 2 and 3

## 3. What object would make the wall trivial (own words)
A unitary automorphic family over a FIXED base in which the zeros of zeta are local parameters: representations Pi_gamma, in L^2 (or
unitary), on some group or cover, with Hecke-Satake data at the primes, whose archimedean (or p-adic) exponent is beta for the zero
1/2 + beta + i gamma, and with Sym^k Pi_gamma (or tensor powers) again unitary in the same family. Then the unitarity bound 1/2 is the fixed
half-width, Sym^k multiplies beta by k, and k beta < 1/2 for all k forces beta = 0, exactly as Langlands gets Ramanujan from functoriality.
The point the construction adds: the amplifier itself is cheap (Eisenstein induction already makes zeros local and Sym^k-stable); the
missing ingredient is unitarity at a parameter that a zero controls, and inside automorphic forms the +1 shift of every constant term
puts zeros exactly where unitarity is not available. So the target is not "an operation on zeros" but "a Hilbert space in which a zero
of zeta is a spectral parameter and Hecke operators still act" -- Hilbert-Polya with the Hecke algebra required, which the Hecke-
equivariance obstruction (OBJECT_PHI sec. 10) shows cannot be a point interaction on GL_2. Item 2 did not get closer to such an object;
it located the obstruction (two out of three, with the reason for each pair) more sharply than "global vs local".
