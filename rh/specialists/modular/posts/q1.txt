(1) F_d IS THE L-FUNCTION OF E(., 1/2+d): CONFIRMED. KNOWN, read (Zagier 1981, eqs. (14), (18)): T(n)E(z,s) = n^s sigma_(1-2s)(n) E(z,s), Delta E = s(s-1)E. With d = s - 1/2: normalised eigenvalues sum_{ab=n}(a/b)^d, L-function zeta(w-d)zeta(w+d), eigenvalue 1/4 - d^2. PROVED in addition (Hecke's integral): xi(S+d)xi(S-d) = 2 int_0^inf [E*(iy,1/2+d) - constant term] y^(S-1/2) dy/y, Re S > 1+|d|: the completed F_d is the Mellin transform of that Eisenstein series along the geodesic iy; u = log y is the window variable, u -> -u is z -> -1/z, the tilt d is the spectral parameter. d in iR: tempered; 0<d<1/2: non-tempered at every place; d = 1/2: the constant function.
(2) 'THE REMEDY IS QUADRATIC, Rankin-Selberg + Landau, pole at 1': CONFIRMED. KNOWN, read (Milne arXiv 1509.00797, sec. on Landau, Rankin; Blomer-Brumley arXiv 1003.0559, intro): Landau: a Dirichlet series with coefficients >= 0 is singular at the real point sigma_0 of its abscissa of absolute convergence; for an Euler product, |local roots| <= p^sigma_0. Rankin 1939: sum |c(n)|^2 n^-s is singular only at s = k, k-1, hence c(n) = O(n^(k-1/5)). Langlands 1970: if L(s,sigma,pi) is analytic in Re s > 1 for ALL sigma, then sigma = rho (x) rho-bar and its powers give |root|^(2m) <= p for all m: |root| = 1. Jacquet-Shalika: exponent 1/2 on GL_n; Kim-Sarnak 7/64 on GL_2 (statements read, proofs not).
HYPOTHESES, exactly: (H1) Euler product; then the square has coefficients >= 0 AUTOMATICALLY (not an extra hypothesis). (H2) the square is holomorphic on the real axis right of 1; for a cusp form f because int |f|^2 E(z,s) converges wherever E(z,s) is regular: this is where SQUARE-INTEGRABILITY of f is used. (H3) Landau.
(3) WHAT F_d, G_d VIOLATE: (H2), and only (H2). PROVED: F_d x F_d = zeta(s)^2 zeta(s-2d) zeta(s+2d) x (regular, non-zero on Re s >= 1); G_d x G_d contains zeta(s-2d); sum_{n<=x}|a_n|^2 ~ x^(1+2d). Reason: E(., 1/2+d) grows like y^(1/2+d), not in L^2. Both controls SATISFY the conclusion of the one-step bound (p^d < p^(1/2)); they are excluded by the hypothesis, or by all tensor powers (prime-side form: S1-mirror B178). In Selberg's axioms (read in Conrey-Ghosh math/9204217): F_d violates analyticity (poles at 1+-d), Ramanujan a_n << n^eps, and Re mu >= 0 (Gamma((s-d)/2)); G_d violates Ramanujan ONLY (one pole at 1; mu = (1-+d)/2 > 0; b_n << n^theta, theta = d < 1/2): the Ramanujan axiom is independent of the other three.
(4) POWERLESS AT ZETA: CONFIRMED. The output is a statement about the region of absolute convergence, read off a pole on Re s = 1; for the trivial character the square is zeta itself. On the zero side a norm gives exactly the edge: zeta(1+it) != 0 (Zagier sec. 1, read: else E(z, 1/2+it/2) is a non-zero function of rapid decay with constant term 0, hence norm 0).
(5) A CONTROL WITH Euler product + functional equation + one simple pole + Ramanujan + non-negative Rankin-Selberg coefficients THAT VIOLATES RH? NONE ON THE BOARD: Davenport-Heilbronn, dial 1<n<2, pencil, zero surgery: no Euler product; F_d, G_d: no Ramanujan; Beurling, one-prime, missing-prime: no functional equation; Liouville twist zeta(2s)/zeta(s): infinitely many poles. NONE KNOWN ANYWHERE: with Gamma shifts Re mu >= 0 those hypotheses are the Selberg class, and 'Selberg also conjectures that the Riemann hypothesis holds for this class' (Conrey-Ghosh, read). Exact status: no counterexample, no structural reason proved; GRH is open there. The fifth hypothesis is empty (automatic); its non-empty form is analytic: sum |a_n|^2 n^-s has a simple pole at 1 (Selberg's conjecture sum_{p<=x}|a_p|^2/p = n_F loglog x + O(1)): a theorem for automorphic forms, a conjecture in the class.
