## (a) Definitions and coefficients
(a1) Cusp member f: lambda_f as above. Eisenstein member E(., 1/2+it): lambda_t(n) = sum_{ab=n} (a/b)^(it) (Zagier (14), (18), read:
     the n-th coefficient of E*(z,s) is 2 sqrt(y) n^(s-1/2) sigma_(1-2s)(n) K_(s-1/2)(2 pi n y), divided by xi*(2s) for E itself); its weight in
     Kuznetsov / the spectral large sieve is (1/4pi) |rho_t(1)|^2 dt with |rho_t(1)|^2 proportional to 1/|zeta(1+2it)|^2 cosh(pi t)-type
     factors (FROM MEMORY). Residual member: the constant 1, Hecke eigenvalue sigma_1(n)/sqrt n = lambda_(-i/2)(n) (non-tempered,
     |lambda(p)| = p^(1/2) + p^(-1/2)); it has no non-constant Fourier coefficient and does not appear in Petersson / Kuznetsov.
(a2) EXACT COPY (COPY_BARRIER section 0): a member with lambda(n) = 1_{(n,r)=1} for n <= D.
     PROVED: no Hecke eigen-system (cusp, Eisenstein or residual) is a copy, for any r and D >= p^2 with p not dividing r:
     lambda(p)^2 = lambda(p^2) + 1 is incompatible with lambda(p) = lambda(p^2) = 1. Exact copies do not exist in GL(2) Hecke families.
(a3) The right generalisation is by L-function: sum_n mu(n) lambda(n) n^(-s) = G(s)/L(s, pi), G absolutely convergent in Re s > 1/2 (Euler
     factor (1 - lambda x)/(1 - lambda x + x^2), x = p^(-s); PROVED). So A_f carries the zeros of L(s,f); A_t carries the zeros of
     zeta(s+it)zeta(s-it): every Eisenstein member "sees" the zeros of zeta (at heights shifted by +-t); only the Eisenstein members do.

## (b) Positive proportion?
(b1) The Eisenstein members ARE the t-family, inside GL(2). In a spectral family of size H ~ T^2 (Weyl law, cusp forms with t_j <= T)
     the continuous part has total weight ~ T (log T)^c: a fraction about H^(-1/2) (FROM MEMORY: Weyl law with Selberg's
     remainder; the Eisenstein contribution to Kuznetsov's diagonal is of lower order). As a COUNT that is delta = 1/2, the same
     exponent as Theorem 2, by a different mechanism (dimension of the continuous spectrum against the discrete one).
(b2) And the copy DIRECTION has zero weight. The members closest to a single sum are |t| << 1/log D (then lambda_t(n) ~ d(n), A_t ~ sum
     mu(n) d(n) W, the 1/zeta^2 sum). Their weight is int_{|t| < 1/log D} dt/|zeta(1+2it)|^2 ~ int t^2 dt ~ (log D)^(-3) (PROVED from
     zeta(1+2it) ~ 1/(2it)). Structural reason (PROVED, Zagier (14)): E(z,1/2) = 0 identically (phi(1/2) = -1), so the Plancherel density
     of the Eisenstein spectrum vanishes at the centre t = 0. In GL(2) the copy direction is not just sparse: it is a zero of the
     spectral measure.
(b3) THE ONE WAY A COPY ENTERS WITH FULL WEIGHT: Rankin-Selberg. Petersson (FROM MEMORY): sum_f omega_f lambda_f(m) lambda_f(n) = delta_(m,n) +
     (Kloosterman terms). So the family AVERAGE of |lambda_f(n)|^2 is 1, the trivial character, with full weight. PROVED (Hecke relations,
     squarefree n): lambda_f(n)^2 = sum_{d|n} lambda_f(d^2), hence
        A_f^RS(D) := sum_n mu(n) lambda_f(n)^2 W(n/D) = sum_d mu(d) lambda_f(d^2) sum_{(m,d)=1} mu(m) W(dm/D),
     whose d = 1 term is A_1(D) ITSELF, in every member. Equivalently sum mu(n) lambda_f(n)^2 n^(-s) = G(s)/(zeta(s) L(s, sym^2 f)): zeta is a
     factor of every member's L-function. Copies of density ONE.
(b4) But then (PROVED): (i) first moment: A_1 = sum_f omega_f A_f^RS - (off-diagonal) is N71's tautology (each member contains A_1;
     bounding the average needs zero-free regions for zeta L(sym^2 f) on average, which contain zeta's). (ii) second moment: a diagonal
     mu-specific bound sum_f omega_f |A_f^RS|^2 << H D^(1+eps) with every member containing A_1 is RH-strength by itself (E3's circularity:
     density-one copies). (iii) a general-coefficient bound pays as in Theorem 1: with a_n = 1, Cauchy-Schwarz and the Petersson average
     sum_f omega_f |sum_{n<=D} lambda_f(n)^2|^2 >= (sum_f omega_f)^(-1) (sum_f omega_f sum_n lambda_f(n)^2)^2 ~ H D^2, so Delta >= H D: like H exact copies.
     So the answer to (b) is: yes, in the Rankin-Selberg family, and it is circular; no, in the Hecke family itself.

## (c) First line where E5 meets the barrier or the wall
- Hecke family {lambda_f}: the amplifier never starts (no copies, (a2)). The general spectral large sieve (Deshouillers-Iwaniec,
  FROM MEMORY: (T^2 + D) sum|a_n|^2) holds with NO Theorem 1 penalty precisely because no member is a copy; it bounds mu-sums whose
  L-functions are L(s,f), not zeta. The only members carrying zeta are the Eisenstein ones, i.e. the t-family, with weight fraction
  H^(-1/2) and zero weight at the copy direction (b1-b2). Through them E5 is the t-family, and the t-family with sums of length D >> T is
  the short-family / Nyman-Beurling wall of S2 (N53, N65). FIRST LINE: (a3)+(b1): "the members whose mu-sums detect zeta are archimedean
  characters".
- Rankin-Selberg family {lambda_f^2}: copies of density one, (b3); the first line is (b4): first moment = N71 tautology, second
  moment = E3 circularity, general sieve = Theorem 1. FIRST LINE: "every member's L-function contains zeta as a factor".
- N49's formula with delta: Hecke family delta undefined (no copies); Eisenstein count delta = 1/2 -> 3/4 at best, but they are not
  copies of one sum, so even 3/4 is not available; Rankin-Selberg delta = 1 -> 1/2, circular.
RISK (one I would take): a family in which the copy enters with full weight but NOT as a factor of every member's L-function. In GL(2)
the copy can only arise as an average of |lambda_f|^2 (Petersson's delta) and then ζ divides L(f x f-bar). A family whose average is the
trivial character without each member containing it would need an orthogonality relation for a family of forms whose Rankin-Selberg
squares are NOT of the form zeta x (something): impossible for GL(n) cuspidal families, since L(pi x pi-bar) always has zeta as a factor
(its pole at s = 1 IS the factor; FROM MEMORY, Jacquet-Shalika). PROVED in the form: any family whose averaged |coefficients|^2 is the
constant and whose members are Hecke eigen-systems has zeta | L(pi x pi-bar) for each member. So E5 for automorphic families closes at
the same pole that N63 found behind every positive-weight method: Landau's pole at s = 1.
