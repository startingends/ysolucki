## 1. What was read
**All 20 pages (pp. 111-130): sections 1-4 and the references, every page**, as images. Displays were legible; no
page was skipped.

Notation: g(c) = sum_{d mod c} (d/c)_3 e(d/c), e(z) = exp(2 pi i (z + z bar)), c = 1 mod 3; g(c)^3 = mu(c) c^2 c bar;
g~(c) = g(c) N(c)^{-1/2}, |g~(c)| = 1 on squarefree c, 0 otherwise; g~_l(c) = g~(c)(c bar/|c|)^l;
Lambda = von Mangoldt on Z[omega]. Generalized Gauss sum g(r,c) = sum_{d mod c} (d/c)_3 e(rd/c).

## 2. Main statements, with their numbers (all unconditional)
- **Theorem 1.** For every eps > 0, l in Z: `sum_{N(c) <= X, c = 1(3)} g~(c) Lambda(c) (c bar/|c|)^l << X^{30/31+eps} + |l| X^{29/31+eps}`.
- **Theorem 2.** The arguments of g~(pi), pi prime, are uniformly distributed: there is A > 0 with
  `#{N(pi) <= X : theta_1 < arg g~(pi) <= theta_2} = ((theta_2 - theta_1)/2pi) Li(X) + O(X exp(-A (log X)^{1/2}))`.
- **Conjecture** (Patterson, [16] = Crelle 303 (1978)), restated p. 113: for l = 0,
  `sum g~(c) Lambda(c) = b X^{5/6} + O(X^{1/2+eps})`, b = 2(2 pi)^{2/3}/(5 Gamma(2/3)); for l != 0 the sum is
  O(X^{1/2+eps}). "It expresses a bias of the g~(pi) towards being positive and real."
- **Theorem 3** (Vaughan's identity reduction). For u <= X^{1/2}:
  `H_l(2X) - H_l(X) << X^eps ( X u^{-1/9} + sum_{N(alpha) <= u^2, alpha = 1(3)} max_{X <= z <= 2X} |F_l(z, alpha)| )`,
  where H_l(X) = sum_{N(c) <= X} g~_l(c) Lambda(c) and **F_l(X, alpha) = sum_{N(c) <= X, c = 0 mod alpha} g~_l(c)**.
- **Theorem 4 (the input from metaplectic forms).** If N(alpha) <= X^{2/3}:
  `F_l(X, alpha) << delta_l X^{5/6} N(alpha)^{-1} + N(alpha)^{-5/8} X^{3/4+eps} + |l| N(alpha)^{-1/4} X^{1/2+eps}`
  (delta_l = 1 if l = 0, else 0). More precisely (p. 129) `F_l(X,alpha) = delta_l p(alpha,1) (6/5) X^{5/6} + O(...)`.
- **Proposition 1** (pieces of Vaughan's identity), **Proposition 2**: Sigma_3(X,u) << X^eps (X^{7/8} + X u^{-1/9})
  (the Type II sum).
- **Lemma 1** (bilinear form with cubic symbols, Vinogradov-Jutila method): for |X(a)|, |Y(b)| <= 1 on squarefree
  a in C(I), b in C(J):
  `sum X(a) Y(b) (a/b)_3 (b/a)_3 << (t(I) t(J))^eps ( n(I)^{1/2} t(I)^{1/2} n(J)^{7/8} + n(I)^{1/2} n(J) t(J)^{1/4} )`.
- **Lemma 2.** theta(w, chi) = sum chi(a) e^{-2 pi N(a) w} << E(chi) w^{-1} + N(f)^{(1+eps)/2} for w <= 1 (Hecke L-series
  functional equation).
- **Lemma 3** (formal, "a reflection of the Hecke theory of the metaplectic group applied to Eisenstein series"):
  expresses psi_alpha(r,s,l) = sum_{c = 0 (alpha)} g(r,c) N(c)^{-s} (c bar/|c|)^l through psi(r,s,l) =
  sum_c g(r,c) N(c)^{-s} (c bar/|c|)^l, e.g. (iii) psi_alpha(r,Omega) Delta_alpha(Omega) =
  sum_{d | alpha} mu(d) N(d^2) Omega(d^2) Omega(alpha) g(r, alpha/d) psi(r alpha/d, Omega).
- **Lemma 4** (from Patterson [15] = "A cubic analogue of the theta series" I, II, Crelle 296 (1977), Thm 6.1 and
  9.1). psi(r,s,l) continues meromorphically to C; entire in Re s > 1 if l != 0; for l = 0 at most a simple pole at
  s = 4/3, residue (r = 1 mod 3, squarefree) **p_0(r) = c_0 conj(g(1,r)) N(r)^{-2/3}**,
  c_0 = (2 pi)^{5/3} (3^{3/2} 8 Gamma(2/3) zeta_{Q(omega)}(2))^{-1}; for alpha squarefree the residue of
  psi_alpha(r,s,0) is p(alpha,r) = c_0 N(alpha)^{-1} conj(g(1,r)) N(r)^{-2/3} prod_{pi | alpha}(1 + N(pi)^{-1})^{-1};
  and with sigma_1 = 3/2 + eps, |s - 4/3| > 1/12, sigma_1 - 1/2 <= sigma <= sigma_1:
  psi_alpha(r,s,l) << N(alpha)^{(3/2)(sigma_1 - sigma) - 1} N(r)^{(1/2)(sigma_1 - sigma)} (1 + l^2 + t^2)^{sigma_1 - sigma}.
  Proof: F(r,s,l) = G_l(s) psi(r,s,l) zeta(3s-2,l), G_l(s) = (2pi)^{-2s} Gamma(s + |l|/2 - 1/3) Gamma(s + |l|/2 - 2/3),
  is entire (l != 0) or has at most simple poles at 4/3, 2/3 (l = 0), with a functional equation s <-> 2 - s,
  l <-> -l relating F(r,.) to a combination of F(r eta, .) over eta | 9; then Phragmén-Lindelöf.

## 3. The method in ten lines
Vaughan's identity (section 3) splits the prime sum into Type I sums F_l(X, alpha) (sums of g~ over multiples of a
modulus alpha) and a Type II bilinear sum in which twisted multiplicativity g~(ad) = (a/d)_3 (d/a)_3 g~(a) g~(d)
leaves a bilinear form in the cubic symbol, bounded by Lemma 1 (Cauchy-Schwarz twice, fourth power of character
sums, Hecke theta series of Lemma 2). Section 4 treats Type I: Lemma 3 reduces sums over c = 0 mod alpha to the
basic Dirichlet series psi(r,s,l) of Gauss sums, which are Fourier coefficients of Kubota's cubic metaplectic
Eisenstein series (Patterson 1977). Their meromorphic continuation, the single pole at s = 4/3 (the theta function's
residue), the functional equation and Phragmén-Lindelöf give Lemma 4; a truncated Perron formula (Titchmarsh Lemma
3.12) then gives Theorem 4, the X^{5/6} term being the residue at s = 4/3 (s + 1/2 = 4/3 in the normalized sum).
Choosing u = X^{9/31} balances the two estimates (Theorem 1). Theorem 2 follows from Theorem 1 plus Kubilius's
Hecke-character prime number theorem via Erdős-Turán.

## 4. Where it stops
- Only a power saving X^{30/31}; the conjectured X^{5/6} main term over primes is NOT reached (the Type II bound
  X^{7/8} and the Type I range N(alpha) <= X^{2/3} limit it). p. 113: "Theorem 1 is neither the most general nor the
  most precise result of its type".
- The spectral input is ONLY the Eisenstein series / theta on the cubic cover (continuation, functional equation,
  residue). No Kuznetsov formula, no cusp forms, no spectral large sieve appear.
- p. 114: the method extends to Gauss sums of order n > 2 in fields containing the n-th roots of unity; the one
  difficult point is [15] Theorem 6.1 for general n, "a consequence of the theory of Eisenstein series on metaplectic
  groups ... generally valid although it has not been discussed in detail in print".
- p. 129 Remark: without Patterson's Theorem 9.1 (the residue), Phragmén-Lindelöf alone gives p(alpha,1) <<
  N(alpha)^{-3/4+eps}, enough for Theorem 1.

## 5. What it changes in our record
- **Lead's question (c), short ranges.** The relevant bound here is Theorem 4: Gauss sums over the arithmetic
  progression c = 0 mod alpha up to X, with saving N(alpha)^{-5/8} X^{3/4} in the error, valid for N(alpha) <= X^{2/3}.
  It is a bound for sums of length X/N(alpha) in a progression, not for short intervals.
- **Lead's question (d).** The X^{5/6} is the residue at s = 4/3 of psi(r,s,0) = sum g(r,c) N(c)^{-s}, i.e. the pole
  coming from the theta function (residual spectrum of the cubic cover); residue p_0(r) proportional to
  conj(g(1,r)) N(r)^{-2/3}, i.e. to a theta Fourier coefficient. Same mechanism as the X^{5/6} in Louvel's Kuznetsov
  formula (`louvel-1107.0197.md`), seen from the Eisenstein series rather than from the spectral side.
- **SHORT_FAMILY.md (3b)/(3c) and N55.** This paper's Type I/Type II split uses no family average over twists and no
  exact spectral formula for a mean square; the only exact identity is the functional equation of the Gauss-sum
  Dirichlet series (dual length via s <-> 2 - s), the same kind of duality the record already identifies in the
  QRH paper's theta step.
