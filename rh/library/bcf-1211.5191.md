## 2. What it proves
d_N² = inf_{A_N} (1/2π)∫_R |1 − ζA_N(½+it)|² dt/(¼+t²), A_N(s) = Σ_{n ≤ N} a_n n^{−s} (Báez-Duarte's form; RH ⟺
d_N → 0). V_N(s) := Σ_{n ≤ N} (1 − log n/log N) μ(n) n^{−s}.
- **Background quoted.** Balazard–de Roton (RH): d_N² ≪ (log log N)^{5/2+ε}/√(log N). BDBLS 2000/2005
  (unconditional): liminf d_N² log N ≥ Σ_{Re ρ=½} 1/|ρ|² (distinct zeros on the line); Burnol: ≥ Σ m(ρ)²/|ρ|². Expected:
  d_N² ~ (1/log N)Σ m(ρ)²/|ρ|²; under RH Σ m(ρ)/|ρ|² = 2 + γ − log 4π, so for simple zeros d_N² ~ (2 + γ − log 4π)/log N.
  Landreau–Richard's graphs had suggested V_N is NOT optimal.
- **Theorem 1.** If RH holds and Σ_{|Im ρ| ≤ T} 1/|ζ′(ρ)|² ≪ T^{3/2−δ} for some δ > 0, then
  (1/2π)∫ |1 − ζV_N(½+it)|² dt/(¼+t²) ~ (2 + γ − log 4π)/log N. (The hypothesis forces simple zeros; Gonek /
  Hughes–Keating–O'Connell predict the sum ~ (6/π³)T.) So, conditionally, the BDBLS conjecture holds and the
  Levinson-weighted Möbius polynomial attains the optimal constant.
- **Theorem 2 (polynomial analogue).** P with simple zeros on or outside |z| = 1, 1/P = Σ a_n z^n,
  W_N(z) = Σ_{n ≤ N}(1 − n/N)a_n zⁿ: (1/2π)∫|1 − PW_N|² dθ ~ (1/N) Σ_{|ρ|=1} m(ρ)².
- **Lemma 2 (unconditional).** For 0 < Re s < 1: V_N(s) = (1/ζ(s))(1 − (1/log N)(ζ′/ζ)(s)) + (1/log N)Σ_ρ R_N(ρ,s)
  + (1/log N)F_s(1/N), R_N(ρ,s) = Res_{z=ρ} N^{z−s}/(ζ(z)(z−s)²), F_s entire (from the trivial zeros).
- **Lemma 3 (RH, simple zeros, hypothesis).** On Re s = ½ ± ε: Σ_ρ R_N(ρ,s) ≪ N^{∓ε}|s|^{¾−δ/2+ε}.

## 3. Method in ten lines
1. Write V_N = (1/log N)(1/2πi)∫ N^w/ζ(s+w) dw/w² and shift far left: residues at w = 0 (double pole), at every
   nontrivial zero (R_N terms) and at trivial zeros (F_s).
2. So 1 − ζV_N = (1/log N)·[ζ′/ζ(s) − ζ(s)Σ_ρ R_N(ρ,s) − ζ(s)F_s(1/N)]: exactly O(1/log N) pointwise away from zeros.
3. The NB integral becomes (1/log² N)(1/2πi)∫_{(½−ε)} (…)(s)(…)(1−s) ζ(s)ζ(1−s) ds/(s(1−s)).
4. Shift to Re s = ½ − ε (the integrand is analytic in between).
5. The cross term −(1/log² N)∫ (ζ′/ζ)(1−s) Σ_ρ R_N(ρ,s) ζ(s)/(s(1−s)) ds has, at each zero, a double pole with
   residue (log N)/|ρ|² + O(…); moving the line to ½ + ε gives (1/log N)Σ 1/|ρ|².
6. All other terms are O(1/log² N), using Lemma 3, the 1/|ζ′(ρ)| hypothesis (convergence of Σ 1/(|ζ′(ρ)||ρ|^α) for
   α > 5/4 − δ/2) and Lindelöf (which follows from RH) for |ζ| near the line.
7. The polynomial case is the same computation with finitely many zeros (no convergence issue).
8. No mean value theorem for ζ times a long Dirichlet polynomial is used: the weight 1/(¼+t²) plus RH-pointwise
   control replaces it.
9. Each zero contributes (log N)/|ρ|² · (1/log² N): the distance is a sum of LOCAL contributions at the zeros,
   weighted by 1/|ρ|², so dominated by low zeros (bounded height).
10. The 1/|ζ′(ρ)| hypothesis is what controls the sum over zeros of the residues (the "infinitely many zeros" issue).

## 4. What is assumed
RH; Σ_{|Im ρ| ≤ T} 1/|ζ′(ρ)|² ≪ T^{3/2−δ} (implies simple zeros). Lindelöf is used and deduced from RH. Lemma 2 is
unconditional.

## 5. Where it stops
- The asymptotic for d_N² is conditional on RH + the ζ′ moment bound. Unconditionally nothing new.
- No statement for weights other than (1 − log n/log N); no lower-order term (the record's lane B finds the
  Levinson recipe off the optimum by A/log² N, consistent with Theorem 1's error O(1/log² N)).
- No barrier stated.

## 6. What it changes in our record
- `rh/NOTEBOOK.md` N53 (a) "Conversely RH gives bounded approximants": for the Levinson-weighted Möbius approximant
  (N53's w_N(k) = 1 − log k/log N) the published result needs RH AND the 1/|ζ′(ρ)|² moment bound, and then gives far
  more than boundedness (‖1 − ζV_N‖² → 0 at the optimal rate). Under RH ALONE the published convergence is for the
  weights μ(n)n^{−ε} with ε = ε(N) (Báez-Duarte 2003, Balazard–de Roton 2008; see `balazard-deroton-0812.1689.md`),
  which also gives boundedness. Whether RH alone gives boundedness for N53's exact weights was not found in print by
  this reader (not searched beyond the papers read).
- N53 (b)/(c) and IDEAS #34 form (4): BCF evaluate the NB integral for the Möbius approximant WITHOUT any long
  mollifier mean value theorem; under RH the integral is computed by residues at the zeros. So "long mollifiers at
  bounded height" is not where a proof under RH has to go through a mean value; the bounded-height NB quantity is a
  sum over zeros of local contributions (log N)/|ρ|². The difficulty of N53 is therefore exactly the unconditional
  direction (no RH, so no pointwise control of 1/ζ near the line).
- IDEAS #5 (claim: optimal coefficients ≈ μ(k) × smooth cut-off; d_N² log N → 2λ_1 = 0.0461914): the leading-order
  part of this claim is BCF Theorem 1 (conditional). #5's novelty field already lists BCF "from recall"; this digest
  makes it a citation.
- KNOWLEDGE E5: the constant 2 + γ − log 4π is Σ m(ρ)/|ρ|² only under RH; BCF's theorem uses Σ 1/|ρ|² over simple zeros.
