# R. C. McPhedran, "Zeros of Lattice Sums: 1. Zeros off the Critical Line", arXiv 1601.01724 (Jan 2016)

Reader: READER-6, 2026-11-11. arXiv preprint, single author (physicist); journal status not stated in the source.
Mostly numerical.

Object: `S_0(lambda,s) = sum' (p_1^2 + lambda^2 p_2^2)^{-s}` (z = i lambda), symmetrised
`S~_0 = lambda^s Gamma(s) S_0/(8 pi^s)`, symmetric under s -> 1-s and lambda -> 1/lambda.

## 2. Main statements, with their numbers
- Eq. (mac2s2c): `d/d lambda S_0(lambda,s) at lambda = 1 = -s S_0(1,s)`, so zero trajectories leave lambda = 1
  perpendicularly (proved, one line).
- Expansion (sym5)-(sym9): `S~_0(lambda,s) = C~(0,1;s) + sum_m S_{2m}(s) sin^{2m} phi`, `tan phi = (lambda - 1/lambda)/2`,
  with S_{2m} explicit combinations of the angular sums `C~(1,4m;s) = Gamma(2m+s)/(8 pi^s) sum' cos(4m theta)/(p_1^2+p_2^2)^s`
  (Hecke L-functions of Q(i) with Grossencharacter of infinity type 4m; the paper does not say so). This is the
  Taylor expansion of the family at the CM point i in the direction of the imaginary axis.
- **Theorem 1.** A zero trajectory through a point s_0 on the line with dS~/ds != 0 stays on the line on an
  interval around s_0; at a point where also dS~/ds = 0 the trajectory runs along the line on one side and leaves
  it at right angles on the other (square-root branching). Proof: numerator real, denominator imaginary.
- Factorisations (sym10a) for lambda^2 = 1,2,3,4,5,6,7 from Borwein et al. "Lattice Sums Then and Now"; lambda^2 = 5,6
  are sums of two products (class number 2).
- Eq. (sym12): the constant-term part `T_+(lambda,s)` has all zeros on the line for lambda < 1 (Lagarias-Suzuki
  argument; for lambda > 1 by symmetry of S, not of T_+).
- Numerical: the Potter-Titchmarsh off-line zero of S_0(sqrt 5, s) near 0.9329 + 15.6682 i lies on a trajectory
  that reaches the line at a double zero at lambda^2 between 4.0007109410 and 4.0007109411 (just above lambda^2 = 4,
  sandwiched between a prefactor zero of S_0(2,s) at t ~ 16.3846 and a zero of L_{-4} at t ~ 16.3425) and again at
  lambda^2 in (6.343471, 6.343472). So for lambda in (about 2.0002, about 2.51863), S_0 has zeros off the line near
  t ~ 16 (the text says "the range of lambda is from 2 to around 2.51863").

## 3. The method in ten lines
Implicit-function argument on the line (the function is real there up to a phase); Taylor expansion in
sin phi around the square lattice; Kober/Chowla-Selberg Bessel expansion for numerics.

## 4. What is assumed
Numerics only for the trajectory; "this cannot be proved numerically" for the double zero at the transitions.

## 5. Where its frontier is
Proved: local structure (Theorem 1). Numerical: one trajectory. Not done: any global or counting statement.

## 6. What it explicitly does not do
No statement for non-rectangular lattices; it notes that an earlier attempt (arXiv 1007.4111) to relate RH for
angular lattice sums to RH for the square-lattice Epstein function "contained an error" (Heath-Brown).

## 7. Facts in our own record that this paper corrects or shows to be already known
- **N45 (1)** (collisions): KNOWN locally (Theorem 1), the same fold mechanism as in `2110.09368.md`.
- **N45 (2)** (Taylor expansion at a CM point generates Hecke L-functions): the expansion (sym5)-(sym9) at z = i,
  along the imaginary axis, is an instance: coefficients are polynomials in s times the angular lattice sums
  C~(1,4m;s), which are (reader's identification, elementary: `sum' cos(4m theta) |lambda|^{-2s}` over Z[i] =
  `4 Re L(s, psi^{4m})`, psi((a)) = (a/|a|)) Hecke L-functions with Grossencharacters of infinity type 4m.
- Point of fact for the lead: the off-line interval near lambda^2 = 4 ends at lambda^2 = 4.00071, i.e. at a point
  0.00018 away (in lambda) from the class-number-one-type point z = 2i (where S_0 = 2(1-2^{-s}+2^{1-2s}) zeta L_{-4});
  so off-line zeros at height ~16 occur within distance 1.8e-4 (in lambda) of that point. One height, one
  trajectory, numerical: an illustration of the situation conjectured in N45 (4), not evidence about infinitely
  many heights.

## 8. Open questions the paper itself states
Generality of the trajectory picture; using the factorisable cases to estimate the density of zeros on and off
the line.
