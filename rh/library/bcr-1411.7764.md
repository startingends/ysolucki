## 2. What it proves (all unconditional unless marked)
- **Background quoted (§1).** Balasubramanian–Conrey–Heath-Brown (Crelle 357, 1985): for θ < ½ and φ = 1_[1,2],
  I = T Σ_{d,e ≤ T^θ} a_d ā_e/[d,e] · (log(T(d,e)²/(2π de)) + 2γ + log 4 − 1) + o(T). For a MOLLIFIER they go to
  θ < ½ + 1/34 = 9/17 (38% of zeros on the line). The formula fails in general for θ > 1; BCH conjecture it holds
  for all θ < 1 (the "θ = 1 conjecture"). At θ = ½ "an important change occurs": below ½ only diagonal terms
  contribute; above ½ off-diagonal terms contribute to the MAIN term.
- **Theorem 1.** For θ < ½ + δ, δ = 1/66 (i.e. θ < 17/33), and ANY coefficients a_n ≪ n^ε:
  I = Σ_{d,e ≤ T^θ} (a_d ā_e/[d,e]) ∫ (log(t(d,e)²/(2π de)) + 2γ) φ(t/T) dt + O(T^{3/20+ε} N^{33/20} + T^{1/3+ε}).
  Remark in the text: the off-diagonal terms contribute to the main term "roughly those d and e for which the
  logarithm is negative". Using DFI 1997 (Invent. 128) Theorem 2 alone gives δ = 1/190.
- **Proposition 2 (from Bettin–Chandee, "Trilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions", then a preprint).** For
  α_m (m ~ M), β_n (n ~ N), ν_a (a ~ A):
  Σ_a ΣΣ_{(m,n)=1} ν_a α_m β_n e(a m̄/n) ≪ ‖α‖‖β‖‖ν‖ (1 + A/(MN))^{1/2} ((AMN)^{7/20+ε}(M+N)^{1/4}
  + (AMN)^{3/8+ε}(AN+AM)^{1/8}).  This is the arithmetic input that crosses ½ for general coefficients.
- **Theorem 2 (conditional).** If the trilinear bound (1.3)
  S_{A,M,N} ≪ ‖α‖‖β‖‖ν‖(M+N)^{½+r+ε}A^t + ‖ν‖A^{½}(‖α‖_∞‖β‖N^{½+ε} + ‖α‖‖β‖_∞M^{½+ε})
  holds for A ≪ (NM)^{(0.5−r)/(1+2t)+ε}, then Theorem 1's formula holds with error T^{½−t+ε}N^{½+r+2t} + T^{1/3+ε}
  for θ < ½ + (0.5 − r)/(1 + 2(r + 2t)). DFI gives (r, t) = (23/48, ½); Bettin–Chandee give (9/20, 7/20).
- **Conjecture 1** ((r,t) = (0,0) for A ≪ (NM)^{½+ε}) and **Corollary 1**: Conjecture 1 ⇒ the formula for every
  θ < 1 ⇒ the Lindelöf Hypothesis. The authors say Conjecture 1 "appears to be strictly stronger than" Lindelöf
  (Lindelöf only gives I ≪ T^{1+ε} Σ|a_n|²/n).
- **Appendix A, Proposition.** For A ≪ (MN)^{½+ε}: max over |α|,|β|,|ν| ≤ 1 of |S_{A,M,N}| ≫ (AMN)^{½−ε}(M+N)^{½}
  + A(M+N)^{1−ε}. So Conjecture 1 is best possible up to ε-powers (examples: smooth α plus primes in residue classes
  mod 4; and an average over Dirichlet characters with Hölder).
- **Corollary 2.** ∫_T^{2T} |ζ(½+it)|³ dt ≪ T (log T)^{9/4} (previously known only on RH, Heath-Brown 1981). Uses
  Theorem 1 with A = Σ d_{1/2}(n) n^{-s} of length T^{½+η}, 0 < η < 1/66 (Lemma 2), Gabriel convexity, and an
  absorption argument. §5.1 sketches 2k = 2 + 2/n moments (not claimed).
- **Theorem 3 (A = BC product, Deshouillers–Iwaniec input).** For J = ∫|ζ|²|A|²|B|², A of length N, B of length K,
  N ≥ K: the BCH main term + O(T^ε(T^{½}N^{3/4}K + T^{½}NK^{½} + N^{7/4}K^{3/2})). Uses Prop. 3 = Deshouillers–Iwaniec
  (Acta Arith., "Power mean values ... II", 1984) bilinear bound for incomplete Kloosterman sums
  Σ_ℓ Σ_j |Σ_u Σ_v c(u,v) e(u ϱ̄ v̄ j̄ /ℓ)|. "With N, K chosen suitably" this gives θ < ½ + 1/10 for products.
- **Theorem 4 (Watt input).** A smooth (α_n = ψ(n)) of length N ≪ T^{½+ε}, B arbitrary of length K ≪ T^{1/4}, both
  supported near the top of their ranges (ξ_1 ≤ 1/5, ξ_2 ≤ 1/16): the main term + O(T^{½+ε}K² + KN^{3/4}T^{3/8+ε}
  + T^{39/40 + ξ_1/8 + 2ξ_2/5 + ε}). So θ < ¾ for "A(s) pretending to be ζ(s)" times B of length up to T^{¼−ε}.
  Input: Watt 1995 (J. Number Theory 53), Prop. 4.1, a Deshouillers–Iwaniec-type spectral bound.

## 3. Method in ten lines
1. Approximate functional equation: |ζ(½+it)|² = 2 Σ_{m1,m2} (m1m2)^{-½}(m1/m2)^{it} W(2π m1 m2/t) + O(T^{-2/3}).
2. Expand |A|²: terms with m1 n2 = m2 n1 are the diagonal D; the rest is S, indexed by Δ = m1 n2 − m2 n1 ≠ 0.
3. Integration by parts in t kills |Δ| > D := M N_1/T^{1−ε}; small Δ linearised.
4. The congruence n1 m2 ≡ −Δ (mod n2) is opened by POISSON in m2 mod n2: dual variable h, phase e(−h Δ n̄1/n2).
5. h = 0 gives an explicit term A_0; by the functional equation of ζ, D + A_0 = the full main term (the "negative
   logarithm" part comes from here). This is where θ > ½ starts to matter for the main term.
6. h ≠ 0 (|h| < H_d = N_2 T^ε/(dM)) leaves a TRILINEAR form in a = hΔ, n1, n2 with Kloosterman fractions e(a n̄1/n2),
   with a ranging up to A = N_1N_2/(d²T^{1−ε}). For θ < ½, A < 1 and these terms are empty; θ > ½ is exactly A > 1.
7. Theorem 1: Prop. 2 bounds it by T^{3/20}N^{33/20}; this is o(T) iff N < T^{17/33}.
8. Theorems 3, 4: if a_n = (α ⋆ β)(n) is a convolution, the Kloosterman fraction becomes e(u ϱ̄ v̄ j̄ /ℓ) with one
   more free variable, and Deshouillers–Iwaniec / Watt bounds (spectral theory of Kloosterman sums) apply.
9. The coefficients a_n enter only through ‖a‖_2 and ‖a‖_∞ in Theorem 1: no structure of a_n is used.
10. Appendix: the conjectured trilinear bound is sharp, so the trilinear route alone reaches θ < 1 at best.

## 4. What is assumed
Theorem 1, Prop. 2, Theorems 3-4, Corollary 2: unconditional. Theorem 2 and Corollary 1: the trilinear bound (1.3) /
Conjecture 1. a_n ≪ n^ε throughout; φ smooth with support in [1,2] (height T to 2T, never bounded height).

## 5. Where it stops
- Last thing proved for GENERAL coefficients: θ < 17/33 = ½ + 1/66. First thing not proved: any θ ≥ 17/33.
- The exponent is set by the trilinear Kloosterman-fraction bound; Theorem 2 makes the dependence exact:
  θ_max = ½ + (0.5 − r)/(1 + 2(r + 2t)). With (r,t) = (0,0) one gets θ < 1, i.e. the BCH "θ = 1 conjecture", which
  would imply Lindelöf.
- **Stated barrier.** θ = 1 is the ceiling of the method and of the formula: the BCH formula "fails in general when
  θ > 1" (stated, no proof given here) and Conjecture 1 is shown best possible (Appendix), so even the optimal
  trilinear bound gives only θ < 1. Nothing in this paper addresses θ > 1. The reason given for the ½ barrier: below
  ½ only diagonal terms contribute; above ½ off-diagonal terms enter the main term "which seems difficult to manage
  given the generality of A".
- Explicitly not done: the analogous ASYMPTOTIC for the twisted second moment of Dirichlet L-functions in the
  q-aspect (DFI get only an upper bound for length q^{½+δ'}); "our proof ... would not extend ... additional input
  is needed". Moments 2 + 2/n: sketched only, not claimed.
- Open question stated: other applications of Theorem 4 (e.g. large gaps between zeros, Bredberg).

## 6. What it changes in our record
- `rh/NOTEBOOK.md` N53 (b): "known only for lengths up to about T^(1/2+δ) (Bettin–Chandee–Radziwiłł; recalled,
  unread)". Now exact: δ = 1/66 for arbitrary coefficients a_n ≪ n^ε (Theorem 1), with error
  T^{3/20}N^{33/20}; for convolution coefficients ½ + 1/10 (Theorem 3) and ¾ for smooth × short (Theorem 4). For
  Möbius-type coefficients the record should cite Conrey 1989 (θ < 4/7; see `conrey-1989.md`), not BCR.
- N53 (b) speaks of "twists (a/b)^(it), a, b ≤ N ≫ T". BCR's whole framework is θ < 1 (N < T) at height [T, 2T];
  the regime N ≫ T at bounded height is outside every theorem here, and BCR state the formula FAILS in general for
  θ > 1. So the NB regime of N53 is not "BCR with a longer length": it is beyond the paper's own stated ceiling.
- N57 (self-dual point / convexity barrier, broken by amplification + Kuznetsov + Kloosterman): in BCR the ½ point is
  where the dual variable a = hΔ of the Poisson step first becomes non-empty (A = N²/T > 1); crossing it uses a
  Kloosterman-fraction bilinear/trilinear estimate (DFI; Bettin–Chandee), with NO amplifier and, for general
  coefficients, no spectral formula (the Bettin–Chandee bound is not quoted with its method here; DFI 1997 is
  elementary-plus-Weil, from memory). Spectral input (Deshouillers–Iwaniec, Watt) appears only when the coefficients
  factor (Theorems 3, 4). So "amplification + Kuznetsov" is not the mechanism of the known crossings of θ = ½ for
  this mean value; the mechanism is Poisson + bounds for sums of Kloosterman fractions, with Kuznetsov entering only
  through DI-type bilinear bounds for factorable coefficients.
- IDEAS #34 (short-family wall): BCR's ½ is a regime boundary of the same "family ≈ sum length" type (the t-family of
  length T against ζM, a Dirichlet polynomial of effective length T^{½}N after the approximate functional equation:
  they are equal exactly at θ = ½, and A = N²/T > 1 is the same condition — reader's reading, not stated in BCR), and it is crossed only by
  a fixed amount (1/66) for arbitrary coefficients; the paper gives no parity-type obstruction at ½. The record's
  statement "only arithmetic specific to μ can help" (N53 (c)) is contradicted at the level of exponents: BCR cross
  ½ with NO arithmetic of the coefficients at all.
