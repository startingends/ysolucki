# A. Olevskii, A. Ulanovskii, "Fourier quasicrystals with unit masses", arXiv 2009.12810 v1

## 1. Reference and extent of reading
- Alexander Olevskii (Tel Aviv), Alexander Ulanovskii (Stavanger). arXiv 2009.12810, **v1 only** (27 Sep 2020,
  "8 pages"). The arXiv API gives no journal reference; the C. R. Math. (Paris) 2020 publication named in the brief
  was not checked against the arXiv text.
- Source: single gzipped TeX `cryst.tex`, saved as `rh/library/src/crystal_2009.12810/x/main.tex` (gitignored; never
  compiled). **Read whole: 17,240 / 17,240 characters (322 lines)**, including commented-out lines (an earlier
  abstract saying the construction of [OU20] "characterizes all crystalline measures of this type").
- Companion read in full for the converse: Olevskii–Ulanovskii, "A Simple Crystalline Measure", arXiv 2006.12037
  (note `olevskii-ulanovskii-2006.12037.md`).

Conventions: crystalline measure = atomic tempered μ with atomic μ̂, support Λ and spectrum S locally finite; Fourier
quasicrystal (FQ) = in addition |μ| and |μ̂| tempered. Fourier transform ĥ(t) = ∫ e^{−2πitx} h(x) dx. "Relatively
uniformly discrete" = finite union of uniformly discrete sets.

## 2. What it proves (all unconditional)
- **Proposition 1.** μ positive, tempered, with μ̂ a measure satisfying ∫_{(−R,R)} d|μ̂| = O(R^m) for some m (|μ̂|
  tempered). Then there is C with **μ(a, b) ≤ C(1 + b − a) for all a < b.** (No discreteness of S assumed.)
- **Corollary 1.** If μ = Σ_{λ∈Λ} δ_λ and μ̂ is a measure with (2), then Λ is relatively uniformly discrete.
- **Lemma 1.** For an FQ μ = Σ δ_λ (0 ∉ Λ): Σ_λ (1/(w − λ) + 1/λ) = α − 2πi Σ_{s∈S, s<0} a_s e^{−2πiws} for Im w > 0;
  the mirror formula with s > 0 holds for Im w < 0.
- **Theorem 1.** Let μ = Σ_{λ∈Λ} δ_λ be an FQ. Then there is an exponential polynomial
  p(x) = Σ_{k=1}^n b_k e^{iγ_k x}, n ∈ ℕ, b_k ∈ ℂ, γ_k ∈ ℝ, **with real simple zeros, such that Λ is the zero set of p.**
- Converse (stated after Theorem 1, proved in arXiv 2006.12037): for every exponential polynomial p with real simple
  zeros there is an FQ with unit masses supported on its zero set.
- **Remark 1.** With minor changes: if μ = Σ c_λ δ_λ, c_λ ∈ ℕ, is an FQ, then Λ is the zero set of an exponential
  polynomial with imaginary frequencies and real, not necessarily simple, zeros. **Remark 2.** Conversely "one may
  check" that every exponential polynomial with imaginary frequencies and real zeros gives an FQ of that form.
  (Whether c_λ equals the multiplicity is not said in Remark 1.)

## 3. Method in ten lines
1. Positivity + |μ̂| tempered: test against g ∗ 1_{(a−½, b+½)}, whose Fourier transform is ≤ (1 + b − a)|ĝ|: linear
   bound on μ(a, b) (Prop. 1). So Σ 1/|λ|^{1+ε} < ∞.
2. Canonical product ψ(z) = Π (1 − z/λ)e^{z/λ} is entire of order one (Borel).
3. Test μ against i h(ελ)/(w − λ), whose Fourier transform equals 2π h(εw) e^{−2πiwt} on t < s_1 (largest negative
   spectral point): ψ'/ψ = α − 2πi Σ_{s<0} a_s e^{−2πiws} in the upper half-plane (Lemma 1), similarly below.
4. Integrate: ψ(w) = C e^{αw} exp(Σ_{s<0} (a_s/s) e^{−2πisw}); p := ψ e^{−αw} is bounded in Im w ≥ r and of controlled
   growth below; Phragmén–Lindelöf makes p of exponential type, bounded in strips.
5. Expanding the exponentials gives p = Σ_{u∈U} b_u e^{−2πiuw}, U ⊂ the additive semigroup generated by S_−.
6. Paley–Wiener applied to p·(sin πεw/πεw)²: the spectrum of p is in (−σ − ε, ε), so b_u = 0 for u < −σ: finitely
   many terms remain. The work is in steps 3–4 (turning the spectral data into the logarithmic derivative).

## 4. Where it stops
- Only unit (Remark 1: positive integer) masses. Real positive non-integer masses, signed masses, and measures whose
  Fourier transform has a continuous part are outside.
- Remarks 1 and 2 are stated without proof ("minor changes", "one may check").
- No description of WHICH exponential polynomials have only real zeros; that question is left as is (the paper
  reduces the classification of unit-mass FQs to it).

## 5. What it changes in our record
- **NOTEBOOK N123 and specialists/operator/BOOTSTRAP.md (ii).** Positive measure + integer masses + discrete Fourier
  support (with |μ̂| tempered) is a CLOSED class here: zero sets of finite exponential polynomials with real zeros, all
  of bounded density (Prop. 1). The zero measure of ζ has density log(T/2π)/2π → ∞ (Riemann–von Mangoldt, standard),
  so it is not in this class; nor, by Prop. 1, in any class of positive measures with |μ̂| a tempered measure. Under
  RH its Fourier transform (Guinand–Weil, standard, not from this paper) has a non-atomic archimedean part, so the
  Fourier side is not a discrete measure anyway. The "(ii) discreteness with integer multiplicities" of N123 is
  therefore a property this theorem cannot supply for ζ: its hypotheses fail at the density.
- Kurasov–Sarnak's measures (`kurasov-sarnak-2004.05678.md`) are the special case p(x) = P(e^{−iξx}), frequencies
  m·ξ with m in the support of P.

## 6. Reliability
- Short note by two experts; the proof is complete except "One may check that U is locally finite" and the omitted
  remarks. Not checked against a journal version. No zeta, L-function or prime appears anywhere in the text.
