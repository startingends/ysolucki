# Library: whole papers, read in full

**For knowledge and understanding only** (owner, 2026-11-11). Reading whole papers is how a run starts at
the frontier instead of rediscovering it. It is NOT a direction. A digest never says what the project should
do, never ranks approaches, and reading a paper is never a reason to follow it. The owner's words: "don't let
it trick you into thinking that's the way to go, or that it's actually important; it's just for increasing
your knowledge and understanding."

How it works
- A reader seat downloads the whole paper (full text kept under `rh/data/papers/`, gitignored), reads ALL of
  it, and writes a digest here in its own words: `rh/library/<id>.md`.
- Every digest has the same parts: what was read (characters read / total; must be all of it) · the main
  theorems, stated exactly, with theorem numbers · the method in ten lines · what is assumed · **where its
  frontier is: the last thing it proves and the first thing it cannot** · what it explicitly does not do ·
  facts in our own record that this paper corrects or shows to be already known (file and entry) ·
  open questions the paper itself states. No recommendations.
- Every run's brief points here: read the digests relevant to you before you start, as background.
