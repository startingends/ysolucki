Notation of the paper. Gamma = SL_2(Z), Delta = y^2(d_x^2 + d_y^2) (so -Delta >= 0), lambda_s = s(1-s),
E_s(z) = sum over Gamma_inf\Gamma of Im(gamma z)^s, E_s = c_s E_{1-s}, c_s = xi(2-2s)/xi(2s), <1,1> = pi/3.
Pseudo-Eisenstein series Psi_phi(z) = sum phi(Im gamma z), phi in C_c^inf(0, inf). For a functional theta,
`E theta(s) := theta(E_{1-s})` (spectral coefficient) and `theta E_s` its value on E_s.

## 2. Main statements, with numbers

### 2a. The space and the functionals (sections 2, 3)
- **Space.** E^0 = the L^2 closure of the pseudo-Eisenstein series with test data = the orthogonal complement of
  the cusp forms in L^2(Gamma\H): the constants plus the continuous spectrum. ("In [CdV-pseudo] this smaller space
  is suggested to be the orthogonal complement to the discrete spectrum, but it turns out to be necessary to
  retain the constants".) **Eisenstein-Sobolev spaces** E^r = completion of the pseudo-Eisenstein test series in
  the norm |<f,1>|^2 (1+lambda_1)^r/<1,1> + (1/4 pi i) int_(1/2) |E f(s)|^2 (1+lambda_s)^r ds (section 3.4); E^r is
  isometric to C + L^2((1/4 + t^2)^r dt). E^{-r} is the dual of E^r.
- **Why the cuspidal part is removed** (intro and section 3.5): an automorphic Dirac delta is in H^{-1-eps} for every
  eps > 0 but not in H^{-1} (pre-trace formula). Its non-cuspidal part `delta^nc_{z0} = 1/<1,1> +
  (1/4 pi i) int E_{1-s}(z0) E_s ds` is in E^{-1-eps} for any point (pre-trace bound), and Sobolev embedding
  E^{1+eps} in C^0 holds (section 3.5).
- **Eisenstein-Heegner distribution** (section 3.6): for a fundamental discriminant d < -4,
  theta_d = sum of delta^nc_z over the h(d) Heegner points; eq. (3.6-): `E theta_d(s) = (sqrt|d|/2)^s zeta(s)
  L(s, chi_d)/zeta(2s)` (= (sqrt|d|/2)^s zeta_k(s)/zeta(2s), k = Q(sqrt d)); footnote: for d = -3, -4 the formulas
  are corrected by factors 3 and 2. "The Eisenstein-Heegner distributions theta_d belong to the space
  E^{-1/2-eps} for any eps > 0, from the Landau bound 1/zeta(1+it) = O(log T) and the deeper second moment bound
  for zeta(s)L(s,chi) on the critical line." (The introduction states the weaker E^{-3/4-eps}.) This is where
  arithmetic enters: it is what puts theta inside E^{-1}.
- Section 3.7: for Re w > 1/2 the equation (-Delta - lambda_w)u = theta has a unique solution
  u_{theta,w} = theta(1)/(<1,1>(lambda_1 - lambda_w)) + (1/4 pi i) int theta E_{1-s} E_s ds/(lambda_s - lambda_w),
  in E^{3/2-eps}. Section 3.8: constant-term functional eta_a f = int_0^1 f(x + ia) dx, a > 1, in E^{-1/2-eps};
  spectral coefficient a^{1-s} + c_{1-s} a^s; **Prop. 9** (a compactly supported real measure restricted to
  E^inf is in E^{-1-eps} with E mu(s) = mu(E_{1-s})).

### 2b. The abstract tool (section 2)
- **Theorem 4.** For S >= c > 0 symmetric with Friedrichs space V_1, and Theta a finite-dimensional subspace of
  V_{-1} with Theta meeting (the image of) V only in 0: the Friedrichs extension S~_Theta of S restricted to
  D cap ker Theta has domain `{x in V_1 cap ker Theta : S# x in V + Theta}`, and S~_Theta x = y iff
  S# x = y + theta for some theta in Theta. **Corollary 5**: a solution x in V_1 of (S# - lambda)x = theta in Theta
  with x in ker Theta is an eigenvector; lambda is real (>= c; >= 0 if S is only non-negative). Lemma 2 (density
  of D cap ker Theta), Lemma 3. Theorem 8: the same when the ambient space changes to the orthocomplement of an
  S-stable subspace (the Lax-Phillips situation).

### 2c. The operator at the CM point (section 4.1)
- **Definition.** For theta in E^{-1}, theta real, theta not in E^0: S_theta = -Delta on
  E^inf_c cap ker theta (pseudo-Eisenstein test series killed by theta); the **pseudo-Laplacian** S~_theta is its
  Friedrichs extension, an operator on E^0 (form domain E^1 cap ker theta, operator domain as in Theorem 4).
  Introduction: "for u in V, (S~_theta - lambda)u = 0 if and only if (-Delta - lambda)u = c theta for some constant
  c, and theta u = 0."
- **Theorem 10.** "For real lambda_w > 1/4, if the equation (-Delta - lambda_w)u = theta has a solution in E^1
  then E theta(w) = 0. More precisely, ... int_{Im w - eps}^{Im w + eps} |E theta(1/2 + it)| dt <<_{w,eps}
  eps^{3/2}."
- **Corollary 11.** "The discrete spectrum lambda_w > 1/4 of S~_theta, **if any**, is of the form w(1-w) for
  w in 1/2 + iR such that E theta(w) = 0." With theta = theta_d this is: eigenvalues > 1/4 only at s(1-s) for
  zeros s of zeta_k ON the critical line. For theta = delta^nc at omega = e^{2 pi i/3} (intro): zeros of
  zeta(s) L(s, chi_{-3}).
- **Remark 12.** For theta in E^{-1+eps}, E theta(w) = 0 on the line is also sufficient for a solution u in E^1,
  "However, such u is not in the domain of the self-adjoint operator S~_theta unless also theta u = 0, which does
  not follow from E theta(w) = 0 in general."
- **Remark 13.** "there appears to be no general assurance of existence of any discrete spectrum whatsoever."
- **Remark 14.** For Re w > 1/2 off (1/2, 1], Im(theta u_{theta,w}) != 0 "is elementary" (Herglotz property).

### 2d. Lax-Phillips and the two-constraint operator (sections 4.2-5)
- **Theorem 16.** For a > 1 and Theta = pseudo-Eisenstein series with data supported in [a, inf), the Friedrichs
  extension S~_Theta of -Delta on E^0_Theta (non-cuspidal "pseudo-cuspforms": constant term vanishing above
  height a) has compact resolvent, meromorphic in w, compact off a discrete subset of (1/2 + iR) u [0, 1]; purely
  discrete spectrum. ("Essentially as in [Lax-Phillips] but restricting to the orthogonal complement E^0 to
  cuspforms".)
- **Theorem 18.** For a > 1 the parameters of eigenvalues > 1/4 of S~_Theta are exactly the zeros of
  a^w + c_w a^{1-w} on Re w = 1/2; simple; eigenfunctions the truncated Eisenstein series wedge^a E_w, with
  (-Delta - lambda_w) wedge^a E_w = 2(1-2w) a^{w+1} eta_a. Remark 19: they are not smooth.
- Theorem 20, Corollaries 21, 22: density, exotic eigenfunction expansions converging in E^1.
- **Theorem 23.** With theta a real combination of theta_d and a not a Heegner height: the Friedrichs extension
  S~_{theta,a} of -Delta on E^inf_c cap ker theta cap ker eta_a has eigenvectors exactly the u in its domain
  with (S# - lambda_w)u = A theta + B eta_a.
- Theorems 24-27 (explicit pairings): eta_a(v_{w,a}) = a^{1-w}(a^w + c_w a^{1-w})/(2w-1); theta_d(v_{w,a}) =
  (a^{1-w} theta_d E_w - R_w(d,a))/(2w-1), R_w = sum over Heegner points of height y > a of
  (a^{1-w} y^w - a^w y^{1-w}); eta_a(u_{theta,w}) = theta(v_{w,a}); theta(u_{theta,w}) =
  |theta(1)|^2/(<1,1>(lambda_1 - lambda_w)) + (1/4 pi i) int |theta E_s|^2 ds/(lambda_s - lambda_w).
- **Corollary 28** (unconditional non-vanishing). For all a > 1, Re w > 1/2, w not in (1/2, 1]:
  a^{1-w}(a^w + c_w a^{1-w}) (|theta(1)|^2/(<1,1>(lambda_1 - lambda_w)) + (1/4 pi i) int |theta E_s|^2
  ds/(lambda_s - lambda_w)) != (a^{1-w} theta E_w - R_w(theta,a))^2/(2w-1). (Vanishing would give a non-real
  eigenvalue of S~_{theta,a}.)
- **Theorem 29.** G(w,a) := F(w,a)/(a^w + c_w a^{1-w}), F the 2x2 determinant (a above the Heegner points), is
  meromorphic in C with G(w,a) = G(1-w,a); explicitly
  G = J_{theta,w} + [(a^w - c_w a^{1-w})/(a^w + c_w a^{1-w})] theta E_w theta E_{1-w}/(2(2w-1)),
  J_{theta,w} = |theta(1)|^2/(<1,1>(lambda_1 - lambda_w)) + (1/4 pi i) int (theta E_s theta E_{1-s} -
  theta E_w theta E_{1-w}) ds/(lambda_s - lambda_w).
- **Corollary 30.** "The only zeros of the function G(w,a) ... are on Re(w) = 1/2 and [0,1]." (Unconditional.)
- Section 5.8 ("An important remark"): the same for any real combination sum b_nu delta^nc_{z_nu} with
  sum b_nu E_s(z_nu) = 0 at the point; consequence stated: if the on-line zeros of such a combination "had a
  spectral interpretation for some a = a_0 > max Im(z_nu) this would be so for all a > a_0, implying that the
  corresponding eigenvalues w(1-w) would be independent of a. For a -> inf this is analogous to the condition
  formulated by Colin de Verdiere in the special case ... z_1 = 1/2 + i sqrt3/2, and, tentatively, suggested by
  him as a spectral intepretation of the zeros of zeta_{Q(sqrt-3)}(s) on the critical line."
- Section 5.9, **Theorem 31**: from Goldfeld-Hoffstein (quoted verbatim), sum over squarefree -x < m < -1 of
  L(s, chi_m)/zeta(2s) = A(s) x + B(s) x^{3/2 - s} + O(x^{19/32+eps}), uniformly for 1/2 <= Re s <= 1,
  |Im s| < omega(x). No later use of it is made in the text.

### 2e. Meromorphic continuation; the two sheets (section 6)
- **Theorem 43.** For theta = conj(theta) in E^{-1+eps} and continuous Phi from the space M of moderate-growth
  continuous functions: w -> u_{theta,w,Phi} continues meromorphically to C; with
  J_{theta,w,Phi} = theta(1)Phi(1)/((lambda_1 - lambda_w)<1,1>) + (1/4 pi i) int (theta E_{1-s} Phi E_s -
  theta E_{1-w} Phi E_w) ds/(lambda_s - lambda_w), **J_{theta,1-w,Phi} = J_{theta,w,Phi}** and
  **u_{theta,w,Phi} = J_{theta,w,Phi} + theta E_{1-w} Phi E_w/(2(1-2w))**. The continuation of u_{theta,w} lives
  in M, not in E^{1+eps}.
- **Corollary 46** ("Debunk illusion of symmetry"). For Re w < 1/2:
  **u_{theta,w} = u_{theta,1-w} + theta E_w E_{1-w}/(1-2w)**; so u_{theta,w} is in E^{1+eps} iff theta E_{1-w} = 0.
- **Corollary 47.** On Re w = 1/2: Re(theta u_{theta,w}) = J_{theta,w}, Im(theta u_{theta,w}) =
  -theta E_{1-w} theta E_w/(4 Im w).
- **Corollary 48.** For Re w <= 1/2 the continued u_{theta,w} is in E^{1+eps} iff theta E_w = 0.
- **Corollary 49.** An eigenfunction of S~_theta with eigenvalue > 1/4 is a multiple of the continued u_{theta,w};
  then theta E_w = 0 = theta E_{1-w} and theta u_{theta,w} = 0; conversely these conditions give an
  eigenfunction. **Corollary 52.** "A necessary and sufficient condition for lambda_w > 1/4 to be an eigenvalue for
  S~_theta is that theta u_{theta,w} = 0" (on the line this forces theta E_w = 0 and J_{theta,w} = 0).
- **Corollary 53.** a^w + c_w a^{1-w} = 0 implies Re w = 1/2 or w in [0, 1] ("already observed in [Hejhal-Polya],
  but there for non-spectral reasons").
- **Section 6.7, "Remarks on appealing but incorrect arguments"**: if one believed u_{theta,1-w} = u_{theta,w},
  Remark 14 would put the zeros of theta u_{theta,w} on the line; a Backlund argument-principle count gives
  (T/pi) log T + O(T) zeros of theta u_{theta,w} up to height T; on the line theta u = 0 implies theta E = 0; so
  zeta_k would have 100% of its zeros on the line. "The argument is fundamentally flawed, at the point where one
  fallaciously imagines that u_{theta,1-w} = u_{theta,w}: the functional equation of u_{theta,w} involves an extra
  term, which vanishes exactly for theta E_w = 0, thus thwarting this naively optimistic approach."

### 2f. Spacing (sections 7, 8)
- Theorem 54, Prop. 55, Theorem 56, Corollaries 57, 58: exotic eigenfunction expansion of the solution in the
  eigenbasis f_j of S~_Theta; theta u = sum_j |theta(f_j)|^2/(lambda_{s_j} - lambda_w) when theta E_w = 0.
- **Corollaries 59-62 (interleaving).** Between adjacent zeros of a^s + c_s a^{1-s} on the line, tau ->
  theta v_{theta, 1/2 + i tau} is real, strictly increasing from -inf to +inf (Cor. 59); hence (Cor. 62) "There is
  at most one w on Re(w) = 1/2 between adjacent zeros s_j and s_{j+1} of a^s + c_s a^{1-s} ... such that lambda_w
  is an eigenvalue for S~_theta."
- **Prop. 63**: psi(t) = arg xi(1+2it) = t log t + O(t log t/log log t), psi'(t) = log t + O(log t/log log t)
  (from Titchmarsh (5.17.4)). **Corollary 64**: the gaps between consecutive real zeros of
  a^{1/2+it} + c a^{1/2-it} at height t lie in [(1-eps) pi/log t, (1+eps) pi/log t] for 1 < a <= a_0, t large.
  **Theorem 65**: dt/da = (-t/a)/(log a + psi'(t)), denominator != 0 by Maass-Selberg (equal to
  <wedge^a E, wedge^a E>/2).
- **Theorem 67 / Corollary 68.** For zeros w_1, w_2 of theta E_w at height > T_0: if theta u_{theta,w_1} >=
  theta u_{theta,w_2} (in particular if both are 0, i.e. both eigen-parameters of S~_theta), then
  |tau_2 - tau_1| >= (1 - eps) pi/log tau_1. So eigen-parameters of S~_theta, if any, are at least (1-eps) times
  pi/log T apart (unconditional).
- **Corollary 69** ("assuming the Riemann Hypothesis and pair correlation"): "At most 94% of the zeros of zeta(s)
  give eigenvalues lambda_s = s(1-s) for S~_theta." Proof: Montgomery's pair-correlation density gives
  int_0^{1/2}(1 - (sin pi u/pi u)^2) du ~ 0.11315 for pairs closer than half the mean spacing 2 pi/log T, and one
  zero of each such pair is excluded. (The text does not write out how 0.11315 becomes 94%; this reader: one
  excluded zero may serve two pairs, 1 - 0.11315/2 = 0.943.)
- **Remark 70.** "This shows that the optimistic simple version of the conjecture at the end of [CdV-pseudo]
  cannot hold. Namely, with theta the Eisenstein-Dirac delta at e^{2 pi i/3}, it cannot be the case that all zeros
  rho_j of zeta(s) give eigenvalues for S~_theta." **Remark 71.** "Unless we believe that a subset of on-the-line
  zeros of zeta(s) is naturally distinguished, this might suggest that the discrete spectrum is empty."
- **Theorems 72, 73** (operator S~_{Theta,theta} on pseudo-cuspforms killed by theta; a adjusted by Baire so that
  theta E_{s_j} != 0 at the Lax-Phillips parameters): its eigen-parameters on the line are the solutions of
  cos(tau log a + psi(tau)) J_{theta,w} = sin(tau log a + psi(tau)) theta E_{1-w} theta E_w/(-4 tau), and
  **there is exactly one** between two consecutive zeros of cos(tau log a + psi(tau)) (these eigenvalues EXIST; they
  are not zeros of zeta_k).
- Prop. 74, **Corollary 75**: for all large tau, R'(tau log tau) <= ((log a + psi'(tau))/(log tau + 1))(R^2 + 1),
  R(tau log tau) := 2 tau J(1/2 + i tau)/(theta E theta E) ("the graph of t -> R(t log t) has slope essentially
  bounded above by the slope of t -> tan(t log t)").
- **Corollary 76** (unconditional, under a configuration hypothesis): if 1/2 + it, 1/2 + it' are adjacent on-line
  zeros of theta E_w, neither a zero of J, and J_{theta, 1/2 + i tau} has a unique zero between them with positive
  tau-derivative, then |t' - t| >= (pi/log t)(1 + O(1/log log t)). **Corollary 77**: if instead J has no on-line
  zero between them but a pair of off-line zeros 1/2 +- eps + i tau_0 with eps very small, then
  |t' - t| >= (pi/(2 log t))(1 + O(1/log log t)). Remark 78: similar results for off-line zeros (not given).

## 3. Method in ten lines
1. Remove the cusp forms: on E^0 (constants + Eisenstein spectrum) the point evaluation at a CM point, summed over a
   class group, has spectral coefficient (sqrt|d|/2)^s zeta_k(s)/zeta(2s).
2. Mean-square bounds for zeta_k on the line and 1/zeta(1+it) = O(log t) put that functional in E^{-1/2-eps},
   hence in E^{-1}, the dual of the form domain; on all of L^2 it would only be in H^{-1-eps}.
3. A functional in the dual of the form domain, not in the space, gives a Friedrichs extension of the
   restriction to its kernel (Theorem 4): eigenvalue equation = inhomogeneous equation (-Delta - lambda)u = c theta
   plus the side condition theta u = 0.
4. Solving by division in the spectral variable (Theorem 10) forces E theta(w) = 0 at any eigenvalue > 1/4, and
   self-adjointness forces w on the line (Corollary 11).
5. The side condition theta u_{theta,w} = 0 is a scalar equation for the Stieltjes transform of |theta E_s|^2
   (the Krein-type function); on the line its imaginary part is -|theta E_w|^2/(4 Im w) (Cor. 47), so an eigenvalue
   needs both theta E_w = 0 and J_{theta,w} = 0 (Cor. 52).
6. Vector-valued meromorphic continuation (Gelfand-Pettis, Grothendieck) of the resolvent applied to theta
   (Thm 43): the continuation across the line differs from the reflected value by theta E_w theta E_{1-w}/(1-2w)
   (Cor. 46).
7. Adding the Lax-Phillips constraint eta_a and a 2x2 determinant gives a symmetric meromorphic G(w,a) built from
   zeta_k whose zeros are forced onto the line by self-adjointness (Thm 29, Cor 30).
8. Interleaving with the Lax-Phillips eigenvalues (truncated Eisenstein series, zeros of a^w + c_w a^{1-w}) via
   a monotone rank-one secular function (Cor. 59-62).
9. Regularity of arg xi(1+2it) on the 1-line makes the Lax-Phillips zeros asymptotically equally spaced
   (Cor. 64); moving a slides them (Thm 65); so eigen-parameters of S~_theta are >= (1-eps) pi/log T apart.
10. Montgomery's pair correlation has 11% of pairs closer than that, so (on RH + pair correlation) <= 94%.

## 4. What is assumed
- Unconditional: everything except Corollary 69, which assumes RH and Montgomery's pair-correlation conjecture.
- Inputs quoted: Hardy-Littlewood second moment, Landau's bound for 1/zeta(1+it), Titchmarsh (5.17.4) for
  arg zeta(1+it), Backlund's argument-principle count, Lax-Phillips (pp. 204-206), Goldfeld-Hoffstein (Thm 31,
  unused), Hejhal-Polya (for Cor. 53).
- Several steps are called "routine" without proof (Lemma 15, Lemma 17, Corollaries 64, 66, Theorem 67 "elementary
  from the above").
- Corollary 59 and Theorem 73 need theta(f_j) != 0 for every Lax-Phillips eigenfunction; for Theorem 73 this is
  arranged by a Baire-category perturbation of a; Corollary 59 uses it without comment.

## 5. Where its frontier is
- LAST THING PROVED: a self-adjoint operator S~_theta on the non-cuspidal space whose spectrum above 1/4 is
  CONTAINED in {s(1-s) : zeta_k(s) = 0, Re s = 1/2} (Cor. 11), with a condition that is necessary and sufficient for
  a given zero to give an eigenvalue (theta u_{theta,w} = 0, i.e. J_{theta,w} = 0 as well, Cor. 52); an
  unconditional minimal spacing (1-eps) pi/log T of its eigen-parameters (Cor. 68); and an unconditional
  look-alike G(w,a) (zeta_k data plus a cut-off a) with zeros only on the line and [0, 1] (Cor. 30).
- FIRST THING NOT PROVED: that S~_theta has ANY eigenvalue. Intro: "definitive proof of presence or absence of
  discrete spectrum for the operator S~_theta seems difficult. Apart from numerical tests, which suggest that there
  is no discrete spectrum ..." No zero of zeta_k is shown to be an eigenvalue; no mechanism making J_{theta,w}
  vanish at the zeros is given.
- Off the line: no statement places an off-line zero in the operator picture except by exclusion (Cor. 11, Cor.
  48: at an off-line zero with Re w < 1/2 the continued u_{theta,w} is an honest E^{1+eps} solution of the
  inhomogeneous equation with non-real lambda_w, but it is not an eigenfunction, since theta u != 0). The
  two-sheet identity (Cor. 46) is stated and section 6.7 records explicitly that the naive symmetry argument
  built on it fails exactly at the zeros.

## 6. What it explicitly does not do
- Abstract: "Unfortunately, there seems to be no reason for this discrete spectrum to be large."
- Intro: "this is not what one might hope: there is no assertion of existence of any eigenvalues of S~_k, and
  there is no assertion that every zero of zeta_k(s) gives an eigenvalue."
- Remark 12 (zero of E theta is sufficient for a solution but not for the side condition), Remark 13, Remark 71.
- No use of the Euler product or Hecke operators anywhere in sections 4-8: the arithmetic enters only through the
  Sobolev index of theta (section 3.6). Section 5.8 states that the considerations apply to any real combination of
  Eisenstein-Dirac deltas; a commented-out passage of the source (lines 4273-4277) says the flawed argument of 6.7
  "would also imply that Epstein zeta functions E_s(z_o) and all linear combinations of them have 100% of their
  zeros on the critical line" and breaks off at "These conclusions may well be true, but".

## 7. Facts in our own record that this paper corrects or shows to be already known
- **rh/NOTEBOOK.md N43, "WHERE THE OFF-LINE ZEROS ARE" (lead's two-sheet derivation): ALREADY KNOWN, in this
  paper.** Corollary 46 is exactly the identity "first-sheet value minus continued (second-sheet) value
  = theta E_w theta E_{1-w}/(1-2w)" for theta u_{theta,w}, which is the paper's Krein-type function (Stieltjes
  transform of |theta E_s|^2 plus the constant term, NO cusp-form atoms and NO logarithmic regularisation, because
  the cusp forms are removed); Corollary 47 is N43's "jump across the continuous spectrum is the density" on the line;
  Remark 14 is N43's "Herglotz, so no non-real zeros on the physical sheet" (called elementary). The consequence
  N43 draws (an off-line zero is a point where the two sheets agree) follows from Cor. 46 in one line. Section 6.7
  is the paper's own record that the symmetric reading of this identity would give 100% of zeros on the line and
  is "fundamentally flawed". N43's own label "Rule 2: this is a reformulation until the second sheet has a
  structure" agrees with what the paper does with it: nothing beyond the identity.
  This reader's algebra (from Theorems 29 and 43, not stated in the paper): with X(w) := theta E_{1-w} theta E_w/
  (2(1-2w)), theta u_{theta,w} = J + X and G(w,a) = J - r_a(w) X, r_a(w) = (a^w - c_w a^{1-w})/(a^w + c_w a^{1-w});
  for fixed Re w > 1/2, r_a(w) -> 1 as a -> inf, so G(w,a) tends pointwise to J - X, which is the value at w of
  the other sheet (= the continued theta u at 1 - w). Cor. 30 says G(., a) has zeros only on the line for every
  a above the Heegner heights. Nothing in the paper about uniformity in a or the limiting zero set.
- Added after reading Garrett's notes (`garrett-notes.md`): Garrett's Bristol 2018 notes, section 4, state that
  the resolvent E^0 -> E^0 does NOT continue past Re w = 1/2; it continues only as a map E^0 -> C^0. So N43's
  'second sheet' is the continuation of a scalar (C^0-valued) function, not of an operator on the Hilbert space.
  The point-interaction family at omega and its Krein function are Colin de Verdiere 1982 (`cdv-pseudo-laplaciens-1982.md`).
- **N43, "WHAT I RECALL AND MUST NOT TRUST"**: partly wrong.
  (i) "built self-adjoint operators whose discrete spectrum is lambda_s for zeros of E(omega, s) ON the line":
  the operator's discrete spectrum above 1/4 is CONTAINED in that set and may be EMPTY; the paper proves no zero is
  an eigenvalue, says numerics suggest none, and Remark 71 suggests the spectrum is empty. A zero is an eigenvalue
  iff ALSO J_{theta,w} = 0 (Cor. 52).
  (ii) "such an operator can carry only part of the zeros (a spacing obstruction, linked to pair correlation)":
  right in shape. The spacing obstruction (eigen-parameters >= (1-eps) pi/log T apart) is unconditional
  (Cor. 68); the proportion "at most 94% of the zeros of zeta(s)" assumes RH AND Montgomery's pair correlation
  (Cor. 69); it concerns zeros of zeta(s) (one factor of zeta_K), not all zeros of zeta_K.
  (iii) N43 does not say which space: it is NOT L^2 of the modular surface but its non-cuspidal part E^0 (constants
  kept). The pre-trace / positive-proportion paragraph of N43 is not in this paper (the paper uses the moment of
  zeta_k only to get the Sobolev index).
- **rh/specialists/operator/OBJECT_PHI.md section 3(a)** ("capacity zero ... the pseudo-Laplacian of the functional
  'value at omega' is Delta itself"): true on all of L^2 (the paper agrees: delta not in H^{-1}), but the repair
  list there misses the one this paper uses, which is Colin de Verdiere's own suggestion: restrict to the
  non-cuspidal spectrum, where the CM-point functional IS in E^{-1/2-eps} subset E^{-1} (by the second moment of
  zeta_k on the line), so "vanish at omega" is a closed condition on the form domain and S~_theta is a genuinely new
  self-adjoint operator. Section 3(b) ("an embedded eigenvalue needs BOTH Re Q_T = 0 and form factor = 0") agrees
  with Cor. 52 (J_{theta,w} = 0 and theta E_w = 0). Section 1(b)'s Q(s) - Q(1-s) = Phi(s)^2/(...) is Cor. 46 here
  (Eisenstein part only). Section 4's S = (Q(s) + Q(1-s))/2 corresponds to the paper's J_{theta,w}, which Theorem 43
  proves symmetric in w <-> 1-w and meromorphic. The IMPOSTOR remark of OBJECT_PHI (nothing uses that omega is
  special) agrees with section 5.8 and with the commented-out Epstein remark.
- **rh/HUNCHES.md F2, row W1** ("pseudo-Laplacians (Colin de Verdiere) give exactly the look-alike theorems; what
  is missing is the one whose boundary condition is 'vanish at omega'"): the operator with "vanish at omega" (on the
  non-cuspidal part) EXISTS: it is S~_theta of section 4.1 (built by these authors, 2020). What it gives: spectrum
  contained in the on-line zeros, existence of eigenvalues open, <= 94% under RH + pair correlation. The look-alike
  theorems (zeros of a^w + c_w a^{1-w}, Cor. 53; zeros of G(w,a), Cor. 30) are also here. **F1 (ii)** (zeros of

  paper's normalisation; F3's Phi(s) = E*(omega, s) is, up to the factor Lambda(2s), the paper's theta E_s for
  d = -3 (with the footnote's factor 3).
- **docs/KNOWLEDGE.md B7** (spectral circularity): this paper is an instance where the operator is defined without
  knowing the zeros and is proved self-adjoint; what cannot be proved is that its spectrum is non-empty. **B2**:
  section 5.8 and the commented passage show the authors know the construction is shared by Epstein zeta
  functions. **B1**: no Euler product used after section 3.6.

## 8. Reliability
- arXiv preprint, single version (2020); no journal reference on arXiv; refereeing status not checked. 80 pages.
- Internal inconsistencies seen by this reader (none affects the main statements as I read them):
  intro says theta in E^{-3/4-eps}, section 3.6 says E^{-1/2-eps}; Theorem 72 writes "lambda_w = w(1-w) < -1/4"
  for parameters on the line (should be > 1/4); the eigen-equation of section 8 has denominator -4 tau in 8.1 and
  2 tau in 8.2; Corollary 61 is stated "at most one" and proved "exactly one"; the proof of Theorem 23 writes
  "(C theta cap eta_a)" for the span; the table of contents repeats "2.1" and "3.7".
