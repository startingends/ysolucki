## 2. Main theorems (from the part read)
- **Theorem 1.1.** With ℋ_1 = functions analytic in |Im z| < 1/2+ε with sup_y ∫|f(x+iy)|(1+|x|)dx < ∞:
  "There exist two sequences of rapidly decaying and even entire functions U_n(z), n = 1, 2, …, and
  V_{ρ,j}(z), 0 ≤ j < m(ρ), with ρ ranging over the nontrivial zeros of ζ(s) with positive imaginary part,
  such that for every even function f in ℋ_1 and every z = x+iy in the strip |y| < 1/2 we have
  f(z) = Σ_n f̂(log n/4π) U_n(z) + lim_k Σ_{0<γ≤T_k} Σ_j f^{(j)}((ρ−1/2)/i) V_{ρ,j}(z)", with
  U_n^{(j)}((ρ−1/2)/i) = 0, Û_n(log n'/4π) = δ_{n,n'}, V^{(j')}_{ρ,j}((ρ'−1/2)/i) = δ, V̂_{ρ,j}(log n/4π) = 0.
  Unconditional: neither RH nor simplicity of zeros is assumed (multiplicities enter through derivatives).
- **Corollary 1.1.** (i) An even f ∈ ℋ_1 with f̂(log n/4π) = 0 for all n ≥ 1 and f^{(j)} = 0 at all zeros vanishes
  identically. (ii) An even f ∈ ℋ_1 divisible by ζ(1/2+is) is determined by the values f̂(log n/4π).
  "both Theorem 1.1 and the above corollary … break down if one removes any single point".
- **Theorem 2.1, 2.2 (not used later in the paper).** Any Fourier interpolation formula with nodes Λ,
  Λ* (Λ sparse) forces Dirichlet-series kernels E_±(x,z) that continue meromorphically with simple poles at x
  and on Λ and satisfy E_+ = −E_−; and dually.
- **§2.4 (Kulikov's density theorem, quoted).** N_Λ(T) + N_Λ*(W) ≥ 4WT − C log^{2+η}(4WT); for the zeros and
  the points ±log n/4π it gives N(T) ≥ (T/2π) log(T/2πe) − C log^{2+η}T, matching Riemann–von Mangoldt.
- **Theorem 3.1.** For φ analytic of moderate growth on the upper half-plane, k ≥ 0, ε = ±1, there is a unique
  2-periodic F of moderate growth, F = Σ_{n≥ν_ε} a_n e^{πinτ}, with
  F(τ) − ε(τ/i)^{−k}F(−1/τ) = φ(τ) − ε(τ/i)^{−k}φ(−1/τ); ν_− = ⌊(k+2)/4⌋, ν_+ = ⌊(k+4)/4⌋.
- **Proposition 3.1 (cited to Berndt–Knopp, Thm 6.3).** "dim M_k(Γ_θ, ε) = ν_ε(k)", and a form whose expansion
  starts at n ≥ ν_± vanishes identically.
- Propositions 3.2 to 3.4: the modular kernels 𝒦_k^±(τ,z), the contour-integral construction
  F_k^±(τ,φ) = ½∫_{−1}^{1}𝒦_k^±(τ,z)φ(z)dz, and decay of F_k^±(ix,s). The coefficients α^±_{n,k}(s) of
  F_k^±(τ,s) (φ = (τ/i)^{−s}) are entire of exponential type, and
  Γ_ℝ(s)α^±_{n,1/2}(s/2) = 2∫_0^∞ b_n^±(x)x^{s−1}dx links them to the Radchenko–Viazovska functions.

## 3. The method (as announced in the introduction; the proofs are in the unread part)
1. D(w,s) = Σ β_n(s) n^{−w/2}, β_n the Fourier coefficients of a modular integral for the theta group.
2. H(w,s) = ζ(s)/ζ(w)·D(w,s) satisfies H(1−w,s) = −H(w,s), H(w,1−s) = H(w,s).
3. Contour integration of f((w−1/2)/i)H(w, iz+1/2) over Re w = 1/2+ε gives the formula; residues at the zeros.
4. Replacing ζ by any F with a functional equation of the same Gamma type gives further bases ("the single
   function D(w,s) generates an abundance of Fourier interpolation bases").
5. Only the functional equation and the Dirichlet-series (additive) representation are used; the paper
   contrasts this with the Riemann–Weil formula, whose deduction "starts from the Euler product".

## 4. What is assumed
Nothing about zeros. f even, in ℋ_1 (strip slightly wider than 1/2, one integrable moment).

## 5. Where its frontier is (as far as read)
- Proves: the two-sided interpolation basis attached to ALL nontrivial zeros (wherever they are) and the
  nodes log n/4π; uniqueness (Cor. 1.1).
- States it cannot / does not: a classification of crystalline measures "would probably be very difficult to
  obtain"; whether Kulikov's bound plus a minimality assumption yields a general Riemann–von Mangoldt
  formula is posed as "interesting to see". The frontier of sections 4 to 7 is not recorded here (unread).

## 6. What it explicitly does not do (as far as read)
No positivity statement, no statement on the location of zeros; the formula holds equally if zeros are off
the line. §2 is "not explicitly use[d] … anywhere else in the paper".
