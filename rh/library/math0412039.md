## 1. What was read
**70,841 / 70,841 characters** (2,104 lines): abstract, sections 1-5, appendix, bibliography, every line, with
the Read tool. The 19 leading comment lines are a version history only.

Conventions: `E*(z,s) = pi^{-s} Gamma(s) (1/2) sum' y^s/|mz+n|^{2s}`, `E*(z,s) = E*(z,1-s)`, poles at s = 0, 1
(residues -1/2, 1/2). Fourier coefficients `a_n(y,s) = 2|n|^{s-1/2} sigma_{1-2s}(|n|) sqrt(y) K_{s-1/2}(2 pi |n| y)`
(n != 0), constant term `a_0(y,s) = zeta*(2s) y^s + zeta*(2-2s) y^{1-s}`, `zeta*(s) = pi^{-s/2} Gamma(s/2) zeta(s)`.
**The paper is about the constant term and about integrals of E over regions; it proves nothing about the zeros
of s -> E*(z,s) for a fixed point z.**

## 2. Main statements, with their numbers (all unconditional)
- **Theorem 1.1.** `Z_{2,Q}(s) := zeta*(2s)/(s-1) - zeta*(2-2s)/s` (Weng's rank-2 zeta of Q, = minus the integral
  of E* over the semistable region `D_ss = {|x| <= 1/2, 0 < y <= 1, |z| >= 1}`, eq. (113), (121)) has all zeros on
  Re s = 1/2. (Special case T = 1 of Thm 1.2, via Zagier's identity `int_F E*(z,s) dmu = 0` for 0 < Re s < 1.)
- **Theorem 1.2.** For each fixed T >= 1, `I(T,s) = int_{y>=T} E* dmu = -zeta*(2s) T^{s-1}/(s-1) + zeta*(2-2s) T^{-s}/s`
  has all zeros on Re s = 1/2. Text after it: "The hypothesis T >= 1 cannot be relaxed. It can be shown that the
  Riemann hypothesis fails to hold for all values 0 < T < 1 by an argument principle method as in Hejhal [p. 89]"
  (no proof given in this paper).
- **Theorem 1.3.** For each y >= 1, `a_0(y,s)` satisfies the MODIFIED RH (zeros on the line or real in (0,1)).
  With `y* := 4 pi e^{-gamma} = 7.055507...`: (1) for 1 <= y <= y* all zeros are on the line; (2) for y > y*
  exactly two zeros are off the line, real and simple, `rho_y, 1 - rho_y`, 1/2 < rho_y < 1; rho_y is
  non-decreasing in y and -> 1 as y -> infinity. (Footnote: heuristically sigma_y = 1 + O(1/log y).)
  At y = y* there is a multiple zero at s = 1/2 (text after Thm 1.3).
- **Theorem 2.1** (Polya/de Bruijn-type lemma). F entire of genus 0 or 1, real on R, F(s) = +-F(1-s), all zeros in
  |Re s - 1/2| < a. Then for real c >= a, `|F(s+c)/F(s-c)| > 1` on Re s > 1/2 and `< 1` on Re s < 1/2; hence
  `F(s+c) + e^{i theta} F(s-c)` has all zeros on Re s = 1/2 for every theta. Remark (2): only constant modulus of F
  on the line is really used; hypothesis (i') `F(s) = e^{i alpha} F(1-s)` suffices given a block-convergent
  Hadamard product (they say GL(n) principal L-functions have one, via [La04b] Thm 2.1(4)).
- **Lemma 3.1 (Hejhal, Prop. 5.3(f) of Hejhal 1990).** For y >= 1 all non-real zeros of a_0(y,s) are on the line;
  proof: the Maass-Selberg relation (402) for the truncated E*_T, T >= 1: if a_0(T,s) = 0 the right side vanishes,
  the left side is `(s - s bar)(1 - s - s bar) ||E*_T||^2`, non-zero unless Re s = 1/2 or s real.
- **Lemma 3.2.** For every y > 0, a_0(y, sigma) != 0 for real sigma outside (0,1) (positivity of E* for sigma > 1).
- **Lemma 3.3.** (a) d/dsigma of `F(y,sigma) = y^{2 sigma - 1} f(sigma)`, `f = ((1-sigma)/sigma) xi(2 sigma)/xi(2 sigma - 1)`,
  vanishes at sigma = 1/2 exactly for y = y*; (b) `-f'/f` is strictly increasing on (1/2,1) (uses all zeta zeros
  have |gamma| > 14).
- **Theorem 4.1.** (1) For T >= 1 the zeros of I(T,s) are simple, and
  `N(I(T,.);U) = N(xi(2s);U) + (2/pi)(log T) U + O(log U)`. (2) As T >= 1 increases, each zero with Im > 0 has
  strictly DECREASING imaginary part (argument of `xi(1+2it) T^{it}` turns faster). Stated without proof for a_0:
  `N(a_0(y,.),U) = N(xi(2s),U) + (4/pi) log y U + O(1)`, and the zero motion for a_0 "is slightly more complicated":
  a multiple zero at y = y*, two zeros migrate to the real axis.
- Tables 1, 2: first 15 zeros of I(T,s) and a_0(y,s) at T, y = 1 and y*. COMPUTED by the authors (method not stated).

## 3. The method in ten lines
Thm 1.2: write `H(T,s) = -xi(2s) T^{s-1} + xi(2s-1) T^{-s}`; Thm 2.1 applied to `F(s) = xi(2s - 1/2)`, a = c = 1/4,
gives `|xi(2s)/xi(2s-1)| > 1` on Re s > 1/2 (zero-by-zero comparison of rho with 1 - rho bar in the Hadamard
product); the factor `T^{2 sigma - 1} >= 1` only helps when T >= 1, so the two terms of H cannot cancel off the line.
Thm 1.3: Maass-Selberg gives "on the line or real"; real zeros are located by monotonicity of `-f'/f` on (1/2,1).
Simplicity (Thm 4.1) from Lagarias's "differenced L-functions" paper (Acta Arith. 120 (2005)): the phase
`theta(2t) + t log T` of `xi(1+2it)T^{it}` is strictly increasing. Everything is "a direct verification without
providing a mechanism" (their section 5). Note: no Euler product of zeta is used beyond the location of zeta's
zeros in 0 < Re s < 1 and |gamma| > 14; the inequality is a statement about xi(2s) vs xi(2s-1), i.e. about a
SHIFT by one full critical-strip width, which is why zeta's own RH is not needed.

## 4. What is assumed
Nothing beyond classical facts: Hadamard product of xi, zeros of zeta in the open strip with |gamma| > 14, the
Maass-Selberg relation (Hejhal 1983), Lagarias 2005 for simplicity.

## 5. Where its frontier is
Last thing proved: RH for I(T,s), T >= 1, with simple zeros moving monotonically; modified RH for a_0(y,s), y >= 1,
with the exact real-zero threshold y*. First thing not done: (i) RH for I(T,s), 0 < T < 1, is asserted to FAIL
(argument principle "as in Hejhal"), not proved here; (ii) simplicity and monotone motion for a_0 not proved
("Numerical and analytic estimates would be needed", sec. 4); (iii) the generalisation to
`H(y,s) = p(s) zeta*(s) y^s + p(1-s) zeta*(2-2s) y^{1-s}` (finitely many off-line zeros, uniformly bounded in
y >= 1) is announced as "We hope to treat this method elsewhere"; (iv) nothing on E*(z,s) at a point.

## 6. What it explicitly does not do (checked over the whole text)
- It does not claim RH for Epstein zeta functions: "the Riemann hypothesis fails in general for Epstein zeta
  functions; they generically have zeros off the critical line, and often have zeros with Re(s) > 1; see Arenstorf
  and Brewer [AB93]" (sec. 1).
- No conceptual explanation: sec. 5 lists as open "a conceptual explanation (if there is one) for the Riemann
  hypothesis property for Fourier coefficients", and "why must the extra condition y >= 1 be imposed, and what is
  the meaning of 'exceptional zeros'".
- No de Bruijn-Newman statement, no flow in z, no use of the Laplace equation in z beyond Zagier's identity.

## 8. Open questions the paper itself states
Conceptual reason for RH of Fourier coefficients; meaning of the y >= 1 condition and of exceptional zeros;
RH for `J_n(T,s) = int_{D_T} a_n(y,s) dmu` (reduced to zeros of `int_y^inf u^{-3/2} K_s(u) du` on the imaginary
axis); the functions `K_n(s) = int_F a_n(y,s) dmu` (K_0 = 0 by Zagier); vertical distribution of these zeros as y
varies (they expect "very smoothly spaced, with no GUE behavior").
