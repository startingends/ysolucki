## 2. Main statements, with their numbers
Proved:
- **Thm 2.1** (analytic continuation, theta integral), **Thm 2.3** (equation for critical zeros
  `-2/(1+4 rho_y^2) + int_0^1 cos(rho_y log t)[theta_3 theta_3 - 1/t] dt/sqrt t = 0`), **Thm 2.4** (two equations
  for off-critical zeros), **Thm 3.3** (an edge zero satisfies f(rho_y*, Delta*) = 0, the t-derivative equation).
  These are calculus on the theta integral.
- **Def. 3.1 / 3.2.** A critical "edge" zero: d rho_y/d Delta = +-infinity; left/right by the sign of
  d^2 Delta/d rho_y^2.
- **Lemmas 3.4-3.7, 4.1, 4.2** (Taylor expansions, formal): near a non-edge critical zero, `delta rho_y = -(a/f) delta Delta`
  and NO off-critical zero exists nearby (Lemma 4.1, last line: "if rho~ is not an edge zero, then there is no such
  off-critical zero in its neighborhood"); near a left edge zero, critical zeros `rho_y = rho_y* +- sqrt(-a/c) sqrt(Delta - Delta*)`
  for Delta > Delta*, and for Delta < Delta* a pair of off-critical zeros `delta rho_x = +- sqrt(-a/c) sqrt(Delta* - Delta)`
  (Lemma 4.2). That is: zeros leave the line only through a double zero, with square-root branching. The sign
  `-a/c > 0` is "checked numerically" for all left edge zeros of Table 1, not proved.
- **Thm 3.8** (Delta -> 0): `zeta^(2)(s,Delta) = Delta^{-2s} pi^{2s-1/2} Gamma(1/2-s) zeta(1-2s)/Gamma(s) +
  Delta^{-1} sqrt(pi) Gamma(s-1/2) zeta(2s-1)/Gamma(s) + O(Delta)` (= the constant term of E; error bound for
  Re s > 0 explicit). **Lemma 3.9:** in the limit the critical zeros are equidistant, `rho_y(n) = pi n/|log(Delta/pi)|`.
- **Lemma 4.3.** `Z(1/2, Delta) = 0` has a unique solution Delta_c* in (0,1] (Montgomery 1988 monotonicity of
  theta_3(e^{-pi t Delta}) theta_3(e^{-pi t/Delta}) on (0,1)).
Numerical (Mathematica, 8-20 digits; one zero ~5 s, one off-critical zero ~60 s):
- Critical zeros for 0 < Delta <= 1, rho_y <= 21 form closed/open curves in (Delta, rho_y); 24 edge zeros
  tabulated (Table 1: e.g. Delta* = 0.1417332..., rho_y* = 0 (edge 1); 2a 0.7984/9.1748; 2b 0.9507/13.5092; ...;
  14a 0.3097/20.1102).
- Every curve of off-critical zeros joins a right edge zero to a left edge zero ("as a rule"); none crosses
  Delta^2 in {1,2,3,4,7} (class-number-one-type factorisations), though "some of the edge zeros are localized very
  close to dashed lines".
- A pair of REAL off-critical zeros for every Delta in (0, Delta_c*] U [1/Delta_c*, inf), Delta_c* ~ 0.141733;
  rho_x -> 0 and 1 as Delta -> 0, rho_x ~ (3/pi) Delta.
- **Conjecture 1.1:** Delta_c* = e^gamma/(4 pi) exactly ("coincides ... by at least 22 decimal digits").

## 3. The method in ten lines
Write Z(s,Delta) as a Mellin integral of `theta_3 theta_3 - 1/t` on [0,1]; on the critical line the equation is
real; differentiate in Delta and rho_y to classify points where the implicit function theorem fails (edge zeros:
double zeros on the line); a second-order expansion with the antisymmetric pair `c(delta rho_y)^2 - c(delta rho_x)^2`
shows that when the real branch disappears it is replaced by a complex-conjugate pair off the line (fold
bifurcation). Then track numerically. For small Delta, Euler-Maclaurin reduces the function to its constant term.

## 4. What is assumed
The assumption that rho_x varies continuously along off-critical curves (sec. 1.2: "A discontinuous change of rho_x
was excluded from our analysis"); signs of a, c from numerics.

## 5. Where its frontier is
Rigorous: local fold structure at a double zero (formal expansions), small-Delta asymptotics, uniqueness of
Delta_c*. Not rigorous: global picture (every off-critical curve joins two edges), positivity of -a/c, the
conjecture on Delta_c*. Only z on the imaginary axis; height <= 21.

## 6. What it explicitly does not do
No statement for generic x (non-rectangular lattices); no counting of off-line zeros; no proof of the
conjecture; no use of Hecke operators or of the Laplace equation in z.

## 7. Facts in our own record that this paper corrects or shows to be already known
- **N45 (1)** "zeros leave the line only through COLLISIONS (double zeros)": KNOWN in this form (Lemma 4.1 and
  4.2, for the one-parameter family z = i/Delta; the general fact is the elementary one that a simple zero of a
  real-analytic real function on the line persists). The "two-parameter de Bruijn-Newman family" picture is
  observed numerically here along the imaginary axis: off-line pairs are born and die at edge (double) zeros, many
  times, at Delta^2 values that are not class-number-one points (e.g. edge 2a at Delta = 0.798, z ~ 1.25 i).
- **N45 (3)** "RH at a class-number-one CM point is a simultaneous re-collision of infinitely many zero pairs":
