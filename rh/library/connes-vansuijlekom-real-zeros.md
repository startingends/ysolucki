## 2. What it proves
Everything in the paper is unconditional. Nothing in it is a statement about the zeta function: the words
"Riemann", "Weil" occur only in the introduction and the bibliography; there is no explicit formula, no prime,
no prolate function and no numerical experiment on zeta in the paper.

Conventions of the paper (different from ours): the distribution D lives on [0, L]; test functions live on an
interval of length L ([0, L], or [-L/2, L/2] after translation); the Fourier transform is
`F(f)(s) = int f(x) exp(-isx) dx`. Our half-width L_ours (r1_A) corresponds to L_paper = 2 L_ours.

- **Corollary 1.1** (of Caratheodory-Fejer 1911). T a Hermitian positive semidefinite Toeplitz matrix of size
  n+1 and rank n, xi in ker T. Then "all the zeros of the polynomial P(z) = sum xi_j z^j lie on the unit
  circle". Reproved as **Proposition 2.2** by a C*-algebra argument (Proposition 2.1: the ideal generated by P
  in C[X, 1/X] is a *-ideal, and c_j defines a positive linear form on the quotient; GNS gives a unitary whose
  eigenvalues are the roots).
- **Remark 2.3.** When the kernel of T has dimension greater than one the statement fails for individual
  kernel vectors; "the correct formulation" is that the common zeros of all kernel vectors lie on the circle.
- **Theorem 3.1** (continuous kernel). h even, real, continuous on [-L, L]; K f(x) = int h(x-y) f(y) dy on
  L^2([0, L]). K is compact self-adjoint. "Assume that its eigenvalue of largest modulus is simple" with
  eigenvector xi; then "all the zeros of the entire function xi-hat belong to R".
- **Proposition 4.1** (matrix of the form). For a distribution D on [0, L] and the basis
  U_n(x) = L^(-1/2) exp(2 pi i n x / L), with psi(x) := (1/pi) int_0^L sin(2 pi x (1 - y/L)) D(y) dy, the matrix
  of Q is q_{m,n} = (psi(m) - psi(n))/(m - n) for m != n and q_{n,n} = psi'(n). (A divided-difference, i.e.
  Loewner, matrix.)
- **Proposition 4.2.** The truncations to indices -N..N are exactly the matrices q_ii = a_i,
  q_ij = (b_i - b_j)/(i - j) with a_{-i} = a_i, b_{-i} = -b_i real; conversely each such matrix comes from a
  unique real distribution with Fourier components supported in -N..N.
- **Remark 4.3.** The form must be defined from D on [0, L] through the formula `<f|g>_Q := D~(f* * g)` of section 4 (label `qua0` in the source), not
  from the even distribution on [-L, L]: delta'_0 on [0, L] gives a non-zero form but its symmetrisation is 0.
  (This is why the introduction calls Theorem 1.2 "slightly unprecise".)
- **Lemmas 5.1 to 5.3** (the mechanism). With D = diag(j), eta = sum e_j, beta = sum b_j e_j:
  DQ - QD = |beta><eta| - |eta><beta| (5.1). If Q >= 0, Q xi = 0, xi even, <xi|eta> = 1, then Q D xi = -beta and
  D' := D - |D xi><eta| is self-adjoint for the inner product defined by Q (5.2). For s not in {-N..N}:
  Det(D' - s) = 0 iff sum_j xi_j/(s - j) = 0 (5.3).
- **Theorem 5.6** (finite-dimensional case). "Let Q be a real symmetric positive matrix of the form [of Proposition 4.2] with
  one dimensional kernel which is even ... Let xi in ker Q." Then (i) the polynomial
  P(s) = sum_k xi_k prod_{j != k} (j - s) has only real roots; (ii) the Fourier transform of
  xi(x) = sum xi_k exp(2 pi i k x) on [0, 1] (zero outside) "is entire and has all its zeros on the real line".
- **Proposition 5.10.** The same for nodes lambda_j with lambda_{-j} = -lambda_j in place of the integers j
  (D any self-adjoint matrix with simple symmetric spectrum): the polynomial
  sum_k xi_k prod_{j != k} (lambda_j - s) has only real roots.
- **Theorem 6.1** (the main theorem; Theorem 1.2 is its announcement). "Let L > 0, and let D be a real
  distribution on the interval [0, L]. Let Q be the quadratic form defined on trigonometric polynomials by
  [the formula `qua0`]. Assume that Q defines a lower-bounded essentially selfadjoint operator and that the minimum of its
  spectrum is a simple, isolated eigenvalue lambda, with even eigenfunction xi" (even = invariant under
  x -> L - x). "Then all the zeros of the entire function xi-hat(z) ... lie on the real line."
  Hypotheses to note: (a) the sign of lambda plays no role (a multiple of delta_0 shifts the form);
  (b) simplicity is in the whole space, not in the even sector only; (c) the eigenvalue must be isolated;
  (d) trigonometric polynomials must be a core.
- **Section 7** (spectral action). Lemma 7.3: for f in a class E^s and bounded R,
  d^n/dt^n tr f(D + tR) at 0 = n! sum R_{i1 i2}...R_{in i1} f'[lambda_i1, ..., lambda_in] (divided
  differences; the perturbation need not be self-adjoint, Araki's expansional is used). Proposition 7.5: with
  f'(lambda_j) = b_j, f''(lambda_j) = a_j and R = -|D xi><eta|, the first and second derivatives of tr f(D + tR)
  both equal <D xi, D xi>_Q.
- **Appendix A.** D(x) = delta_0 + 2 pi b sin(2 pi x) on [0, 1]: the form is 2 + b mu with mu the operator of
  kernel 2 pi Sign(x-y) sin(2 pi (x-y)) (Prop. A.3). Fact A.4 (stated from numerics, "the numerical study ...
  indicates"): every finite truncation has simple extreme eigenvalues, but in the limit the maximum 8/3 and the
  minimum -8/5 (Prop. A.5) have multiplicity 2 (sin, cos of pi x and of 3 pi x). So simplicity at every finite
  stage does not pass to the limit.
- **Appendix B.** Direct verification of Theorem 5.6 for N = 1 (Proposition B.1, two proofs, one by convexity)
  and N = 2 (case analysis with computer-algebra reductions and plots).

## 3. Method in ten lines
1. Toeplitz case: a rank-n positive Toeplitz matrix gives a positive functional on C[X, 1/X]/(P); the GNS
   representation is n-dimensional and X acts by a unitary whose eigenvalues are the roots of P.
2. Continuous kernel: approximate h(x-y) by step functions so that K is a norm limit of Toeplitz forms on
   characteristic functions of equal intervals; the top eigenvector (simple) is a norm limit of vectors whose
   transforms are P(exp(-is alpha)) times an explicit factor; apply Hurwitz.
3. Distribution case: in the Fourier basis the form is a divided-difference matrix (Prop. 4.1), not Toeplitz.
4. Key lemma (5.2): the commutator [D, Q] has rank two, built from eta and beta; if xi spans ker Q and is even,
   then the rank-one perturbation D' = D - |D xi><eta| kills xi and is symmetric for the Q-inner product.
5. Hence D' induces a self-adjoint operator on the quotient Euclidean space, with real spectrum.
6. A rank-one determinant computation (5.3) identifies the characteristic polynomial of D' with
   s times the numerator of sum_j xi_j/(s - j); so that function has only real zeros.
7. Shannon sampling (Prop. 5.5): the transform of a trigonometric polynomial on an interval is
   sin(Ls/2) sum xi_n/(Ls/2 - n pi); so its zeros are real.
8. Infinite dimension (6.1): the gap delta above the simple lowest eigenvalue gives, for N large, a simple
   lowest eigenvalue of the truncation with an even eigenvector within eps + sqrt(eps/delta) of xi.
9. L^2 convergence on a compact interval gives locally uniform convergence of the transforms; Hurwitz.
10. The work is in step 4 (finding D') and in step 8 (where "isolated" and "simple" are used).

## 4. Where it stops
- The theorem is conditional on its spectral hypothesis and the paper does not verify that hypothesis for any
  arithmetic example. Its only sentence on the matter (introduction, about the Toeplitz matrices of number
  theory): "The key difficulty in this context, then, becomes the verification that zero is indeed the
  (simple) minimal eigenvalue of T." The motivation is stated as "potential relevance to the Riemann
  Hypothesis itself"; nothing more is claimed.
- Simplicity cannot be dropped (Remark 2.3) and does not survive limits automatically (Appendix A).
- The proof is by finite truncation plus Hurwitz. The route sketched in section 4 ("one would like to obtain a
  positive linear form on the quotient of the convolution algebra of functions on R by the ideal generated by
  eta") is explicitly not carried out in infinite dimension. In particular the paper gives no representation
  of the form as a positive measure carried by the zeros of xi-hat (no continuous Caratheodory-Fejer
  decomposition) outside the Toeplitz case.
- The method ends at: real zeros of the transform of ONE eigenfunction of ONE window. There is no statement on
  how the eigenfunction or its zeros depend on L, no limit L -> infinity, no convergence of xi-hat to anything,
  no relation between the zeros of xi-hat and any given set of numbers, and no odd-eigenfunction case (a
  commented-out passage in the source starts the case where only the even part of the kernel is
  one-dimensional and is abandoned).
- Remark 7.4 leaves open the extension of the spectral-action calculus to non-self-adjoint D.

## 5. What it changes in our record
- **KNOWLEDGE section 7, entry for this paper** ("first ~40k of 118k verbatim, rest by summary"): the statement
  recorded there is Theorem 6.1 and is correct. To be made exact: the distribution is on [0, L] and the form is
  built by the formula `qua0` of section 4 (Remark 4.3); "simple" means simple in the whole L^2 space; the operator must be
  essentially self-adjoint on trigonometric polynomials. The entry's "the paper does not state what is missing
  for RH" is confirmed after reading the whole text; the one relevant sentence is quoted in part 4. "No
  derivative in the window size, no control example": confirmed, there is neither. The file is 105 055 bytes,
  not 118k.
- **r1_A PROGRESS, "T2 [cited ... read only in abstract]"** and **IDEAS #20 (new input field)**: the citation
  is Theorem 6.1; statement as used there is accurate.
- **r1_A PROGRESS, "Structural identity found on the way"** and **IDEAS #2 log, "A5 windowed Pick/Loewner
  identity"** (the lattice form Q(e_j, e_k) = divided difference of a function T, diagonal = derivative,
  "generic to every truncated convolution form"): the lattice case is **Proposition 4.1** of this paper, with
  the explicit generating function psi, and Proposition 4.2 adds the converse (every such matrix arises).
  The factor (-1)^(j-k) in our formula is the translation from [0, L] to the centred interval (their
  Proposition 5.5(i)). The off-lattice version proved by our critic (arbitrary real frequencies w, w') is not
  in the paper; the paper has divided-difference matrices at arbitrary nodes lambda_j only as an abstract
  class (the form q_ij = (b_i - b_j)/(lambda_i - lambda_j) of section 4.3, Proposition 5.10), not as the matrix of the window form in a non-lattice basis.
- **r1_A PROGRESS "Zeros of the transform of the minimiser (reproduces the Connes et al. phenomenon)"** and
  the Davenport-Heilbronn addendum, also **IDEAS #2 log** ("below its first-failure window the impostor's
  minimiser reproduces the impostor's on-line zeros ... so that phenomenon does not see the Euler product"):
  the REALITY of those zeros is what Theorem 6.1 gives, for any real distribution, with no arithmetic
  hypothesis, whenever the lowest eigenvalue is simple, isolated, with even eigenfunction. So "all zeros of
  fhat_min real" for zeta at L = 1 and for the impostor at L = 1.6 are instances of the theorem (our runs
  report the simple even ground state in those cases); that the theorem cannot distinguish zeta from the
  impostor is visible in its hypotheses. The paper does NOT contain: any computation of such zeros for zeta,
  any statement that they approximate zeta zeros, the height (about 4 pi e^(2L)) up to which they do, or the
  impostor experiment. Those parts of our record are not in this paper. (Whether they are in Connes,
  arXiv 2602.04022, or Connes-Consani-Moscovici, arXiv 2511.22755, both named in our record, was not checked:
  those papers were not on this seat's list.)
- **r1_A "Extremal-measure weights (partial)"** (fit of Q - mu as a sum of point evaluations at the zeros with
  positive weights; "the finite-N problem is not exactly of Caratheodory-Fejer type"): consistent with the
  paper, which uses the Caratheodory-Fejer decomposition only for Toeplitz matrices and replaces it, for the
  divided-difference matrices, by the Q-self-adjoint operator D' whose eigenvalues are the zeros. A positive
  measure on the zeros for the window form is not stated in the paper.
- **ATLAS F1** ("supplies: exact real-rootedness at every finite stage, for free", listing "minimisers of
  truncated Weil forms"): for minimisers the real-rootedness is Theorem 6.1 and is not free; it holds under the
  simple-isolated-even hypothesis, which the paper names as the difficulty.

## 6. Reliability
- arXiv preprint, v1. The acknowledgements thank "an anonymous referee", so a refereed version was in
  preparation; no journal reference is given on arXiv. Authors are established; the main proof (sections 5, 6)
  is short and self-contained and was read line by line here, not re-derived independently.
- Two typographical slips noticed, harmless: in Lemma 5.3's proof "R = -<D xi><eta|"; in Lemma 7.3's proof the
  divided difference is written f[...] where the statement has f'[...].
- Fact A.4 is numerical ("indicates"), and Propositions B.1 and the N = 2 case rest partly on plots and
  computer-algebra case reductions.
- Not verified by this reader: the claim in Lemma 7.2 that the estimates of van Nuland-van Suijlekom carry over
  line by line.
