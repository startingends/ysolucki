## 2. Main theorems (all unconditional; none is a statement about zeros of zeta except Prop. 3.6)
Section 2, cyclic pairs `(D, xi)`: `D` self-adjoint, `xi` a unit cyclic vector in the domain of all `D^n`.
- **Theorem 2.1.** A cyclic pair is unitarily multiplication by `s` on `L^2(R, d mu)` with `xi = 1`, `mu` the
  spectral measure; the subspaces `E_n = span{D^j xi, j <= n}` become polynomials of degree `<= n`.
- **Definition 2.2.** "The formal prolate operator omega(D, xi, lambda) is the operator
  -D^2 + lambda^2 N", `N` the number operator of the orthogonal polynomials. "This definition is formal
  inasmuch as it does not give precisely the domain of the operator."
- **Propositions 2.1 to 2.4.** The pair is even (has a grading `gamma`, `gamma D = -D gamma`, `gamma xi = xi`) iff
  `mu` is symmetric; then `gamma = exp(i pi N)`. `(c_0(N), H, D)` is a spectral triple; its spectral distance
  on `N` is equivalent to `|phi(n) - phi(m)|`, `phi(n) = sum_{j<=n} 1/a_j` (`a_j` the Jacobi coefficients of
  `D`). The formal prolate operator splits into two Jacobi matrices `J^±` with explicit entries in the `a_n`.

Section 3, the archimedean place:
- **Theorem 3.1.** With `h_n` the normalised Hermite functions: (i) `U(h_0) = 2^{-3/4} pi^{-1/2}
  L_inf(1/2 - is)`; (ii) `U(h_{2n})(s) = (-1)^n P_n(s) U(h_0)(s)` with `P_n` polynomials of the parity of `n`,
  orthonormal for the probability measure `dm(s) = (2 pi)^{-3/2} |Gamma(1/4 + is/2)|^2 ds`; (iii), (iv) the
  Hardy-Titchmarsh transform `V = M o U : L^2(R)^ev -> L^2(R, dm)` (`M` = division by `U(h_0)`) is unitary,
  sends `h_{2n}` to `(-1)^n P_n` and the scaling operator to multiplication by `s`; (v)
  "(M o U) o W_lambda o (M o U)^* = -s^2 + 2 pi lambda^2 (4N + 1) - 1/4".
- **Propositions 3.1 to 3.5.** `(S, h_0)` is an even cyclic pair with grading the Fourier transform; `N =
  H/(8 pi) - 1/4`, `H = -d^2 + (2 pi q)^2`; multiplication by `s` in the basis `P_n` is the Jacobi matrix with
  zero diagonal and off-diagonal `(i/2) sqrt((2n+1)(2n+2))`; the spectral metric is equivalent to the
  invariant metric of `R_+^*` restricted to the positive integers; explicit Jacobi matrices of `W_lambda^±`.
- **Lemma 3.3, Proposition 3.6.** For the combinations `psi_l^±` of Hermite functions with `f(0) = fhat(0) = 0`:
  "F_mu(E(psi_l^±))(s) = R_l^±(s) Xi(s)" with explicit polynomials `R_l^±`; and "The spectrum of the operator of
  multiplication by s in the quotient of H_{<=1} [entire functions of order <= 1] by the closure of the
  subspace spanned by the F_mu(E(psi_l^±)) is the set of s in C such that 1/2 + is is a non-trivial zero of
  zeta." (`E(f)(u) = u^{1/2} sum_{n>=1} f(nu)`; proof by reference to Connes-Marcolli, Prop. 2.24.)

Section 4, the semilocal case (`X_S = A_S/Gamma`, `Gamma = {± prod p_j^{n_j}}`, `K_S` the kernel of the module):
- **Proposition 4.1 (with 4.2 = "Theorem 1").** For `f in L^2(R)^ev` let `eta_S(f)` be the class of
  `1_{R_S} (x) f`; then `w_S(eta_S(f))(u) = E_S(f)(u) := u^{1/2} sum_{gamma in Gamma_+ cap Z} f(gamma u)` and
  "F_mu(E_S(f))(s) = (prod_{p in S\{inf}} L_p(1/2 - is)) (F_mu w_inf f)(s)"; `eta_S` maps functions supported in
  `|x| <= lambda` to functions supported in `|x|_S <= lambda`, and intertwines the Fourier transforms.
- **Proposition 4.2.** `V_S := M_S o U_S : L^2(X_S)^{K_S} -> L^2(R, dm_S)`,
  `dm_S(s) = |prod_{v in S} L_v(1/2 - is)|^2 ds`, is the canonical form of the cyclic pair `(S, xi_S)`,
  `xi_S = eta_S(h_0)`; the pair is even, with grading the semilocal Fourier transform.
- **Proposition 4.3.** The identity map `L^2(R, dm) -> L^2(R, dm_S)` is bounded with bounded inverse and
  corresponds to `eta_S` (not unitary).
- **Theorem 4.1.** The semilocal Hermite operator `N_S` (number operator of `(S, xi_S)`) is the harmonic
  oscillator for `S = {inf}`; its eigenfunctions are `eta_S(P_n^S(x) e^{-pi x^2})` with polynomials `P_n^S`
  "obtained by orthonormalization and induction"; the scaling operator is a Jacobi matrix in that basis.
  The paper then writes the "candidate" `W_{lambda,S} = (H + 1/2)^2 + lambda^2 N_S`.
- **Definitions 4.4, 4.5; Proposition 4.5.** Sonin space `S_lambda(K, alpha)` = functions `f` with `f` and its
  Fourier transform vanishing for `|x| < lambda`. For `Q_p`: the `Z_p^*`-invariant part of `S_1(Q_p, e_p)` "is one
  dimensional, with generator sigma_p := eps_0 - (1/p) eps_1" (`eps_n` the indicator of `|x| = p^n`), and
  `sigma_p` is its own Fourier transform.
- **Propositions 4.6, 4.7.** `theta_S(f)` := class of `(tensor of the sigma_p) (x) f` maps the archimedean Sonin
  space into the semilocal one, and `F_mu(w_S(theta_S(f)))(s) = F_mu(w_inf(f))(s) prod_{p}(1 - p^{-1/2 - is})`;
  `<theta_S(f) | eta_S(g)> = <f | g>`.
- **Theorem 4.6 (= "Theorem 2").** "Let S containing inf be a finite set of places and lambda > 0. Then the map
  theta_S is a hilbertian isomorphism of the Sonin spaces theta_S : S_lambda(R, e_inf) -> S_lambda(X_S, alpha)".
  ("hilbertian": isomorphism of topological vector spaces, not isometric.)
- **Proposition 4.8.** The dual Hardy-Titchmarsh transform `upsilon_S = (prod_{v in S} L_v(1/2 + is)) . U_S`
  identifies every `S_lambda(X_S, alpha)` with one space `B_lambda` of entire functions (de Branges's Sonine
  space); with the norm of `L^2(R, ds/|E_S(s)|^2)`, `E_S(s) = prod_{p in S} L_p(1/2 + is)`, it is a de Branges
  space `B_lambda^S` for every `S`.

Section 5, metaplectic representation:
- **Proposition 5.1.** Formally `W_lambda = varpi(h^2 + 4 pi lambda^2 k - 1/4)` in the metaplectic representation.
- **Theorem 5.2.** Requiring `sigma(h) = -iA`, `sigma(k) = N + c` to define a representation of `sl(2,R)` for a
  Jacobi matrix `A` with zero diagonal forces `c = 1/4` and `a_n = (1/2) sqrt((2n+1)(2n+2))`; the moment problem
  is determinate; the measure is proportional to `|Gamma(1/4 + is/2)|^2 ds`; `sigma` is the even part of the
  metaplectic representation.
- **Proposition 5.2.** Moments: `sum c_n (ix)^n/n! = sqrt(2/(e^x + e^{-x}))` (numerators 1, 7, 139, 5473, ..:
  OEIS A126156).

## 3. Method in ten lines
1. Put any self-adjoint operator with a cyclic vector in canonical form (spectral theorem): orthogonal
   polynomials, Jacobi matrix, number operator `N`.
2. Archimedean case: the Mellin transform on the critical line turns scaling into multiplication by `s`, the
   Gaussian into the local factor `L_inf(1/2 - is)`, and (because `Gamma(x+1) = x Gamma(x)`) even Hermite
   functions into polynomials times that factor.
3. Hence the classical prolate operator is `-S^2` plus a multiple of the number operator: a formula that makes
   sense for every cyclic pair.
4. Semilocal case: summing `f` over the positive `S`-units that are integers multiplies the Mellin transform
   by the Euler factors of the primes in `S`; the Gaussian becomes the product of local factors; the spectral
   measure becomes `|prod_{v in S} L_v(1/2 - is)|^2 ds`.
5. That gives a semilocal number operator and so a candidate semilocal prolate operator (formal).
6. Sonin spaces: at a finite prime the invariant Sonin space is one-dimensional, spanned by a self-dual
   `sigma_p`; tensoring with the `sigma_p` multiplies the Mellin transform by the inverse Euler factors
   `prod (1 - p^{-1/2 - is})`.
7. The two maps (`eta_S`: Euler factors; `theta_S`: inverse Euler factors) are dual to each other for the
   `L^2` pairing; each is bounded with bounded inverse because finitely many Euler factors are bounded above
   and below on the line.
8. Duality plus the support properties of `eta_S` give that `theta_S` is onto the semilocal Sonin space.
9. After multiplying by all local factors, every semilocal Sonin space is the same space of entire functions
   with an `S`-dependent norm.
10. Lie-algebra rigidity (Thm 5.2): the archimedean Jacobi coefficients are the only ones for which the Jacobi
    matrix and the number operator generate `sl(2,R)`.

## 4. What is assumed
- Nothing conditional. No use of RH.
- Results imported without proof: the semilocal trace formula framework (Connes 1999; Connes-Marcolli book,
  Prop. 2.30 for `w_S`, Prop. 2.24 for the argument of Prop. 3.6); de Branges's theorem that the archimedean
  Sonine space is a Hilbert space of entire functions; Connes-Moscovici (PNAS 2022) for the statement that the
  Sonin space corresponds, "up to a finite dimensional possible discrepancy", to the negative spectrum of the
  prolate operator on the whole line.
- All operator formulas for the prolate operator are "formal": "ignoring the delicate domain definition needed
  to obtain a selfadjoint operator".

## 5. Where its frontier is
**Last things proved.** (i) The canonical form of the scaling operator with the semilocal ground state, with
spectral measure the squared modulus of the product of the local factors of `S` on the critical line
(Prop. 4.2). (ii) Independence of the semilocal Sonin space from `S` as a topological vector space, and its
realisation as one space of entire functions with `S`-dependent de Branges norms (Thm 4.6, Prop. 4.8).

**First things it cannot do.**
- The semilocal prolate operator exists only as a formal expression; no domain, no self-adjointness, no
  spectrum. Of the classical case the paper says "finding the relevant selfadjoint extension is delicate".
- "The computation of the coefficients of the hermitian Jacobi matrix of the cyclic pair for a general S ... is
  deferred to a forthcoming paper." The polynomials `P_n^S` are not computed for any `S` other than `{inf}`.
- The candidate is not unique: Remark 4.2 gives "another guess" (multiplication by `|x|_S^2` plus its Fourier
  conjugate) and shows the two guesses differ; a "second potential candidate" from the Weil representation of
  `SL_2(A_S)` is announced for a forthcoming paper.
- Nothing is proved about Weil positivity. The relevant sentences are expectations: "We expect that the use of
  such operator-theoretic tools in the semilocal case opens a way to handle Weil's positivity as in [Connes-
  Consani, archimedean place]"; the paper "provides a more precise strategy for addressing the semilocal Weil
  positivity by comparing the trace functional associated to the operator - which is automatically positive
  for a selfadjoint operator - with the Weil functional". No such comparison is carried out.
- No limit over `S` (all primes) is considered. Boundedness of `eta_S` and `theta_S` rests on `S` being finite.
- Proposition 3.6(ii) is a spectral realisation of ALL non-trivial zeros as the spectrum of multiplication by
  `s` on a quotient of entire functions by (the closure of) the polynomial multiples of `Xi`; it carries no
  information on where the zeros are.

## 6. What it explicitly does not do (checked over the whole text)
- No windowed Weil form, no eigenvalue of it, no positivity statement, no numerical experiment.
- No statement that any operation in the paper preserves reality of zeros.
- No result on zeros of zeta beyond Prop. 3.6. The "infrared" and "ultraviolet" matches with zeros are quoted
  from earlier papers (Connes-Consani 2023; Connes-Moscovici 2022), not reproved.
- The Sonin space is described for every `S`, but its relation to a semilocal operator's negative spectrum is
  stated only as a constraint "in the search of the semilocal analogue of the prolate operator".
- Burnol's structure function for the archimedean Sonine space appears only in a block commented out with
  `\iffalse`; no Painleve-type statement occurs anywhere in the source.

  (Connes-Consani 2021; Connes-Consani-Moscovici 2022)"): per this paper's own account of the literature, Weil

  2022 paper is Connes-Moscovici (PNAS), on the negative spectrum of the prolate operator and the ultraviolet
  behaviour of the zeros; it is not a positivity result. This paper (2024) contains no positivity theorem.
- **docs/IDEAS.md #20 and rh/experiments/r1_A (novelty fields listing arXiv 2310.18423, "abstracts only")**: now

- **docs/IDEAS.md #2 log, "A1 prolate law" (mechanism)**: this paper restates the mechanism of Connes-Consani
  in one sentence: "When applied to eigenfunctions of the prolate operator for positive eigenvalues, [the map
  E] generates extremely small eigenvalues of the Weil quadratic form restricted to functions with fixed
  compact support", the eigenfunctions being "reconstructed ... by Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization". No rate.
- **docs/IDEAS.md #7 ("adjoin p" one prime at a time; novelty UNSEARCHED)**: an exact "adjoin the primes of S"

  on the critical line by `prod_{p in S} L_p(1/2 - is)` (Prop. 4.1), `theta_S` by the inverse factors (Prop.
  4.6), both bounded with bounded inverse for finite `S`, and the Sonin space is stable (Thm 4.6). The paper
  makes no claim about real zeros being preserved by either map and does not take `S` to infinity.
- **rh/NOTEBOOK.md N19, N22 and docs/KNOWLEDGE.md section 7 ("Burnol's work on Sonine spaces / Painleve ...
