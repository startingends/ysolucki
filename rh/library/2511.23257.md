Conventions: a distribution `D` on `[0, L]` = continuous linear form on `C^inf([0,L])`. Hilbert space
`L^2([0,L])` with basis `U_n(x) = L^{-1/2} exp(2 pi i n x/L)`. The form (label `qua0` in the source, section 4):
`<f|g>_Q := D~(f^* * g) = int_0^L ((f^* * g)(x) + (f^* * g)(-x)) D(x) dx`, `f^*(x) = conj f(-x)`.
Fourier transform `F(f)(s) = int f(x) e^{-isx} dx`. "Even" = invariant under `x -> L - x`. In the project's
notation the window has half-width `a = L/2` and the distribution lives on `[0, 2a]`.

## 2. Main theorems (all unconditional; nothing in the paper is a statement about zeta)
- **Corollary 1.1** (of Caratheodory-Fejer 1911). "Let T in M_{n+1}(C) be a Hermitian, positive semidefinite
  Toeplitz matrix of rank n, and let xi in ker T. Then all the zeros of the polynomial P(z) := sum xi_j z^j lie
  on the unit circle." Reproved as **Proposition 2.2** from **Proposition 2.1** (for real `T`: the ideal `J`
  generated by `P` in `C[X, 1/X]` is a *-ideal, `X^0..X^{n-1}` is a basis of the quotient, and
  `phi(X^j) = c_j` is a positive linear form on it).
- **Remark 2.3.** With a kernel of dimension greater than one the statement fails for individual kernel
  vectors; "the correct formulation" is about the common zeros of all of them.
- **Theorem 3.1.** "Let h in C([-L,L]) be an even real continuous function. Let K be the operator on L^2([0,L])
  given by (Kf)(x) = int h(x-y) f(y) dy. Then K is a compact selfadjoint operator. Assume that its eigenvalue
  of largest modulus is simple and let xi be its eigenvector. If we extend xi ... to be zero outside [0,L]
  then all the zeros of the entire function xi-hat belong to R."
- **Proposition 4.1.** With `psi(x) := (1/pi) int_0^L sin(2 pi x(1 - y/L)) D(y) dy`, the matrix of `Q` in the
  basis `U_n` is `q_{m,n} = (psi(m) - psi(n))/(m - n)` for `m != n`, `q_{n,n} = psi'(n)`.
- **Proposition 4.2.** The truncations to `{-N..N}` are exactly the matrices `q_ii = a_i`,
  `q_ij = (b_i - b_j)/(i - j)`, with `a_{-i} = a_i`, `b_{-i} = -b_i` real; each such matrix comes from a unique
  real distribution with Fourier components in `{-N..N}`. (Off-diagonal entries see only the imaginary parts of
  the Fourier coefficients; diagonal entries involve all Fourier components of `D`.)
- **Remark 4.3.** The form must be built from `D` on `[0,L]`, not from an even distribution on `[-L,L]`:
  `delta'_0` on `[0,L]` gives a non-zero form although its symmetrisation is 0. (This is why the introduction
  calls Theorem 1.2 "slightly unprecise".)
- **Lemmas 5.1 to 5.3.** `D = diag(j)`, `eta = sum e_j`, `beta = sum b_j e_j`, `gamma e_j = e_{-j}`.
  (5.1) `DQ - QD = |beta><eta| - |eta><beta|`. (5.2) If `Q >= 0`, `Q xi = 0`, `gamma xi = xi`, `<xi|eta> = 1`:
  `Q D xi = -beta` and `D' := D - |D xi><eta|` is self-adjoint for the inner product of `Q`.
  (5.3) `Det(D' - s) = -s prod_i (i - s) sum_j xi_j/(j - s)`.
- **Proposition 5.5** (Shannon sampling). For `f` on `[0,L]` shifted to `[-L/2, L/2]`:
  `F(f^sigma)(s) = sin(Ls/2) sum_n fhat(n)/(Ls/2 - n pi)`.
- **Theorem 5.6.** "Let Q be a real symmetric positive matrix of the form [of Prop. 4.2] with one dimensional
  kernel which is even with respect to gamma. Let xi in ker Q. (i) All the roots of the following polynomial
  are real: P(s) = sum_k xi_k prod_{j != k} (j - s). (ii) The Fourier transform xi-hat(z) of the function
  xi(x) := sum xi_k exp(2 pi i k x) on [0,1], 0 outside, is entire and has all its zeros on the real line."
- **Proposition 5.10.** Same with any simple symmetric spectrum `lambda_{-j} = -lambda_j` in place of the
  integers: `sum_k xi_k prod_{j != k} (lambda_j - s)` has only real roots.
- **Theorem 6.1** (the main theorem; Theorem 1.2 announces it). "Let L > 0, and let D be a real distribution on
  the interval [0, L]. Let Q be the quadratic form defined on trigonometric polynomials by [qua0]. Assume that Q
  defines a lower-bounded essentially selfadjoint operator and that the minimum of its spectrum is a simple,
  isolated eigenvalue lambda, with even eigenfunction xi. Then all the zeros of the entire function xi-hat(z),
  z in C, the Fourier transform of xi lie on the real line."
- **Section 7.** Lemma 7.3: for `f` in a class `E^s` and bounded (not necessarily self-adjoint) `R`,
  `d^n/dt^n tr f(D + tR)|_0 = n! sum R_{i1 i2}..R_{in i1} f'[lambda_i1, .., lambda_in]` (divided differences;
  the exponential is defined by Araki's expansional). Proposition 7.5: with `f'(lambda_j) = b_j`,
  `f''(lambda_j) = a_j`, `R = -|D xi><eta|`: the first and second derivatives of `tr f(D + tR)` at 0 both equal
  `<D xi, D xi>_Q`.
- **Appendix A.** `D(x) = delta_0 + 2 pi b sin(2 pi x)` on `[0,1]`: `Q = 2 + b mu`, `mu` the operator with kernel
  `2 pi Sign(x-y) sin(2 pi (x-y))` (Prop. A.3; part (ii): in general the Schwartz kernel is `D(|x-y|)`). Fact A.4
  (from numerics: "the numerical study ... indicates"): each finite truncation has simple extreme eigenvalues,
  but for the limit operator the maximum 8/3 and the minimum -8/5 (Prop. A.5) each have multiplicity 2
  (`sin, cos` of `pi x`, resp. `3 pi x`).
- **Appendix B.** Theorem 5.6 checked by hand for `N = 1` (Prop. B.1, two proofs) and `N = 2` (case analysis
  with computer-algebra reductions and plots; includes the case of an odd kernel vector).

## 3. Method in ten lines
1. Toeplitz: a rank-`n` positive Toeplitz matrix gives a positive functional on `C[X,1/X]/(P)`; in the GNS
   representation (dimension `n`) `X` is unitary and its eigenvalues are the roots of `P`.
2. Continuous kernel: approximate `h(x-y)` uniformly by step kernels that are Toeplitz on characteristic
   functions of equal intervals; the rank-one spectral projection of the top eigenvalue is a norm limit; the
   transform of each approximant is `P(e^{-is alpha})` times an explicit factor; Hurwitz.
3. Distributions: in the Fourier basis the form is a divided-difference (Loewner-type) matrix (Prop. 4.1),
   the shape of the second variation of the spectral action.
4. The commutator `[D, Q]` has rank two, made of `eta` (even) and `beta` (odd) (Lemma 5.1).
5. If the kernel of `Q >= 0` is one-dimensional and spanned by an even `xi`, the rank-one perturbation
   `D' = D - |D xi><eta|` kills `xi` and is symmetric for `<.,.>_Q` (Lemma 5.2).
6. So `D'` descends to a self-adjoint operator on the Euclidean quotient: real spectrum.
7. Rank-one determinant formula (5.3) identifies the characteristic polynomial of `D'` with `s` times the
   numerator of `sum_j xi_j/(s - j)`.
8. Shannon sampling (5.5): the transform of a trigonometric polynomial cut to an interval is `sin` times that
   same partial-fraction sum; hence real zeros (Thm 5.6).
9. Infinite dimension (6.1): add a multiple of `delta_0` so that `lambda = 0`; the gap `delta` above the simple
   bottom eigenvalue gives, for large `N`, a simple bottom eigenvalue of the truncation with an even
   eigenvector within `eps + sqrt(eps/delta)` of `xi`; `L^2` convergence on a compact interval gives locally
   uniform convergence of transforms; Hurwitz.
10. The work is in step 5 and in step 9, where "simple", "isolated", "even" and "trigonometric polynomials are a
    core" are each used once.

## 4. What is assumed
- In Theorem 6.1: (a) `D` real, on `[0,L]`; (b) the form on trigonometric polynomials of period `L` is lower
  bounded and "essentially selfadjoint" (used as: trigonometric polynomials are a core of the operator `A`);
  (c) the bottom of the spectrum is an eigenvalue that is simple in the whole space (not only in the even
  sector) and isolated; (d) its eigenfunction is even.
- NOT assumed: any sign of `lambda`. A multiple of `delta_0` shifts the form by a multiple of the identity
  (section 4), so the theorem applies equally when the form is not positive.
- No arithmetic input of any kind. The words "Riemann Hypothesis" occur in the introduction only.

## 5. Where its frontier is
**Last thing proved.** For one window and one real distribution: if the bottom eigenvalue is simple, isolated,
with even eigenfunction, the transform of that eigenfunction has only real zeros (Thm 6.1).

**First thing it cannot do.**
- Verify its own hypothesis in any case of arithmetic origin. The only sentence on this (introduction, about
  Toeplitz matrices arising in number theory): "The key difficulty in this context, then, becomes the
  verification that zero is indeed the (simple) minimal eigenvalue of T." The motivation is "its potential
  relevance to the Riemann Hypothesis itself"; no further claim is made, and the paper applies Theorem 6.1 to
  no example other than the toy distribution of Appendix A.
- Carry simplicity through a limit: Appendix A is a counterexample to "simple at every finite stage implies
  simple in the limit".
- Give the structural statement behind the Toeplitz case. In section 4: "one would like to obtain a positive
  linear form on the quotient of the convolution algebra of functions on R by the ideal generated by eta ...
  Instead of working directly with this infinite dimensional situation, our strategy is" truncation plus
  Hurwitz. So outside the Toeplitz case there is no decomposition of the form as a positive measure carried by
  the zeros of the transform.
- Say anything about where the real zeros are, how they move with `L`, or what `xi-hat` converges to.

## 6. What it explicitly does not do (checked over the whole text)
- No explicit formula, no prime, no prolate function, no computation for zeta or any L-function.
- No dependence on the window size `L`: no derivative, no limit `L -> infinity`.
- No treatment of an odd ground state or of a degenerate bottom eigenvalue in infinite dimension (the passage
  that starts it is commented out in the source).
- No control example of arithmetic type; the hypotheses do not distinguish distributions with or without an
  Euler product.
- Remark 7.4 leaves open the spectral action for non-self-adjoint `D`.
- Lemma 7.2 is proved by reference ("follows line-by-line the proof of" van Nuland-van Suijlekom, Thm 6).

## 8. Open questions the paper itself states
- Introduction: verifying "that zero is indeed the (simple) minimal eigenvalue" in the number-theoretic
  Toeplitz setting is "the key difficulty".
- Section 4: the direct infinite-dimensional construction (a positive form on a quotient of the convolution
  algebra by the ideal generated by the eigenvector) is described as what "one would like to obtain" and is not
  carried out.
- Remark 7.4: "It would be interesting to extend the above definition of the spectral action for not
  necessarily self-adjoint perturbations to the case where also the assumption on self-adjointness on the
  operator D is relaxed."
