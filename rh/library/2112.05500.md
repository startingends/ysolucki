Notation of the paper. `W_lambda xi = -d/dx((lambda^2 - x^2) d xi/dx) + (2 pi lambda)^2 x^2 xi` on `L^2(R)`;
Fourier transform `F f(y) = int f(x) e^{-2 pi i x y} dx`; `P_lambda` = multiplication by the indicator of
`[-lambda, lambda]`, `Phat_lambda = F P_lambda F^{-1}`. Sonin's space is used without a displayed definition:
functions vanishing on `[-lambda, lambda]` whose Fourier transform also vanishes there (in the text: "the
orthogonal of the eigenspaces of W_sa associated to the classical prolate functions and their Fourier
transforms"; semiclassically "p^2 - lambda^2 >= 0 and q^2 - lambda^2 >= 0"). Here `lambda` is the half-length
of the interval on the additive line of `L^2(R)`, with the two special values `1` and `sqrt 2`; it is NOT the
window parameter of a Weil form, and no Weil form appears in the paper. The symbol `chi(n)` in section 7.2 is
an eigenvalue of the differential operator `W_sa` on `[-lambda, lambda]`, not the eigenvalue `chi` of the
band-limiting (Fourier compression) operator used in arXiv 2511.22755 and in our record.

## 2. Main statements, with their numbers
### Proved in the text (operator theory; no zeta function enters)
- **Lemma 2.1.** "The deficiency indices of W_min are (4, 4)." (`W_min` = closure of `W` on Schwartz space.)
- **Lemma 2.2.** For `xi in Dom W_max` and `a = ±lambda`, `p(x) d xi/dx` is continuous near `a` and
  `xi -> (p xi')(a)` is a non-zero continuous linear form on `Dom W_max` vanishing on `Dom W_min`.
- **Lemma 2.3.** "If xi in Dom W_min then P_lambda xi in Dom W_max and W P_lambda xi = P_lambda W xi. The same
  holds with respect to Phat_lambda."
- **Lemma 2.4, Lemma 2.5.** Fourier transforms of `(1/2) log((lambda^2 - x^2)^-2)` (equal to
  `cos(2 pi lambda y)/|y|` away from 0) and of the indicator of `[-lambda, lambda]`; a basis
  `{alpha_±, beta_±, alphahat_±, betahat_±}` of `Dom W_max / Dom W_min` in each parity.
- **Definition** (unnumbered): `W_sa` is `W_max` restricted to the boundary conditions
  `lim_{x -> ±lambda} (lambda^2 - x^2) xi'(x) = 0` (16) and two oscillatory conditions at infinity ((17): for the
  even part, `x sin(2 pi lambda x) xi' - (2 pi lambda x cos(2 pi lambda x) - sin(2 pi lambda x)) xi -> 0`; (18): for
  the odd part, `x cos(2 pi lambda x) xi' + (2 pi lambda x sin(2 pi lambda x) + cos(2 pi lambda x)) xi -> 0`).
  Equations are numbered (1) to (32) through the paper.
- **Theorem 2.6.** "(i) W_sa is selfadjoint and commutes with the Fourier transform. (ii) W_sa commutes with
  the projections P_lambda and Phat_lambda. (iii) W_sa is the only selfadjoint extension of W_min commuting
  with P_lambda and Phat_lambda. (iv) The spectrum of W_sa is discrete and unbounded on both sides."
  ((iv): all four end points `±lambda`, `±inf` are limit-circle; `±lambda` non-oscillatory, `±inf` oscillatory.)
- **Corollary 2.7.** An eigenfunction of `W_sa` is regular on each side of `lambda` "with a possible
  discontinuity at lambda", and at infinity its leading term is proportional to `sin(2 pi lambda x)/x` (even
  case) or `cos(2 pi lambda x)/x` (odd case).
- **Lemma 3.1** (`lambda = 1`, even functions "for simplicity"). "For any mu in R the asymptotic expansion of the
  unique solution xi_mu which at infinity is asymptotically ~ -sin(2 pi x)/(pi x) is Borel summable and is equal
  to the Fourier transform of the unique even solution phi_mu which is zero on [-1, 1] and agrees with f_mu(x)
  for x > 1." (`f_mu` the solution regular at `x = 1`; uses Richard-Jung, Ramis, Thomann, Fauvet 2017,
  Prop. 14.)
- **Corollary 3.2.** "With the above notation, assume mu is a negative eigenvalue. Then phi_mu belongs to the
  Sonin space." Followed by: "we do not claim (although this is supported by numerical evidence) that all
  eigenvalues of the restriction of W_sa to Sonin's space are negative, however there could be only finitely
  many exceptions."
- **Lemma 4.1.** `I(a) := int_1^inf (sqrt(a + y^2 - 1)/sqrt(y^2 - 1) - 1) dy = a K(1 - a) - E(1 - a) + 1`
  (complete elliptic integrals), and `I_lambda(a) = lambda^2 I(a lambda^-4)`.
- **Lemma 5.1, Lemma 5.2, Proposition 5.3** (Darboux factorisation on `[lambda, inf)`). With
  `p = x^2 - lambda^2`, `L = d(p d) + 4 pi^2 lambda^2 x^2` (this is `W` there), `nabla = p^{1/2} d/dx`,
  `U xi = p^{1/4} xi`: for every solution `w` of the Riccati equation
  `sqrt(x^2 - lambda^2) w' + w^2 = -4 pi^2 lambda^2 x^2 - (1/4) x^2/(x^2 - lambda^2) + 1/2`,
  `L = U^*(nabla + w)(nabla - w)U`; the solutions are `w_z`, `z in C \ R`, built from `u = u_1 + z u_2` (`u_j` real
  solutions of `Lu = 0`); and for `Dslash = [[0, nabla + w], [nabla - w, 0]]`,
  `U^* Dslash^2 U = diag(L, L + 2 nabla w)`.

### Stated as a semiclassical approximation
- **Proposition 4.2.** "The semiclassical approximation to the number of negative eigenvalues xi of W_sa with
  -xi <= E^2 on even functions is the same as on odd functions and is equal to 2 sigma(E, lambda) where
  sigma(E, lambda) ~ (E/2 pi)(log(E/2 pi) - 1 + log 4 - 2 log lambda) + lambda^2 + o(1)."
  The "proof" computes the area of the phase-space region
  `{q >= lambda, p >= lambda, (p^2 - lambda^2)(q^2 - lambda^2) <= (E/2 pi)^2 + lambda^4}` for the classical
  Hamiltonian `H_lambda = (p^2 - lambda^2)(q^2 - lambda^2)`, "via the formal relation
  W_lambda ~ -4 pi^2 H_lambda + 4 pi^2 lambda^4".
- **Theorem 6.1** (`lambda = sqrt 2`). "The operator 2 Dslash has discrete simple spectrum contained in
  R u iR. Its imaginary eigenvalues are symmetric under complex conjugation and the counting function N(E)
  counting those of positive imaginary part less than E fulfills
  N(E) ~ (E/2 pi)(log(E/2 pi) - 1) + O(1)." Its proof takes the count from Proposition 4.2
  (`2 sigma(E/2, sqrt 2)`). (This reader: no statement in the paper proves that the true eigenvalue count
  differs from the semiclassical area by `O(1)`; the counting part of Theorem 6.1 is therefore at the level of
  Proposition 4.2.)

### Numerical observations
- Three figures "The spectrum (in blue) compared to zeros of zeta (red)": the first 30 and the first 100
  eigenvalues of `2 Dslash` (`lambda = sqrt 2`) against the first zeros (heights to about 240). The two point
  sets are visually close and not identical at low index. No table, no numbers, no stated precision or method.
- The introduction says the "coincidence holds for two values lambda = 1 and lambda = sqrt 2"; the body treats
  `lambda = sqrt 2` (Theorem 6.1); `lambda = 1` appears only as the simplifying choice of section 3.

### Speculative remarks (section 7, announced as such)
- A two-dimensional Lorentzian metric `ds^2 = -alpha dt^2 + dx^2/alpha`, `alpha = -4(x^2 - 2)`, "a black hole in
  two space-time dimensions with horizon at x = ±sqrt 2".
- The positive eigenvalues of `W_sa` on even functions of `[-lambda, lambda]` satisfy
  `chi(n) = (2n + 1/2)^2 + O(1)` (Osipov-Rokhlin-Xiao, Thm 3.11), "the same as that of the squares of the trivial
  zeros of the Riemann zeta function with the same shift of 1/2".
- "Spectral truncation" (compression to Sonin's space) to remove the real eigenvalues of `2 Dslash`.

## 3. The method in ten lines
1. The prolate differential operator `W_lambda` commutes with the Fourier transform and, formally, with
   cutting to `[-lambda, lambda]`; the classical theory uses it only inside the interval.
2. Treat it on the whole line: two interior singular points `±lambda`, limit-circle everywhere, deficiency
   indices (4, 4).
3. Describe `Dom W_max / Dom W_min` by eight explicit boundary functionals (four per parity), exchanged in
   pairs by the Fourier transform.
4. The extension that keeps `(lambda^2 - x^2) xi' -> 0` at `±lambda` and its Fourier image at infinity is
   self-adjoint, Fourier-invariant, and the only one commuting with `P_lambda` and `Phat_lambda`.
5. Sturm-Liouville theory with an oscillatory limit-circle end point gives discrete spectrum unbounded in
   both directions on the complement of the interval.
6. An eigenfunction living outside `[-1, 1]` has a divergent expansion at infinity whose Borel sum is the
   Fourier transform of that eigenfunction (resurgence, from Richard-Jung et al.).
7. Since `W_sa` commutes with `P` and `Phat` and the inside spectrum is positive, a negative-eigenvalue
   eigenfunction is orthogonal to the ranges of both: it lies in Sonin's space.
8. Count negative eigenvalues semiclassically: phase-space area of `{(p^2 - lambda^2)(q^2 - lambda^2) <= ...}`
   in the Sonin quadrant, an elliptic integral with expansion `(E/2 pi)(log(E/2 pi) - 1 + log 4 - 2 log lambda)`.
9. Take a first-order "square root" by the Darboux process (Riccati equation, a family indexed by
   `z in C \ R`); at `lambda = sqrt 2` and with the factor 2, the count becomes Riemann's main term.
10. The work is in steps 2 to 5 (the self-adjoint extension). The link to zeta is step 8 (a main term of a
    counting function) and three pictures.

## 4. What is assumed
- Sections 2, 3, 5: nothing beyond cited results on the prolate equation (Richard-Jung-Ramis-Thomann-Fauvet
  2017; Naimark, Weidmann, Zettl for limit-circle theory; Slepian-Pollak for oscillation at infinity). Several
  proofs are sketches ("One checks ...", "By straightforward calculation ...", "The proof is straightforward").
- Proposition 4.2 and the counting statement of Theorem 6.1: the semiclassical correspondence
  `q -> x`, `p -> (1/2 pi i) d/dx`, used formally.
- Corollary 3.2 is written for `lambda = 1` and even functions.
- No hypothesis on zeta; no property of zeta is used anywhere except the Riemann-von Mangoldt main term as an
  object of comparison.

## 5. Where its frontier is
**Last things proved.** `W_sa` exists, is unique with the commutation property, has discrete spectrum unbounded
on both sides; eigenfunctions of negative eigenvalues lie in Sonin's space; the semiclassical count of negative
eigenvalues has the shape of the Riemann-von Mangoldt main term for a suitable `lambda` and normalisation.

**First things it cannot do.**
- Any statement about individual zeros: the match is of the leading terms of two counting functions
  ("ultraviolet behavior") plus pictures. Nothing says an eigenvalue is near a zero.
- "we do not claim ... that all eigenvalues of the restriction of W_sa to Sonin's space are negative".
- The operator `2 Dslash` "would define a spectral triple if 2 Dslash were self-adjoint (or skew adjoint) but
  its spectrum contains both real and imaginary pieces".
- The trivial zeros: "To obtain a convincing relation one would need to analyze the extension of 2 Dslash to
  (two copies of) the even functions on R as well as the conditioning of the Hilbert space needed to
  eliminate the positive square roots of the chi(n)."
- Primes: the abstract and introduction describe the result as having "spectrally captured the contribution of
  the archimedean place to the mysterious zeta spectrum". No prime, Euler factor or explicit formula appears in
  the body.

## 6. What it explicitly does not do (checked over the whole text)
- **No positivity statement of any kind.** Weil positivity is mentioned only through the citation of
  Connes-Consani (Selecta 2021, arXiv 2006.13771): "In [that paper] the compression of f(S) to Sonin's space
  (for lambda = 1) was shown to be the root of Weil's positivity at the archimedean place on test functions with
  support in the interval [2^{-1/2}, 2^{1/2}], but since Sonin's space is not preserved by scaling, one could not
  restrict scaling to this space. It turns out that W_lambda commutes with the orthogonal projection on Sonin's
  space."
- No Weil quadratic form, no window, no test functions, no lowest eigenvalue of a form, no inequality between
  a Weil form and any form built from prolate functions or the Sonin projection.
- No eigenvalue of the band-limiting operator `Phat P Phat`, no asymptotics of `1 - chi`.
- No error term for the eigenvalue counting function; no proof of a Weyl law for `W_sa`.
- No table of eigenvalues; no statement of how the eigenvalues in the figures were computed or which Riccati
  solution was used (the spectrum of `Dslash^2` does not depend on it by Proposition 5.3).
- No relation, proved or conjectured, between the eigenfunctions of `W_sa` in Sonin's space and zeros of zeta.
- Loose ends noticed by this reader (they do not affect the statements above): three figures share one label;
  the boundary functionals in the display before (13) are all written with `alpha_+`, `beta_+`, `betahat_-` on
  the right for both signs; in the proof of Lemma 2.4 (ii) the formula "F f(y) = 2 pi i y F f(y)" has lost a derivative; Theorem
  2.6 (ii) is proved for the vectors `beta_±` only (the case of `betahat_±` and of `Phat_lambda` is left to the
  Fourier symmetry).

## 8. Open questions the paper itself states
- Whether all eigenvalues of the restriction of `W_sa` to Sonin's space are negative ("supported by numerical
  evidence"; "there could be only finitely many exceptions").
- The extension of `2 Dslash` to (two copies of) the even functions on `R` and "the conditioning of the Hilbert
  space needed to eliminate the positive square roots of the chi(n)", in order to relate the positive spectrum
  to the trivial zeros.
- Whether spectral truncation to Sonin's space yields a spectral triple without the real eigenvalues
  (section 7.3, stated as something "one can" do, not done).
- The geometric meaning of Theorem 6.1 (the two-dimensional black-hole metric), offered as a speculation.
