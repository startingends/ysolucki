## 2. Main statements
Proved (unconditional):
- **Proposition 2.1.** "Let lambda > 1. The following formula defines a lower bounded lower semi-continuous
  quadratic form QW_lambda : L^2([1/lambda, lambda], d^*u) -> (-inf, +inf]:
  QW_lambda(f,f) := int |fhat(t)|^2 (2 theta'(t)/2 pi) dt + 2 Re(fhat(i/2) conj fhat(-i/2))
  - sum_{1<n<=lambda^2} Lambda(n) <f | V(n) f>", with `theta` the Riemann-Siegel angle function and
  `<f|V(n)g> = n^{-1/2}((f^* * g)(n) + (f^* * g)(1/n))`. In the proof: every term except the first is a bounded
  form on `L^2`, so the domain is that of the archimedean term `Q_inf`.
- **Lemma 2.2.** With `U(u) := u^{i pi/log lambda}`, "the space of Laurent polynomials C[U, 1/U] is a core for
  the quadratic form QW_lambda." (The proof uses `theta'(t) = (1/2)(log|t| - log 2 - log pi) - 1/(48 t^2) + ..`
  and states that the form norm of `Q_inf` is equivalent to `int |fhat(s)|^2 (1 + log(1+s^2)) ds`; on the way it
  approximates in that norm by smooth functions supported in the open interval.)
- **Proposition 2.3 / Corollary 2.4.** `QW_lambda(f) = liminf QW_lambda(g_n)` over Laurent polynomials `g_n -> f`;
  "The lower bound of QW_lambda is the limit, when N -> inf, of the smallest eigenvalue of the restriction of
  QW_lambda to the linear span E_N of the functions U^k for |k| <= N."
- **Lemmas 2.5 to 2.7.** In the real basis `xi_n` (cosines for `n >= 0`, sines for `n < 0`) the matrix splits,
  `sigma = sigma^+ (+) sigma^-` (even and odd under `u -> 1/u`); closed formulas for the entries: the pole term
  is rank one in each block (Lemma 2.7); the primes give a finite sum over `1 < m <= e^L`; the archimedean
  term is one integral plus `(theta_sym(0)/2)(gamma + log(4 pi (e^L - 1)/(e^L + 1)))`.
- **Proposition 4.1.** `D(lambda,k) := (1 - Pi(lambda,k)) D_0 (1 - Pi(lambda,k))`, `D_0 = -i u d/du` with periodic
  boundary conditions, with multiplication by periodic functions, is a spectral triple (finite-rank
  perturbation; Kato-Rellich). `Pi(lambda,k)` (Definition 3.1) is the projection on the span of `k`
  orthonormalised "prolate vectors".
- **Proposition 4.2.** For `E = 2 pi mu` the number of non-zero eigenvalues of `D(lambda,k)` in `(0, E]`
  "fulfills N'(E) ~ <N(E)>" (`<N(E)> = (E/2 pi) log(E/2 pi) - E/2 pi`; the proof is a count "up to a log mu
  term").
- **Lemma 5.1, Proposition 5.2.** For self-adjoint matrices and projections `P_1 <= P_2`:
  `mu_n(D_{P_2}) <= mu_n(D_{P_1})`; hence `lambda_n(D(lambda,k+1)) <= lambda_n(D(lambda,k))`.
- **Lemmas 6.1, 6.2.** For `f` of bounded variation, rapid decay, `O(u^2)` at 0 and integral 0:
  `E(f)(u) := u^{1/2} sum_{n>0} f(nu)` is `O(u^{1/2})` at 0 and of rapid decay; `Sigma_mu g(u) := sum_k g(mu^k u)`
  converges geometrically on `E(f)`; equivariance under scaling.
- **Definition 6.1.** "A zeta-cycle is a circle C of length L = log mu such that the subspace
  Sigma_mu E(S_0^ev) is not dense in the Hilbert space L^2(C)." (`S_0^ev`: even real Schwartz functions with
  `f(0) = 0 = int f`.) **Proposition 6.3**: the `n`-fold cover of a zeta-cycle is a zeta-cycle.
- **Theorem 6.4** (= Theorem 1.1, "our main result"). "(i) Let C be a zeta-cycle. Then the spectrum of the
  action of the multiplicative group R_+^* on the orthogonal complement of Sigma_mu E(S_0^ev) in L^2(C) is formed
  by imaginary parts of zeros of zeta on the critical line. Conversely: (ii) Let s > 0 be such that
  zeta(1/2 + is) = 0, then any real circle C of length an integral multiple of 2 pi/s is a zeta cycle and its
  spectrum ... contains is."
- **Corollary 6.5.** `H(L) := (Sigma_mu E(S_0^ev))^perp != {0}` iff there are `s in Z` (imaginary part of a
  critical zero) and `n in Z` with `sL = 2 pi n`.
- Key identity in the proof: `int E(f)(u) u^{-iz} d^*u = zeta(1/2 - iz) int f(u) u^{1/2 - iz} d^*u` (6.x, label
  `mapEzeta`), valid for real `z` because `f in S_0^ev` kills the pole.

Reported numerically (no proof, no error bars, no interval arithmetic):
- Section 2.2: the archimedean term alone gives a positive even block for `L in [0, log 2]` (smallest
  eigenvalue about 0.00133 at `L = log 2`) and changes sign for `L` slightly beyond `log 2`; adding the prime 2
  restores positivity on `log 2 <= L < log 3`.
- Section 2.3: at `L = log 3`, replacing 2 by a real `p` in `W_p(F) = p^{-1/2} log p (theta(log p) +
  theta(-log p))`: the smallest eigenvalue "is negative for p = 1.9999 and also for p = 2.0005". Also: the
  smallest eigenvalue of `sigma^+` at `mu = 3` is `< 6 x 10^-8`.
- Section 2.4: past each prime power 3, 4, 5, 7 (even and odd blocks), the form without the new term becomes
  negative and the term of that prime power restores positivity (figures, up to `mu` about 7).
- Section 2.5: the smallest eigenvalue `s(L)` of the even block shows "an exponential behavior" in
  `mu = exp L`; the number of small eigenvalues of `sigma^+` "increases roughly like mu"; `sigma^-` has "one
  less small eigenvalue". One value: "when lambda^2 = 11 the smallest positive eigenvalue is 2.389 x 10^-48".
- Section 3: eigenvectors of the small eigenvalues coincide graphically with the orthonormalised prolate
  vectors `epsilon_n` built from `E(phi_n)`, `phi_n` combinations of prolate spheroidal functions
  `PS_{2m,0}(2 pi lambda^2, x/lambda)` vanishing at 0 (agreement for the `m`-th even eigenvector from `mu > m`).
- Sections 4, 5: tables of the low eigenvalues of `D(lambda,k)` against the zeros for `mu = 5.5 .. 10.5`
  (mean absolute error 0.64 to 0.40; normalised RMS deviation 3.8% falling to 1.0%); at special `mu` the
  dependence on `k` disappears and the common value matches a zero; with the criterion "eigenvector of
  `D(lambda,k)` is an eigenvector of `D_0`", for `mu in [5, 16.5]`, the first 31 zeros are reproduced
  (probability of chance agreement "of the order of 10^-50").

## 3. Method in ten lines
1. Write the windowed Weil form through the explicit formula: finitely many primes `<= lambda^2`, a rank-two
   pole term, an archimedean term with symbol `2 theta'(t)/(2 pi) ~ (1/(2 pi)) log|t|`.
2. Only the archimedean term is unbounded; it is lower bounded and closed, so the form is; exponentials
   `U^k` are a core (rescale inward, mollify, truncate the Fourier series).
3. Compute the matrix in a real basis; parity splits it; diagonalise large truncations numerically.
4. Observation: a ladder of extremely small positive eigenvalues, about `2 lambda^2` of them.
5. Explanation offered: the range of `E(f)(x) = x^{1/2} sum f(nx)` lies in the radical of the global form (its
   transform is `zeta(1/2 - iz)` times something). `E(f)` is supported in `(0, lambda]` if `f` is supported in
   `[-lambda, lambda]`, and in `[1/lambda, inf)` if `fhat` is (Poisson: `E(fhat)(x) = E(f)(1/x)`).
6. Both at once is impossible, but prolate functions are nearly both (Slepian-Pollak: about `2 lambda^2`
   eigenvalues of the angle operator of the two cut-off projections are extremely close to 1). So
   `E(prolate combination with f(0) = fhat(0) = 0)`, cut to the window, is nearly in the radical.
7. Use the same prolate vectors, with no reference to the Weil form, to perturb the Dirac operator of the
   circle of length `L` so that they lie in its kernel; compute its low spectrum.
8. Numerical finding: at special lengths the perturbed and unperturbed operators share an eigenvector, and the
   eigenvalue is a zeta zero; the special `mu` form geometric progressions of ratio `exp(2 pi/zeta_n)`.
9. Proof of the clean statement behind step 8 (Thm 6.4): periodise `E(f)` over `mu^Z`; a character `u^{is}` of
   the circle is orthogonal to all `Sigma_mu E(f)` iff `zeta(1/2 + is) int f(u) u^{1/2+is} d^*u = 0` for all `f`,
   iff `zeta(1/2 + is) = 0`; and the character lives on the circle iff `mu^{is} = 1`.
10. The work is in the numerics (sections 2 to 5); the proofs (2.1 to 2.4, 6.4) are short.

## 4. What is assumed
- Nothing for the proved statements. RH is not assumed anywhere.
- The paper recalls without proof: "the positivity of the Weil quadratic form QW_lambda for all lambda implies
  the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), and in case RH holds, QW_lambda is known to be strictly positive" (argument for
  strictness in section 3: a transform of a compactly supported function has `O(r)` zeros in `|z| <= r`).
- Numerics: finite truncations `|n| <= N` ("significant evidence that the increasing of large N does not alter
  substantially the lower part of the spectrum"); precision and `N` are not stated.

## 5. Where its frontier is
**Last thing proved.** Theorem 6.4 and Corollary 6.5: the lengths `L` at which the periodised image of `E` fails
to be dense are exactly the integer multiples of `2 pi/s`, `s` the imaginary part of a zero ON the critical
line, and the scaling spectrum of the orthogonal complement consists of such `s`.

**First things it cannot do.**
- Positivity of `QW_lambda` is not proved for any `lambda`. About earlier work the paper says only: "In
  [Yoshida], the positivity was shown to hold for lambda = sqrt 2 using numerical analysis." Its own evidence is
  numerical and stops near `mu = 11` (Weil form) and `mu = 16.5` (Dirac operators).
- The small eigenvalues, their number, their exponential decay and the identification of their eigenvectors
  with prolate vectors are observed, explained heuristically (steps 5, 6), and not proved; no rate is stated.
- The link between the spectrum of `D(lambda,k)` and the zeros is not proved. Theorem 6.4 concerns the
  subspace `Sigma_mu E(S_0^ev)`, not the finite-dimensional range of `Pi(lambda,k)`; the paper calls the theorem
  "the theoretical explanation" of the coincidences. For general `mu` it says "one cannot expect that they
  reproduce exactly the n-th zero".
- Zeros off the critical line: Theorem 6.4 is about unitary characters, so it sees critical zeros only and
  gives no information on whether other zeros exist.
- Multiplicities: deferred ("taking care, in particular, of eventual multiplicity. We shall discuss this fact
  in details in a forthcoming paper").

## 6. What it explicitly does not do (checked over the whole text)
- No theorem of positivity on any window; no certified bound on any eigenvalue.
- No statement on how the smallest eigenvalue depends on `L` beyond "exponential behavior" in `mu` (plots).
- No zeros of the Fourier transform of an eigenvector of `QW_lambda` are computed or mentioned anywhere.
- No control example (no other L-function, no function without Euler product). The only variation of the
  arithmetic is section 2.3 (moving `p` near 2) and section 2.4 (omitting one prime power).
- No limit `lambda -> infinity` of the forms or of the operators `D(lambda,k)`.
- The unpublished appendix left in the source computes the transforms of Hermite analogues of the prolate
  combinations: `Gamma`-factor times polynomials `P_l`, orthogonal for the measure
  `|Gamma(1/4 - i sqrt(x)/2)|^2 d sqrt(x)`. It is not part of the published paper.

## 8. Open questions the paper itself states
- The "forthcoming paper": the cohomology of the quotient sheaf of `L^2(C)` by the subsheaf generated by
  `Sigma_mu E(S_0)` over the Scaling Site, as "the spectral realization of the critical zeros of zeta, taking
  care, in particular, of eventual multiplicity".
- "the stability of zeta-cycles under coverings is reminiscent of the behavior of closed geodesics in a
  Riemannian manifold, suggesting to look for a mysterious 'cusp' whose closed geodesics would correspond to
  zeta-cycles."
- Implicit throughout sections 2 to 5 (stated as numerical evidence, not as conjectures): positivity of
  `QW_lambda` on the windows tested, the ladder of small eigenvalues, and the resemblance of the spectrum of
  `D(lambda,k)` to the zeros for `k` close to `nu(lambda^2) ~ 2 lambda^2`.
