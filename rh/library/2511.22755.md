Conventions of the paper. Window `[1/lambda, lambda]` in the multiplicative variable `u`, measure
`d*u = du/u`; `L = 2 log lambda` is the FULL length of the window in `log u`; the primes that enter are the
prime powers `n <= lambda^2`. The project's half-width `L_ours` is `log lambda`, so `lambda^2 = e^{2 L_ours}` and
`L_paper = 2 L_ours`. Fourier transform `fhat(s) = int f(u) u^{-is} d*u`. The form, equation (3.19):
`QW_lambda(f,f) = int |fhat(t)|^2 (2 theta'(t)/2 pi) dt + 2 Re(fhat(i/2) conj fhat(-i/2))
 - sum_{1 < n <= lambda^2} Lambda(n) <f | T(n) f>`, `<f|T(n)g> = n^{-1/2}((f* * g)(n) + (f* * g)(1/n))`,
`theta` the Riemann-Siegel angle. Term by term this is the window form of the project (and of Zhu's (1.2),
(1.3), since `2 theta'(t) = Re psi(1/4 + it/2) - log pi`) with `lambda = e^{L_ours}` (this reader's comparison
of the printed formulas). The Hilbert space is the COMPLEX `L^2([1/lambda, lambda], d*u)`: "lowest eigenvalue"
means of the whole form, not of the even sector.
Notation is not uniform in the paper: the bottom of the spectrum is `mu_lambda` in section 3, the smallest
eigenvalue of the truncation is `epsilon_N` in sections 1 and 5, and `epsilon_lambda` in section 8 and Figure 4.

## 2. Main statements, with their numbers
### Proved in the text, unconditional
- **Lemma 3.1.** The Weil functional is `Psi(h) = Psi^#(h + h o iota)` (`iota(u) = 1/u`), with `Psi^#` a
  distribution on `[1, inf)`: `W_{0,2}^#(F) = int_1^inf F(x)(x^{1/2} + x^{-1/2}) d*x`,
  `W_R^#(F) = (1/2)(log 4 pi + gamma) F(1) + int_1^inf (x^{1/2} F(x) - F(1))/(x - 1/x) d*x`,
  `W_p^#(F) = (log p) sum_m p^{-m/2} F(p^m)`. **Proposition 3.2**: on the window,
  `QW(kappa f, kappa g) = Psi^#(F)`, `F(u) = q(f,g)(log u)`, `q(f,g)(y) = (f* * g)(y) + (f* * g)(-y)`.
- **Proposition 3.3** (quoted from Connes-Consani 2106.01715, section 2): "The quadratic form QW_lambda is lower
  bounded and lower semi-continuous." **Proposition 3.4** (quoted from 2106.01715 Prop. 2.3): the span `E` of
  the exponentials `V_n(u) = L^{-1/2} exp(2 pi i n log(lambda u)/L)` is a core, and "the lower bound of
  QW_lambda is the limit, when N -> inf, of the smallest eigenvalue of the restriction of QW_lambda to the
  linear span E_N of the functions V_k with |k| <= N".
- By the representation theorem for closed semibounded forms (Schmuedgen, Thm 10.7) there is, for each
  `lambda > 1`, "a canonical lower bounded unbounded selfadjoint operator A_lambda" with
  `QW_lambda(f,f) = <A_lambda f | f>` (3.23).
- **Theorem 3.6.** "The selfadjoint operator A_lambda has discrete lower bounded spectrum." For every
  `lambda > 1`, primes included. Proof: the prime terms and the pole term are bounded; the archimedean symbol is
  `theta'(t) = (1/2)(log|t| - log 2 - log pi) - 1/(48 t^2) + O(t^-4)`, so it suffices that the embedding of
  `{f : int |fhat|^2 rho < inf}` into `L^2` of the window is compact for any increasing `rho -> inf`, which
  follows from the compactness of `Phat_T P_L` (band-limiting after time-limiting).
- **Corollary 3.7.** "Let lambda > 1. There exists an element phi in L^2([1/lambda, lambda], d*u) such that
  A_lambda(phi) = mu_lambda phi where mu_lambda is the largest lower bound of the spectrum of A_lambda."
- Directly after it: "Note that we cannot assert that mu_lambda >= 0. One has however
  **(3.27)** lambda > lambda' => mu_lambda <= mu_lambda'." Justification given: piecewise smooth functions are
  in the domain and form a core, and `nu <= mu_lambda` iff `QW_lambda(f,f) >= nu ||f||^2` for all piecewise
  smooth `f` supported in the window.
- **Corollary 3.8.** "If the limit when lambda -> inf of the decreasing function mu_lambda is equal to 0 then RH
  holds." (No proof is written; no converse is stated.)
- **Lemma 4.1, Propositions 4.2, 4.3** (the matrix of `QW_lambda` in the basis `V_n`, in closed form).
  Pole term: `W_{0,2}(V_n, V_m) = 32 L sinh^2(L/4)(L^2 - 16 pi^2 mn) / ((L^2 + 16 pi^2 m^2)(L^2 + 16 pi^2 n^2))`
  ("a rank two matrix"; proof: "best verified by direct computation"). Primes (4.3):
  `sum_{1<k<=e^L} Lambda(k) k^{-1/2} q(U_n,U_m)(log k)`. Archimedean term: off the diagonal
  `(alpha_L(m) - alpha_L(n))/(n - m)`, on the diagonal `2 gamma_L(n) - 2 beta_L(n)`, where `alpha_L, beta_L,
  gamma_L` are given through `2F1(1, 1/4 + i pi n/L; 5/4 + i pi n/L; e^{-2L})`, the digamma and trigamma
  functions at `1/4 + i pi n/L` and the Hurwitz-Lerch function `Phi(e^{-2L}, 2, 1/4 + i pi n/L)`.
- **Lemma 5.1.** The truncated matrix `tau = QW_lambda^N` (restriction to `E_N`, `|n| <= N`) is real symmetric
  of the form (5.2): `tau_{ii} = a_i`, `tau_{ij} = (b_i - b_j)/(i - j)`, with `a_{-j} = a_j`, `b_{-j} = -b_j`;
  `b_n = -(1/pi) int_0^L sin(2 pi n y/L) D(y)`, `a_n = 2 int_0^L (1 - y/L) cos(2 pi n y/L) D(y)`, `D` the
  distribution `Psi^#` in the variable `log`.
- **Lemma 5.2.** For any `T` of the form (5.2): `T` commutes with the grading `gamma(V_j) = V_{-j}`, and
  `DT - TD = |beta><eta| - |eta><beta|` with `D V_n = n V_n`, `beta = sum b_j V_j`, `eta = sum V_j`.
- **Definition 5.3.** "A real symmetric matrix T commuting with the Z/2-grading gamma is even-simple if its
  smallest eigenvalue is simple and the corresponding eigenvector xi satisfies gamma xi = xi."
- **Lemma 5.4.** "Assume T >= 0 and Ker T = C xi where gamma xi = xi and <xi | eta> = 1. (i) One has
  T D xi = -beta. (ii) The operator D' := D - |D xi><eta| induces a selfadjoint operator D'' in the Hilbert
  space associated to the inner product defined by T, as quotient by null vectors. (iii) ...
  Det(D'' - s) = Det(D - s) sum_{j=-N}^{N} (j - s)^{-1} xi_j." (Stated as recalled from Connes-van Suijlekom.)
- **Lemma 5.5, Corollary 5.6.** `delta_N := L^{-1/2} sum_{|n|<=N} V_n` (the Dirichlet kernel) satisfies
  `<delta_N | f> -> f(lambda)` for `f` in the domain of the scaling operator `D_log = -i u d/du` with periodic
  boundary conditions.
- **Proposition 5.7.** If the truncated form is even-simple with even eigenvector `xi`, there is a unique
  operator `D_log^{(lambda,N)}` with the domain of `D_log` that agrees with `D_log` on `ker delta_N` and kills
  `xi`.
- **Lemma 5.8.** With the cut `(-1)^{-z} = e^{-i pi z}`, the regularized determinant of the operator with
  spectrum `(2 pi/L) Z` is `det_reg(D - s) = 1 - e^{-iLs}`.
- **Proposition 5.9.** For `xi = sum_{|k|<=N} xi_k V_k` extended by 0,
  `xihat(z) = 2 L^{-1/2} sin(zL/2) sum_j xi_j/(z - 2 pi j/L)`.
- **Lemma 7.1.** Riemann's `Xi` is the Fourier transform of `k = E(h)`, `E(f)(u) = u^{1/2} sum_{n>=1} f(nu)`,
  where `h(u) = (pi/2) u^2 (2 pi u^2 - 3) e^{-pi u^2}` is, up to a scalar, the only combination of the
  Hermite functions `h_0, h_4` with vanishing integral: `h = (sqrt 3/2^{11/4}) h_4 - (3/2^{17/4}) h_0`.
- **Lemma 7.2.** "(i) The eigenfunctions h_{n,lambda} of PW_lambda, suitably normalized, fulfill for n = 0, 4 an
  estimate of the form (with c < inf) max_{x in [-lambda,lambda]} |h_{n,lambda}(x) - h_n(x)| <= c lambda^-2.
  (ii) ... max |h_lambda(x) - h(x)| <= c lambda^-2", `h_lambda` the combination of `h_{0,lambda}, h_{4,lambda}`
  with vanishing integral; `PW_lambda = -d/dx((lambda^2 - x^2) d/dx) + (2 pi lambda x)^2` (prolate parameter
  `2 pi lambda^2`). Proof from Meixner-Schaefke 1954, Satz 9. The constant `c` is not given; Figures 2, 3
  plot `lambda^2 max|h_{n,lambda} - h_n|` for `lambda^2 <= 36` (by eye: about 0.029 for `n = 0`, 0.27 to 0.30
  for `n = 4`, decreasing).
- **Lemma 7.3.** "The Fourier transform of k_lambda converges, when lambda -> inf, towards the Xi-function of
  Riemann uniformly on closed substrips of the open strip |Im(z)| < 1/2." Here
  `k_lambda(u) := E(h_lambda)(u)` for `u in [1/lambda, lambda]` (equation (7.6), the "educated guess" taken from
  2106.01715 section 3). The proof gives the rate `O(lambda^{-1/2 - alpha})` at `Re s = alpha` in `(-1/2, 1/2)` for the
  first part of the error and "tends to 0 ... due to the convergence of the integral" for the tail.

### Proved in the text, under a stated hypothesis on the finite truncation
- **Theorem 5.10 (= Theorem 1.1, the main result).** "Let epsilon_N be the smallest eigenvalue of QW_lambda^N
  assumed simple and xi the corresponding eigenvector assumed even, normalized by delta_N(xi) = 1.
  (i) The operator D_log^{(lambda,N)} [= D_log^{(lambda)} - |D_log^{(lambda)} xi><delta_N|] is selfadjoint in
  the direct sum E'_N (+) E_N^perp where on the subspace E'_N = E_N/C xi the inner product is given by the
  restriction of the quadratic form QW_lambda^N - epsilon_N < | >.
  (ii) The regularized determinant of D_log^{(lambda,N)} is given by
  det_reg(D_log^{(lambda,N)} - z) = -i lambda^{-iz} xihat(z) where xihat is the Fourier transform of xi for the
  duality <R_+^* | R>.
  (iii) The Fourier transform xihat(z) is an entire function, all its zeros are on the real line and coincide
  with the spectrum of D_log^{(lambda,N)}."
  The hypothesis (simple, even) is on the `(2N+1) x (2N+1)` matrix. The sign of `epsilon_N` plays no role
  (`T = QW^N - epsilon_N`). The proof uses only the shape (5.2) of the matrix, not what the entries are ("The
  method we use is general as well as the proof that all the approximating values lie exactly on the critical
  line"). The spectrum contains the unperturbed points `2 pi j/L`, `|j| > N` (so `xihat` vanishes there).

## 3. The method in ten lines
1. Restrict Weil's explicit-formula form to functions on `[1/lambda, lambda]`: only prime powers `<= lambda^2`
   appear; the form is closed and bounded below (from 2106.01715) and its operator has compact resolvent
   because the archimedean symbol grows like `log|t|` (Thm 3.6). So a lowest eigenvector exists.
2. In the exponential basis the matrix is explicit (special functions with parameter `e^{-2L}`) and has the
   divided-difference shape `(b_i - b_j)/(i - j)` off the diagonal.
3. For any matrix `T` of that shape, `[D, T]` has rank two; if `T >= 0` has a one-dimensional kernel spanned by
   an even vector `xi`, the rank-one modification `D - |D xi><eta|` of the diagonal matrix `D` is symmetric for
   the inner product `T` (Lemma 5.4, from Connes-van Suijlekom).
4. Apply this to `T = QW_lambda^N - epsilon_N`: a rank-one perturbation of the scaling operator with periodic
   boundary conditions that kills the minimiser `xi` and is self-adjoint for the shifted Weil inner product.
5. Its characteristic polynomial is `Det(D - s) sum xi_j/(j - s)`; with the zeta-regularized determinant of the
   periodic operator, `1 - e^{-iLs}`, this is `-i lambda^{-iz} xihat(z)`: the zeros of the transform of the
   minimiser are the eigenvalues of a self-adjoint operator, hence real.
6. Compute: `lambda^2` from 9 to 14, `N = 120`, 200 digits; the eigenvalues agree with the first zeros of zeta
   to between 1e-60 and 1e-2.
7. To explain the agreement: `Xi` is the transform of `E(h)`, `h` a combination of two Hermite functions.
8. Replace Hermite by prolate functions of parameter `2 pi lambda^2` (within `c lambda^-2`), giving a function
   `k_lambda` on the window whose transform converges to `Xi` on `|Im z| < 1/2` (Lemmas 7.2, 7.3).
9. From 2106.01715 section 3: `k_lambda` is numerically close to a multiple of the true minimiser
   `xi_lambda`. If that were proved, with step 5 and Hurwitz's theorem the zeros of `Xi` would be real.
10. The work that is done is in steps 1 to 5 and 8 (all elementary once the shape (5.2) is seen); the two
    statements on which the strategy rests (step 5's hypothesis for the full form, and step 9) are not proved.

## 5. Where its frontier is
**Last things proved.** (a) For every `lambda > 1` the window form with its primes is the form of a
self-adjoint operator with discrete spectrum, so a lowest eigenvector exists (Thm 3.6, Cor. 3.7), and its
bottom `mu_lambda` is non-increasing in `lambda` (3.27). (b) For every finite truncation that is even-simple,
the transform of the minimiser has only real zeros and is the regularized determinant of an explicit
self-adjoint rank-one perturbation of the scaling operator (Thm 5.10). (c) The transform of the explicit
prolate guess `k_lambda` converges to `Xi` on `|Im z| < 1/2` (Lemma 7.3).

**First things it cannot do**, in the paper's words (section 8, "The missing steps"): "There are two essential
steps still missing to justify our tentative proof of the Riemann Hypothesis. The first is that, in order to
apply Theorem 5.10 to the Weil quadratic form QW_lambda, one must prove that its smallest eigenvalue - whose
existence is ensured by Theorem 3.6 - is simple and that its corresponding eigenvector xi_lambda is even. The
second step is to establish that k_lambda provides a sufficiently accurate approximation to (a scalar multiple
of) xi_lambda, in order to justify the convergence of the zeros of xihat_lambda towards the non-trivial zeros
of zeta(1/2 + is)." And in section 7: "Justifying rigorously this step is the main remaining obstacle to our
approach to RH." And: "It remains possible that our strategy for proving convergence towards the zeros of
zeta(1/2 + is) will face significant obstacles."
The three "indications" offered: "(1) The 'simple-even' condition holds for all values of lambda for the
prolate-wave operator PW_lambda. (2) The extremely small numbers epsilon_lambda that occur as eigenvalues of
the Weil quadratic form QW_lambda also appear - see Figure 4 - when evaluating the discrepancy for h_lambda to
belong simultaneously to P_lambda and Phat_lambda. (3) The numerical evidence for the proximity between
k_lambda and xi_lambda extends to the higher eigenfunctions of the Weil quadratic form."
The exact point where the proved part ends: between the finite matrix and the full form (the limit `N -> inf`
of Theorem 5.10 is the first Outlook bullet, not a theorem here), and between `k_lambda` and `xi_lambda` (no
estimate of any kind for `k_lambda - xi_lambda`, no norm in which it should be small, no rate that would be
"sufficiently accurate").

## 6. What it explicitly does not do (checked over the whole text)

  range (`lambda^2 <= 2`, Yoshida) is not even quoted (the Yoshida reference is commented out in the source).
- **No inequality between the Weil form and any prolate, Sonin-space or time-and-band-limiting form** (see part
  7, "The lead's question"). The word "Sonin" does not occur in the text (a macro `\son` is defined in the
  preamble and never used).
- No lower bound, upper bound or formula for `mu_lambda`/`epsilon_lambda`; no formula for its ratio to
  `1 - chi(lambda)`; the comparison is Figure 4.
- No Rayleigh quotient of `k_lambda`, no second eigenvalue, no spectral gap, no statement about the odd sector.
- No continuity or derivative of `mu_lambda` in `lambda`; no first-failure or null-vector statement; no converse
  to Corollary 3.8.
- No proof that the full form (`N = inf`) with a simple even ground state gives real zeros (that is in
  Connes-van Suijlekom 2511.23257, cited as [CS]); no proof of the convergence `N -> inf`.
- No variation of the arithmetic input: weights `Lambda(n)` and positions `log n` are never changed; no
  function other than zeta; no control without an Euler product. The accuracy is described as "suggesting a
  deep structural relationship between the restricted Euler products and the location of the zeros".
- No estimate of how the accuracy of the approximate zeros depends on `lambda`, `N` or the height; no statement
  on zeros above the fiftieth.
- The trace formula of Connes 1999 is named ("the cornerstone of this development is the trace formula
  established in [Co-zeta], which relates P_lambda, Phat_lambda, and the map E to QW_lambda") but not written,
  and nothing is derived from it here.
- Loose ends noticed by this reader (none affects a statement above): row 40 of the table is printed twice;
  "Theorem 5.10" is hard-coded in section 8; in the proof of Lemma 5.2 "q_{-i,-j} = q_{i,j}" stands for the
  matrix entries `tau`; in the proof of Theorem 3.6 the window is written `[-L, L]` where its length is `L`;
  the Outlook writes the strip as `Im(z) < 1/2` and Lemma 7.3 as `|Im(z)| < 1/2`; Lemma 4.1 has no written
  proof.

### The lead's question, answered from the text
*Does the paper state or prove any inequality of the form "Weil form on the window >= constant x (an explicit
positive form built from prolate functions / the Sonin space / time-and-band limiting)"?*
**No.** There is no such inequality in the paper, for any window, with or without primes, as a theorem, a
conjecture or a named target. What the paper does contain on the relation between `QW_lambda` and the prolate
side is exactly this:
1. An approximate eigenvector, not a form: `k_lambda = E(h_lambda)` on `[1/lambda, lambda]`, `h_lambda` the
   combination of the prolate functions `h_{0,lambda}, h_{4,lambda}` with vanishing integral; "We refer to
   [2106.01715], Section 3, for the motivation behind the formula for k_lambda and the numerical evidence
   showing that it gives an approximation of a scalar multiple of xi_lambda." Windows: all `lambda`; the
   primes `<= lambda^2` are inside `QW_lambda` and absent from `k_lambda`.
2. Two proved lemmas about `k_lambda` alone, in which `QW_lambda` does not appear: Lemma 7.2 (prolate within
   `c lambda^-2` of Hermite, `n = 0, 4`) and Lemma 7.3 (`khat_lambda -> Xi` on `|Im z| < 1/2`).
3. One numerical comparison of two NUMBERS: Figure 4, `log epsilon_lambda` against `log(1 - chi(lambda))`,
   `2 <= lambda^2 <= 17`, and the sentence that the tiny eigenvalues "also appear ... when evaluating the
   discrepancy for h_lambda to belong simultaneously to P_lambda and Phat_lambda" (indication (2)).
4. One sentence naming the trace formula of Connes (Selecta 1999) as relating `P_lambda`, `Phat_lambda` and
   `E` to `QW_lambda`; no formula.
*What it says remains to be proved*: the two missing steps quoted in part 5 (simple even ground state of
`QW_lambda`; `k_lambda` close enough to `xi_lambda`). Neither is phrased as a form inequality, and positivity
of `QW_lambda` is not listed as a step (the strategy goes through real zeros of `xihat_lambda` and Hurwitz,
not through `mu_lambda >= 0`; the only link to positivity in the paper is Corollary 3.8).
So `docs/IDEAS.md` #27, novelty field ("Connes-Consani-Moscovici 2511.22755 is believed to pursue the case with
primes"), and `docs/HUB.md` line 56 ("the paper nearest to the case with primes"): the paper works on windows
WITH primes, but it contains no comparison of forms; "not found in the six digests: the statement as a form
inequality with a constant" remains true after this seventh paper.
