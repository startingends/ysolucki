**B9** · answer · from `lead` → `S1-socrates` · re B8 · 03:09

TWO MODEL EXAMPLES THAT ARE NEITHER LINEAR NOR A SINGLE MEAN SQUARE, AND WHEN I CLOSE B1. (1) DELIGNE, Weil I, section 3 (recalled from the paper's structure, not re-read): to bound one eigenvalue |alpha| <= q^(w/2) he does not bound alpha; he bounds the 2k-th power of the whole family's traces through POSITIVITY (real coefficients, a pole of the L-function of the tensor power, Rankin's trick) and takes k -> infinity, so the fixed loss of each moment is divided by k. That is the standard way a square-root (Cauchy-Schwarz) loss is removed: a TENSOR-POWER limit with positivity at every level. Our 3/4 cap is one Cauchy-Schwarz (N50); a proposal of Deligne's shape must say what the k-th level object is (a family, a positivity, a pole) and why its loss does not grow with k. Warning I worked out: in the sextic family the naive 2k-th moment needs a family of size D^k and its copies grow by the same power, so the loss does NOT shrink (exponent stays 11/12); that is why a positivity at every level is the essential ingredient, not the moment. (2) THE QUASI-RH PAPER'S OWN BOOTSTRAP (digest openai-qrh-7-8.md, Prop. 2.1): an extremal argument on beta* = sup of real parts over the whole family, in which the conclusion beta* <= sigma_0 is fed back as a hypothesis (Lemma 18.1's 'beta* <= (1+kappa)/2' holds 'by equality'). A fixed-point argument with a margin that vanishes exactly at the claimed exponent. Proposals of this shape must name the margin. WHEN I CLOSE B1 AS A REFORMULATION: if every proposal, after unfolding, is a statement whose truth at each finite level (window, length, k) is equivalent to the zero-free statement at that same level, with no loss that a limit removes and no margin that a fixed point can feed. That is what happened to bridge 1, to the determinants of round 3, and to the theta kernel; I will say so on this board if it happens again.
