## #1 · C-1 Missing-prime law: the prime-filtered Nyman-Beurling distance is a bounded multiple of a forced local fee
- status: open
- source: R1-critB (lane B critic, idea C-1; transcribed from rh/experiments/r1_B/critic/PROGRESS.md by auditor R1-AUD); the object D_S is lane B's (R1-B/#1, merged here); single-missing-prime numbers by r1_judge (JUDGEMENT.md 1.3)
- date: 2026-11-10
- tags: laneB,E5,WJ4,WJ6
- plain english: An old criterion says RH holds exactly when a step function can be matched as closely as we like by mixing sawtooth waves frac(1/(kx)), k = 1, 2, 3, ... Lane B asked what happens if only the waves whose k is built from a few small primes are allowed. Its critic rewrote that as a walk along 1, 2, 3, ... that must stay level, where the step taken at each number may depend only on its allowed prime factors. Every prime that is left out then forces a mistake, a fee of roughly 1/(2 q^2) for the missing prime q, which no cleverness can avoid. Example: allow only the primes 2 and 3; the forced fees add up to 0.0438 and the true best error is 0.04996, 14 percent more. Leave out only the prime 3 instead: fee 0.0552, best error found 0.0607, 10 percent more. RH says the error goes to zero as primes are added; the numbers suggest something sharper: one never pays much more than the unavoidable local fee.
- wall: FAILS RULE: it meets W1 in its Nyman-Beurling form (the distance must tend to 0), stated exactly as wall WJ4 (D_P^2 = O(1/(P log P)) is not known), and gives no reason why it would get past it. Judge: 'still RH with a rate; nobody knows how to bound the remainder above the local fee'. Also PROVED by the critic: D_P(N) >= d_N for every N, so the prime filtration cannot beat the 1/log N floor (Burnol) of the size filtration; the rate in P is a relabelling of the rate in log N.
- new input: A proved, explicit, unconditional floor that the bare criterion lacks: D_S^2 >= m(q) > 0 for every prime set S that misses a prime q (critic's three-interval proof, checked line by line by the judge), with local bound about (1/2) sum over n > 1 coprime to all allowed primes of 1/n^2; and the 'walk' form of the problem in the variable y = 1/x, in which each missing prime forces a repeated step.
- claim: With D_P = dist(chi_(0,1), closed span{frac(1/(kx)) : all prime factors of k are <= P}) in L^2(0, infinity) (RH <=> D_P -> 0): D_P^2 = kappa_P * (1/2) sum_{n>1, gcd(n,P#)=1} n^-2 with kappa_P bounded (1.14..1.2 seen for P = 3..13). (critic, verbatim after the definition)
- prediction: Stated by the critic before computing (critic PROGRESS, 'Prediction stated before computing (rule 4)'): D_P^2 >= about 0.095, 0.044, 0.025, 0.0152 for P = 2, 3, 5, 7, with 'if my truncated lower bounds come out ABOVE the proposer's upper bounds, one of us is wrong'. Outcome: held; lower bounds 0.1247222, 0.0499603, about 0.02879, about 0.01759 against lane B's upper bounds 0.1247227, 0.04996047, 0.02889735, 0.01833983. The constant kappa itself (1.14 to 1.16) was measured, not predicted. Thresholds fixed by the judge before round 2: converged kappa_P <= 1.5 at P = 11 and 13; the one-prime ratio for q = 3 stays below 1.5 as N grows. Impostor version: not formulated (the Davenport-Heilbronn staircase is a round-2 item).
- smallest: Already proved (critic; checked by the judge): D_S > 0 for every S that misses a prime, with an explicit floor. Next smallest (judge, round 2): the exact one-prime cost for q = 3, 5, 7, a one-variable Szego problem. The upper bound D_P^2 = O(1/(P log P)) is wall WJ4 and is RH with a rate.
- worth if true: RH would read 'there is no global surcharge on top of the local fee', and the remainder kappa - 1 becomes a concrete object to attack (judge). Even then it is RH with a rate, so at least as hard as RH.
- first test: Done (critic): independent Gram matrix, y-space lower bounds for P = 2..13, sandwich with lane B's upper bounds. Done (judge): single missing primes at N = 2000. Next (judge, round-2 item 3): converge kappa_P for P = 11, 13 with a well-conditioned y-space solve including the tail; exact one-prime cost for q = 3, 5, 7; additivity of the cost for two missing primes; the Davenport-Heilbronn staircase as the control. Critic's own first test: converge the upper bound for P = 11, 13 (N about 1e7).
- kill line: Critic: kappa_P > 1.5 at P = 11 or 13. Judge adds: the one-prime cost for q = 3 divided by its local fee rises above 1.5 as N grows; or the lower-bound ratio climbs steadily by P = 31.
- novelty: SEARCHED, thinly. Lane B (proposer): one web search for Nyman-Beurling restricted to smooth numbers / a prime-generated semigroup found nothing; also looked at (web search level) Bettin-Conrey-Farmer arXiv 1211.5191, Balazard-de Roton, Darses-Hillion arXiv 1805.06733, Baez-Duarte arXiv math/0505453. Critic: 4 web searches (queries not recorded), Bettin-Conrey-Farmer read through an ar5iv summary only. The local-fee bound and the kappa law themselves: UNSEARCHED. Status: not found in what was read; NOT established as new.
- evidence: PROVED (critic; judge checked line by line): D_S^2 >= m(q) > 0 for any S missing a prime q; D_P(N) >= d_N for every N. COMPUTED, float64, not interval-certified. Critic: lower bound / local bound = 1.33, 1.14, 1.15, 1.16, 1.15, 1.16 for P = 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 (for P >= 5 from an ill-conditioned solve, 3 digits; the upper bounds for P = 11, 13 are far from converged); sandwich with lane B's upper bounds agrees to 6 digits for {2} (0.12472) and {2,3} (0.049960); local bounds 0.0935593, 0.0437872, 0.0249686, 0.0151296, 0.0111043, 0.0082023. Critic's independent Gram matrix equals lane B's to 6.9e-10 on the 200 x 200 block and d_N^2 to 10 digits at N = 10, 50, 100, 200; values at N = 500..2000 rest on lane B's matrix alone. Judge, single missing primes at N = 2000 (lane B's Gram matrix, one run): cost / fee = 1.10 for {3}, 1.24 for {5}, 1.17 for {3,5}, 1.17 for {5,7}; the rows for {7}, {11}, {13} are dominated by the finite-N floor d_N^2 = 0.0061 and say nothing; the fee bound is not valid when 2 is among the missing primes.
