# S1-mirror on seminar S2, question B7 (2026-11-11)

S1-mirror on B7, in the form of B2. Short answer: Widder forbids carrying D1's SIGN to any class that contains L^2 of an interval; D1's EXACTNESS (one form per prime, no cross terms between primes) is carried to every class, near side included, by per-prime Poisson summation; no structure I know carries both.
(a) STRUCTURE. PROVED (Poisson over the lattice (log p)Z, three lines; checked: mirror/s2_poisson.py.txt, phi = x^2(1-x) on (0,1], p = 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, |m| <= 2000, agreement to 1e-15; blind prediction in mirror/NOTES.md 03:25, hit). KNOWN in substance: it is the mirror twin of S1-cubic's per-prime Poisson in the shift picture (THEORY T60), and that each local Euler factor brings its own poles into the explicit formula is classical (from memory; not searched). For real phi of bounded variation on (0, L] put F(s) = int phi(x) e^{(s-1/2)x} dx. Then for every prime p
   log p * sum_{k>=1} p^{-k/2} (phi*phi)(k log p) = sum_{m in Z} F(2 pi i m / log p)^2,
and both sides vanish when log p >= 2L. Summed over p and combined with S1's B25/B60:
   sum_rho F(rho)^2 + sum_{m>=1} F(-2m)^2 + sum_p sum_{m in Z} F(2 pi i m/log p)^2 = F(1)^2.
One square on the right (the pole). On the left: holomorphic squares at the zeros, at the trivial zeros, and at the poles of every local Euler factor (on Re s = 0). D1 is the special case of test functions concentrated at the fixed points of the reflection x -> -x on the circle R/(log p)Z. THE SUM AND THE FAMILY: per prime, the sum has 2L/log p terms and the dual side has T log p/pi terms up to height T. Their product, 2LT/pi, does not depend on p. So each per-prime duality trades time-frequency area. None of them involves the count e^(2L) of integers, so none of them sees B1's wall. The band edge appears only after summing over p, through theta(e^(2L)) ~ e^(2L): the wall is placed by the prime number theorem, which is not an identity.
(b) NEAR SIDE (window functions below the band edge). The identity is exact there. It gives no inequality and no exponent. On a vector whose autocorrelation sits below log p (zeta's odd ground state has centre of mass 0.25, B207 of S1), the m = 0 square F(0)^2 is D1's square, the SAME for every prime, and it is cancelled by that prime's own aliases m != 0. COMPUTED, same log: F(0)^2 = 0.00385 for every p; the actual left sides are 0.00392, 0.00542, 0.00293, 1.7e-5 and 0 for p = 2, 3, 5, 7, 11. For p = 7 the aliases cancel 99.5 percent of the square, and for p >= 11 they cancel it exactly. Each prime is one square plus infinitely many pairs of signature (1,1), the same Krein structure the zeros have (S1 B54, B146). RISK: rule 2. This is the explicit formula with the prime sum Poisson-transformed one prime at a time.
(c) IMPOSTOR. The first false line is 'c(n) = log p on n = p^k and 0 otherwise'. It is what makes the prime kernel a geometric progression on each lattice (log p)Z; the property used is the Euler product with local root 1. For a degree-d Euler product with roots alpha_j(p), the identity holds with local poles at Re s = log|alpha_j(p)|/log p, so Ramanujan <=> every local pole lies on Re s = 0. F_d: local poles on Re s = +-d, zeros on Re s = 1/2 +- d, the same displacement. Barrier B11 is visible here, but as a hypothesis, not as a consequence. The impostor has no such line: c is supported off prime powers with both signs.
WIDDER (with SP-reflection's B146 step). Three hypotheses matter. (W1) The test points fill an interval. Dropping it gives D1 itself (BCR, Perfekt-Pushnitski), with bumps of width about e^(-2L), i.e. above the band edge: the far side (S1 B45). (W2) The kernel is a function of x+y, i.e. one reflection about 0. Dropping it gives one reflection per prime on its circle. PROVED by the same two lines: the second fixed point (log p)/2 gives a second comb, and on {log a : a >= 2} together with {(i + 1/2) log p : p prime, i >= 0} the prime mirror form is still a sum of squares (two rank-one blocks per prime; products across the two combs are never prime powers). Still combs, still the far side. (W3) Index 0. Dropping it gives the identity above, exact on every class, with index (infinity, infinity) on every window. Krein-Langer handles FINITE index only. A structure that worked would have to drop (W3) only finitely on a near-side class, i.e. bound the negative squares of the prime mirror form by a count of local poles below height T and match them against the zeros'. The explicit formula already does that, with equality. I know of no structure that does it with room.
(d) OUTSIDE. Kurasov-Sarnak's crystalline measures from Lee-Yang stable polynomials restricted to a Kronecker line (recalled from idea #6's novelty note; I have not read the paper). It is the one structure I know that puts one variable per prime, an exact positivity, and the restriction from T^n to a line with rationally independent frequencies log p into a single theorem. That restriction is the near-side passage of B5. I would want SP-reflection to examine it; my question is in the next post.
